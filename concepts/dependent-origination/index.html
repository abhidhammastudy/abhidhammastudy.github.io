Dependent origination

12 links, 3 lives

The twelve links, read across three lives.

In brief

  • Dependent origination is a chain of twelve links, from ignorance to ageing and death, showing how the round of births keeps turning.
  • Its principle is simple: when this is, that is; from the arising of this, that arises.
  • The commentaries and the Manual spread the links over three lives: past causes, present results, present causes and future results.
  • The Guide adds that the three periods are a frame for the causal pattern, not a timetable: all twelve links are at work in any one life; the Vibhaṅga's Abhidhamma section reads them in one moment.
  • Ignorance and craving are its two roots; with no self passing from life to life, it avoids both eternalism and annihilationism.
  • SN 12.1 also states it in cessation form: with the cessation of ignorance, formations cease, and so on to the end of suffering.

What it is

“When this is, that is; from the arising of this, that arises” (SN 12.21). The twelve links show how ignorance and craving keep the round (vaṭṭavaṭṭa · round one of three cycles turning in the wheel: defilements, kamma, results) of existence turning. The commentaries, and the Manual after them, spread the links over three lives: past causes, present results, present causes, future results. Saṃsāra, the round of births, appears as a process with no self that transmigrates, which avoids both eternalism (sassata-diṭṭhisassata-diṭṭhi · eternalism the view of a self that lasts for ever; one of the two extreme views) and annihilationism (uccheda-diṭṭhiuccheda-diṭṭhi · annihilationism the view that a self is destroyed at death; the other extreme view). SN 12.1 also states the chain in its cessation form: with the cessation of ignorance, formations cease, and so on to the end of the whole mass of suffering.

In depth

How the Manual presents it

VIII §3 gives the twelve links. Ignorance conditions formations, then consciousness, mentality-materiality, the six bases, contact, feeling, craving, clinging, becoming, birth, and ageing and death with sorrow, lamentation, pain, grief and despair. VIII §4 lists seven ways to analyse the chain: three periods, twelve factors, twenty modes, three connections, four groups, three rounds and two roots (VIII §4–10, Table 8.1).

The periods (VIII §5) put ignorance and formations in the past life, birth and ageing and death in the future, and the eight middle links in the present.

What each link is

The Guide to VIII §3 names the dhammas behind the links. Formations are the volitions in the 29 mundane wholesome and unwholesome cittas. Consciousness is the 32 mundane resultant cittas, from rebirth-linking on. Mentality-materiality is the factors joined to them, with kamma-born matter. Becoming is twofold. Kamma-becoming (kamma-bhavakamma-bhava · kamma-process becoming becoming as the active side: the wholesome and unwholesome volitions that make for a new existence) is the kamma-making process. Rebirth-becoming (upapatti-bhavaupapatti-bhava · rebirth-process becoming becoming as the passive side: the resultant cittas, their factors and kamma-born matter of a new existence) is the 32 resultants with their factors and kamma-born matter.

The Guide adds a detail about feeling: neutral feeling too conditions craving, through its peacefulness.

Groups, connections, rounds, roots

There are four groups of five (VIII §7). The past causes are ignorance, formations, craving, clinging and becoming. The present results run from consciousness to feeling. The present causes are the same five, at work now. The future results are the same five results, shown as birth and ageing and death. That makes the twenty modes.

The three connections fall between formations and consciousness, between feeling and craving, and between becoming and birth. The three rounds (VIII §8) are defilements (ignorance, craving, clinging), kamma (formations and kamma-becoming) and results (the rest). The two roots (VIII §9) are ignorance, running from the past to feeling, and craving, running from the present to ageing and death.

Readings and misunderstandings

The Manual follows the commentaries (Vism XVII) in spreading the links over three lives (VIII §5). The Guide treats the three periods as a frame for showing the causal pattern, not a timetable: no link is confined to its period, and in any one life all twelve are at work and imply one another (Guide to VIII §5; see VIII §7). Vism XVII also works out which of the 24 conditions operate at each link. The suttas show variants. DN 15 runs a two-way bond between consciousness and mentality-materiality. The Vibhaṅga's Abhidhamma section reads the whole chain within a single moment of citta.

The chain is not a first-cause story. Ignorance has its own condition, the taints (MN 9). It is not a row of single causes either, since each link stands in many relations at once. "Birth" means the arising of the aggregates at rebirth-linking, not the day of delivery. SN 12.1 also states the chain in its cessation form: with the cessation of ignorance, formations cease, and so on to the end of the whole mass of suffering (SN 12.1). The two orders are two of the four truths: the arising order is the origin of suffering, the ceasing order its cessation (AN 3.61, 13.6–17.4; see the concept The four noble truths). Stories that show it

Dhammadinnā on feeling

Disciples · MN 44

Questioned by the layman Visākha, the nun Dhammadinnā explained that lust lies latent in pleasant feeling, aversion in painful feeling and ignorance in neutral feeling. The Buddha said he would have answered in the same way.

This joins the colours of feeling in “A map of the eighty-nine” to the wheel in “The wheel across three lives”: feeling (link 7) is where craving (link 8) takes hold.

Two sheaves of reeds

Disciples · SN 12.67

Mahākoṭṭhita asked Sāriputta whether name-and-form and consciousness were made by oneself, by another, by both, or by neither. By none of these, said Sāriputta: each arises with the other as its condition, like two sheaves of reeds standing propped against each other. Pull one away and the other falls.

The two sheaves are consciousness on one side and name-and-form on the other (the mental factors and matter); “Mind and matter together” models the same mutual support: two currents that hold each other up, and that arise together at conception.

The night of awakening

The Buddha's life · MN 36; Ud 1.1–1.3

Under the Bodhi tree, in the first watch of the night he recalled his past lives; in the second he saw beings pass away and reappear according to their kamma; in the third he realised the destruction of the taints. In the days that followed he reviewed dependent origination forward and in reverse.

The three watches map onto the readings: past lives across the planes (“Thirty-one planes”), beings reborn by kamma and its fruit (“Kamma, death and rebirth”), and the wheel with its ending (“The wheel across three lives” and “The tree of purification”).

How a story is read here is a study aid, not the tradition's own reading.

Reflect and discuss

Opening question

Dependent origination is usually read across three lives. Where, in a single moment, could you watch one link become the next?

One way to see it, after you have tried

From feeling to craving, in the javanas that follow a pleasant feeling. The Vibhaṅga also teaches the whole series within a single moment of citta, in its Abhidhamma section, so the chain is not only a story of lives.
