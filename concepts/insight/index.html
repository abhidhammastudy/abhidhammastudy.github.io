Insight

vipassanā

The knowledges that see mind and matter rise and fall.

In brief

  • Insight (vipassanā) is wisdom that sees mind and matter as they are, marked by impermanence, suffering and non-self.
  • It unfolds through seven purifications, from virtue and a concentrated mind up to the path, and within them through ten insight knowledges.
  • They begin with comprehension, which works on formations in groups, past, future and present, before the knowledges that watch present formations arise and pass.
  • Bright lights, bliss and other striking experiences along the way are imperfections of insight, and the meditator must see that they are not the path.
  • Insight takes only conditioned things as its object, so it ripens the mind for the path but does not itself realise Nibbāna, the unconditioned.
  • The reading Ten knowledges, one by one takes each knowledge in turn, with where later teachers differ.

What it is

Insight (vipassanāvipassanā · insight seeing mind and matter as impermanent, suffering and non-self) is wisdom (paññāpaññā · wisdom understanding things as they are; also called non-delusion and the faculty of wisdom) that sees mind and matter as they are: marked by impermanence (anicca), suffering (dukkhadukkha · bodily pain painful bodily feeling: only in unwholesome-resultant body-consciousness) and non-self (anattā). It unfolds within the seven purifications, from purification of virtue to purification by knowledge and vision, and within them through the ten insight knowledges, from comprehension to conformity (IX §25). Insight takes conditioned dhammas as its object: it ripens the mind for the path, but it does not itself realise Nibbāna.

In depth

How the Manual presents it

Chapter IX sets out the field of insight (IX §22–27). There are seven purifications, from the relay-chariots of MN 24. There are three characteristics: impermanence, suffering and non-self. There are three contemplations, one for each characteristic. There are ten insight knowledges, in the order they arise: comprehension, rise and fall, dissolution, fearfulness, danger, disenchantment, desire for deliverance, reflection, equanimity about formations and conformity. Last come the three emancipations and the three doors to them.

The purifications step by step

Purification of virtue is fourfold: restraint by the Pātimokkha, restraint of the senses, pure livelihood, and right use of the requisites (IX §28). Purification of mind is access and absorption concentration (IX §29; Vism XVIII §662). For one who goes by dry insight, momentary concentration, as steady as access, counts instead (Guide to IX §29); the pairing of pure insight with momentary concentration is in the Visuddhimagga's sub-commentary (on Vism I §§3–4). Purification of view discerns mind and matter by characteristic, function, manifestation and proximate cause (IX §30; Vism XVIII §§662–677). Overcoming doubt discerns their conditions in past, present and future (IX §31; Vism XIX §§678–691).

Path and not-path is the next purification (IX §32). Formations of past, future and present are gathered into groups, by aggregate and so on, and comprehended by the three marks (Vism XX §§692–722); then an early knowledge of rise and fall arrives (Vism XX §§723–731). With it come ten imperfections of insight. In the VisuddhimaggaVisuddhimagga · Path of Purification Buddhaghosa's fifth-century manual of practice and doctrine; source of Plates 12–13's order they are light, knowledge, zest, tranquillity, happiness, resolution, exertion, mindfulness, equanimity, and a subtle attachment to them all (Vism XX §§732–736); the Manual's verse gives the same ten in another order (IX §32). The meditator must see them as impermanent, suffering and non-self, and so see that they are not the path (Vism XX §736).

Purification of the way covers nine knowledges, from mature rise and fall to conformity (IX §33; Vism XXI §§737–805). Purification by knowledge and vision is the path knowledge itself (IX §34). Change-of-lineage falls between the last two purifications and belongs to neither (Table 9.2; Vism XXII §806).

Distinctions that matter

Insight is wisdom in sense-sphere cittas: the great (sense-sphere) wholesome cittas with knowledge, or functional ones in an arahant. Its objects are conditioned, mundane dhammas, never concepts and never Nibbāna. Nibbāna is known first by change-of-lineage, then by path and fruition, and afterwards by the reviewing cittas that look back on it (Guide to III §18; IX §34).

The mark that leads out gives the path its name (IX §35–36). Seeing non-self opens the void emancipation, impermanence the signless, and suffering the desireless. The commentaries add which faculty favours which door: faith leans toward impermanence, concentration toward suffering, wisdom toward non-self (Guide to IX §35; Vism XXI §§768–769, 772–774). When equanimity about formations and conformity are fully developed, they are called "insight leading to emergence" (IX §34); the VisuddhimaggaVisuddhimagga · Path of Purification Buddhaghosa's fifth-century manual of practice and doctrine; source of Plates 12–13 counts change-of-lineage in it too (Vism XXI §782).

Insight sees formations: the first truth, and craving among them. Its knowledge of the truths is following knowledge (anubodha), which is mundane and reaches cessation and the path only by learning and hearsay. Path knowledge penetrates all four at once (Vism XVI §563; Guide to IX §34). See the concept The four noble truths.

Common misunderstandings

The "sixteen knowledges" of many modern manuals are the Manual's ten with additions. Before them come defining mind and matter and discerning conditions. After them come change-of-lineage, path, fruition and reviewing. The items are all in Vism XVIII–XXII, but the count is a later arrangement used in modern teaching, for example in Mahasi Sayadaw's The Progress of Insight. The canon's own list, in the Paṭisambhidāmagga, is shorter: it counts fear, danger and disenchantment as one knowledge, and desire for deliverance, reflection and equanimity as one (Vism XXI §§755, 778–781).

Insight is not only thinking about impermanence. Comprehension works on formations in groups, by spans of time, before the knowledges that watch present formations arise and pass; dissolution then infers past and future from what it sees in the present (Vism XX; Vism XXI §745). Bright lights and bliss are imperfections, not attainments. The knowledges of fear and danger are judgements, not moods: the knowledge of fear does not itself fear (Vism XXI §751). Insight does not realise Nibbāna itself; it ripens the mind until the path does. See the readings Ten knowledges, one by one, The tree of purification and Mind and matter together.

Stories that show it

Makhādeva's first grey hair

Jātaka · Ja 9

King Makhādeva told his barber to report the first grey hair. When it came, the king held it in his hand, saw death approaching, handed the kingdom to his son and went forth.

A small object can carry a large process: the same grey hair can stir vanity or urgency (saṃvega), depending on how it is attended to (“The mango and the moment”). MN 83 adds the Buddha's own point: Makhādeva's good custom led to the Brahmā world; the custom he himself founded, the noble eightfold path, leads to Nibbāna.

Ānanda lies down

Disciples · Vinaya, Cūḷavagga XI

The First Council was to begin the next day, and Ānanda, who would recite the suttas, was not yet an arahant. He spent the night in mindfulness of the body. Near dawn he decided to lie down, and as he leaned back, before his head reached the pillow and after his feet had left the ground, his mind was freed.

The path process in “Other processes” runs at the mind door and needs no posture at all: preparation, access, conformity, cleansing (the name change-of-lineage takes for the higher paths), then path and fruition, in the space between standing and lying down.

The night of awakening

The Buddha's life · MN 36; Ud 1.1–1.3

Under the Bodhi tree, in the first watch of the night he recalled his past lives; in the second he saw beings pass away and reappear according to their kamma; in the third he realised the destruction of the taints. In the days that followed he reviewed dependent origination forward and in reverse.

The three watches map onto the readings: past lives across the planes (“Thirty-one planes”), beings reborn by kamma and its fruit (“Kamma, death and rebirth”), and the wheel with its ending (“The wheel across three lives” and “The tree of purification”).

How a story is read here is a study aid, not the tradition's own reading.

Reflect and discuss

Opening question

Insight looks at the same dhammas the Abhidhamma lists. What changes when a list becomes something seen?

One way to see it, after you have tried

Impermanence, suffering and non-self are seen in present dhammas, not inferred about them. Knowing their rise and fall replaces knowing about it. The list stays useful as a map of where to look.
