31 planes

worlds of mind

The worlds, matched to levels of mind.

In brief

  • The Abhidhamma lays out the cosmos as thirty-one planes of existence, each matched to a level of mind.
  • There are four woeful planes, seven happy sense-sphere planes including the human world, sixteen fine-material and four immaterial Brahmā worlds.
  • Each world is reached by the matching kind of mind: unwholesome kamma leads to the woeful planes, and the meditative absorptions to the Brahmā worlds.
  • The same kinds of mind also come and go in this life, so a plane is both a world and a level of mind, yet rebirth there is real.
  • No plane is permanent, and even the longest lifespan comes to an end.
  • The count of thirty-one was assembled by the later tradition; no single sutta lists them all.

What it is

The cosmos is laid out as thirty-one planes of existence, matched to levels of mind: four woeful planes, seven happy sense-sphere planes, sixteen fine-material and four immaterial Brahmā worlds. Each is reached by the kamma or the jhāna that corresponds to it, so each world mirrors a kind of mind, and rebirth there is that mind’s result. Every plane belongs to saṃsāra and is impermanent; even the longest lifespan ends.

In depth

How the Manual presents it

The Manual groups the thirty-one planes into four (V §3–7). The four woeful planes are hell, the animal realm, the ghosts (petapeta · ghost a being of one of the woeful planes, tormented by hunger and thirst) and the titans (asuraasura · titan a being of the woeful plane of the asuras, or, in another sense, of the warring titans). The seven happy sense-sphere planes are the human world and six heavens: the Four Great Kings, the Thirty-three, the Yāma gods, the Tusita gods, the gods who delight in creating, and the gods who wield power over others' creations. The sixteen fine-material planes are three for each of the first three jhānas and seven for the fourth: Great Fruit, the non-percipient beings and the five Pure Abodes. Then come the four immaterial planes.

Mind matched to world

Each world is reached by the matching kind of citta. Unwholesome kamma leads to the woeful planes, and sense-sphere wholesome kamma to the human and heavenly worlds. The jhānas lead to the Brahmā worlds, graded limited, medium or superior (V §31). The first jhāna yields Brahmā's Retinue, his Ministers or the Great Brahmās. The Abhidhamma counts five jhānas where the suttas count four, so its second and third jhānas yield the second-jhāna planes; the fourth yields the third-jhāna planes; and the fifth yields Great Fruit. The fifth jhāna developed with dispassion for perception yields the non-percipient plane. Only non-returners are reborn in the Pure Abodes. Each immaterial attainment yields its own immaterial plane.

The same cittas also occur during life, which is why "plane" can name a level of mind as well as a world. But the world limits what can arise in it. The immaterial planes have no matter, and so no eye, no seeing and no five-door process. The non-percipient plane has no mind at all (IV §27–29).

Lifespans

In the woeful planes, and among humans, there is no fixed span: it depends on the kamma (Guide to V §12). The Four Great Kings live 500 celestial years, nine million human years. The highest sense-sphere gods live 16,000 celestial years, about 9,216 million human years (V §12). Brahmā's Retinue lives a third of an aeon (an incalculable aeon, say the commentators), and the Highest Pure Abode 16,000 great aeons (V §14). The immaterial planes run from 20,000 to 84,000 great aeons (V §16). The Guide explains the aeon with the sutta image of a mountain of granite worn away by a silk cloth (SN 15.5).

Common misunderstandings

No plane is permanent; even 84,000 aeons ends. The Great Brahmā is not a creator. The suttas show him mistaken about his own origin (DN 1) and unable to answer where the elements cease (DN 11).

The thirty-one is a count assembled by the later tradition; no single sutta lists them all. Treating the planes only as states of mind loses something the Abhidhamma insists on: rebirth in them is real. The tradition holds that the human plane, with its mix of pain and ease, is the one best suited to practice. See the readings Thirty-one planes and From kamma to its results.

Stories that show it

Where the Abhidhamma was first taught

The Buddha's life · Atthasālinī, introduction

The commentary tells that the Buddha spent a rainy season in the Tāvatiṃsa heaven, teaching the Abhidhamma to the devas, among them his mother, reborn as a deva. Each day he came down to Lake Anotatta, where he gave Sāriputta the outline, and Sāriputta taught it to five hundred monks. At the end of the rains he descended at Saṅkassa. The Tipiṭaka itself does not tell this story.

This is the tradition's account of where the seven books described in “The library” came from, and why Sāriputta is honoured as the one who first passed it on.

Why Moggallāna smiled

Disciples · SN 19.1

Coming down Vulture Peak, Mahā Moggallāna smiled. Asked why, he said he had seen a skeleton moving through the air, pursued by crows and vultures. The Buddha confirmed that he too had seen such beings, and named the deeds that led there.

An arahant smiles with one of five cittas, none of which makes kamma: the smile-producing consciousness found only in arahants (in “A map of the eighty-nine”), or one of the four joyful great functional cittas. What he saw belongs to the woeful planes of “Thirty-one planes”.

Maṭṭhakuṇḍalī's last thought

Disciples · Dhammapada commentary, v. 2

A miserly brahmin's son lay dying, left on the veranda so visitors would not see inside the house. The Buddha passed and let himself be seen. The boy's mind filled with faith, he died with that thought, and he was reborn in the Tāvatiṃsa heaven.

Death-proximate kamma in “Kamma, death and rebirth”: the last javanas were full of faith, and the rebirth-linking that followed took up their object.

How a story is read here is a study aid, not the tradition's own reading.

Reflect and discuss

Opening question

The thirty-one planes are worlds, and each also matches kinds of citta. Is a plane a place or a state of mind?

One way to see it, after you have tried

In the Abhidhamma it is both. Rebirth in a plane is the result of the matching kind of citta, and in this life the same kinds of citta come and go. The cosmology maps mind onto world.
