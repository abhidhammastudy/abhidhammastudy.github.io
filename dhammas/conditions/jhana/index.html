Jhāna

jhāna

The jhāna factors help by making the mind look closely at its object, in ordinary moments as well as in meditation.

Direction
Mind → mind & matter
Group
Mind → mind & matter
Manual
VIII §§11–28

In depth

Jhāna condition, jhāna-paccayajhāna · absorption deep concentration fixed on one object: five fine-material levels, then four immaterial, is help by close attention. The jhāna factors make the citta and factors they arise with look closely at their object, and they condition the matter that citta produces too. The Manual lists seven jhāna factors, which reduce to five mental factors: initial application, sustained application, zest, feeling (joyful, sad or neutral), and one-pointedness.

The name misleads. Jhāna condition is not limited to meditative absorption. It works in every citta except the ten kinds of sense consciousness, wholesome and unwholesome alike, in ordinary life as in meditation. A thief intent on a lock and a craftsman absorbed in a joint both have it. The ten sense consciousnesses lack it because their grasp is too brief and bare: seeing merely sees, without contemplating.

The matter born of a citta is included among the conditioned states. It does not contemplate anything, but because it is produced by a citta whose factors are looking closely, it is conditioned by them. Jhāna is often paired with path: jhāna holds the mind close to what it knows, while path carries it toward a destination.

What conditions
initial application, sustained application, zest, feeling and one-pointedness, in every citta except the ten sense consciousnesses
What is conditioned
those cittas, their factors, and the matter arising with them

An example. Threading a needle: initial and sustained application keep the mind on the eye of the needle, one-pointedness holds it there, and the feeling of the moment colours it. The steadiness of the hand, mind-born matter, is conditioned by those factors too.

Not to be confused with

  • jhāna as attainment. The jhānas of meditation are states in which these factors become strong and steady. The condition is the same five factors at work in any citta.
  • path. Jhāna factors make the mind look closely. Path factors make it a road toward a destination, good or bad.

Which way it works

  • mind → mind. The jhāna factors, such as initial application and one-pointedness, fix the citta closely on its object: in every citta but the ten sense consciousnesses.
  • mind → matter. The jhāna factors also support the matter that their citta produces.

The same factors, as a category

In chapter VII, the jhāna factors work as this condition. The seven jhāna factors are jhāna condition to the states they arise with and to mind-born matter: they make the mind 'contemplate' its object closely.

Stories that show it

  • The night of awakening: the jhāna factors holding the mind closely on its object in each of the four jhānas
  • The goldsmith and the lotus: the jhāna factors holding the mind on the beautiful object, jhāna after jhāna
  • Āḷāra and Uddaka: the jhāna factors of the immaterial attainments, which lead only to rebirth
  • The last night at Kusinārā: the jhāna factors at work as he rose and fell through the jhānas before the end
