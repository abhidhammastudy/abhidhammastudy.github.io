Kamma

kamma

A volition helps its result: at the same moment, by organising the citta, and across time, as past kamma that ripens later.

Manual

In depth

Kamma condition, kamma-paccayakamma wholesome or unwholesome volition, which later ripens as results, is the help volition gives. Volition, cetanācetanā · volition the willing that organises the other factors; as kamma, it bears results, is the mental factor that organises and drives the other states of a citta, and in the Paṭṭhāna it conditions in two quite different ways.

Conascent kamma works at the same moment. The volition in every citta, of any kind, makes the citta and the other factors do their tasks and stirs up the matter arising with them. Here even a resultant or a functional citta has its volition, though it makes no new kamma.

Asynchronous kamma works across time. A past wholesome or unwholesome volition, once it has ceased, ripens later as resultant cittas and factors and as kamma-born matter, at rebirth and through the course of life. The gap can be a moment or many lives. The relation also holds between a path and its fruit, which follows at once. Kamma-born matter, the Manual adds in its chapter on matter, comes from sense-sphere and fine-material kamma only; immaterial kamma ripens in realms without matter.

Kamma is one of the four conditions into which all twenty-four are gathered. Its asynchronous kind, when strong, is also a natural decisive support.

What conditions
for conascent kamma, the volition in every citta; for asynchronous kamma, past wholesome and unwholesome volitions
What is conditioned
for conascent kamma, the citta, its other factors and the matter arising with it; for asynchronous kamma, resultant cittas and factors and kamma-born matter

An example. A gift given with a glad heart years ago ripens today as a pleasant sight: the past volition of giving is the kamma condition for the present eye-consciousness and its factors. Meanwhile the volition in the present seeing citta organises that citta's own factors as conascent kamma.

Its kinds

  • conascent kamma. Volition organises the citta, factors and matter that arise with it.
  • asynchronous kamma. A past volition produces its results, mental and material, at a later time.

Not to be confused with

  • result. Kamma is the volition that ripens. Result is the help that the ripened states give each other, by being passive.
  • decisive support. Strong past kamma also supports its results by natural decisive support. Kamma condition names its specific force, the volition producing its fruit.

Which way it works

  • mind → mind. Volition coordinates the citta and factors it arises with; past volition, ripening later, produces resultant cittas.
  • mind → matter. Past volition also produces kamma-born matter, from rebirth onwards; present volition supports the matter its citta produces.
