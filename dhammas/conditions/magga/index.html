Path

magga

The path factors lead the mind toward a destination, a good one or a bad one.

Direction
Mind → mind & matter
Group
Mind → mind & matter
Manual
VIII §§11–28

In depth

Path condition, magga-paccayamagga · path (citta) a supramundane citta that arises once, cutting defilements, with Nibbāna as object, is help by leading somewhere. The path factors make the citta and factors they arise with into a road toward a destination. There are twelve path factors. Eight are right: right view, intention, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness and concentration, leading to good destinations and to Nibbāna. Four are wrong: wrong view, intention, effort and concentration, leading to the states of woe. Together they reduce to nine mental factors.

Path condition works in every rooted citta, 71 in all, and in the matter those cittas produce. The eighteen rootless cittas lack it, even though some contain thought, energy or one-pointedness; without a root, those factors do not amount to a path. In resultant and functional cittas the path factors lead nowhere, since those cittas make no kamma, but they are still counted as path factors by their nature.

Newcomers often take path to mean only the noble eightfold path. Here the word is neutral: a greedy plan has its wrong intention, wrong effort and wrong concentration, and it too is a road. The noble path of the four truths is narrower still: the eight right factors arising together in the four supramundane path cittas (Guide to VII §38).

What conditions
the nine mental factors that serve as the twelve path factors, wisdom, initial application, the three abstinences, energy, mindfulness, one-pointedness and wrong view, in rooted cittas
What is conditioned
the 71 rooted cittas, their factors, and the matter arising with them

An example. Deciding not to repeat a piece of gossip: right intention and right speech, with mindfulness and effort, lead the citta toward a good end, and condition the mind-born matter of the closed mouth.

Not to be confused with

  • jhāna. Jhāna factors fix the mind on its object. Path factors move it toward a destination.
  • the noble eightfold path. The supramundane path is path condition at its fullest. Path condition itself includes the mundane path and the wrong one.

Manual: VIII §14; VII §17; Table 8.3; Guide to VII §38 · Suttas: SN 45.8

Which way it works

  • mind → mind. The path factors, right or wrong, lead the mind in their direction: in every citta but the eighteen rootless ones.
  • mind → matter. The path factors also support the matter that their citta produces.

The same factors, as a category

In chapter VII, the path factors work as this condition. Path factors are path condition: they lead the mind toward a destination, good or bad.
