Absence

natthi

Absence is help by ceasing: a citta that has gone gives the next its opportunity.

Direction
Mind → mind
Group
Mind → mind
Manual
VIII §§11–28

In depth

Absence condition, natthi-paccayanatthi · absence Absence is help by ceasing: a citta that has gone gives the next its opportunity., is help by ceasing. A citta and its factors, by passing away, give the next citta the opportunity to arise immediately after. Since two cittas cannot occur at once in a single stream, the ending of one is a genuine help to the next: it clears the way.

Its conditioning and conditioned states are exactly those of proximity: every citta and its factors just ceased, except the Arahant's death citta, for the citta and factors that follow. The Manual's Guide treats absence and disappearance as one relation under two names (Guide to VIII §13), and the whole family, proximity, contiguity, absence and disappearance, falls under decisive support, one of the four conditions into which the Manual folds all twenty-four.

The name can mislead. Absence here does not mean that something is missing, as when a mind lacks greed. It means a state that has been and is now gone, and whose going is the help. It is the opposite of presence: presence helps by being here, absence by having just left.

What conditions
every citta and its factors that has just ceased, except the death citta of an Arahant (as for proximity)
What is conditioned
the citta and factors that arise immediately after

An example. Turning from a worry to the sound of rain: the last citta of worry passes away, and its passing gives the first citta of attending to the rain the chance to arise.

Not to be confused with

  • proximity. The same states. Proximity stresses that the next arises at once with nothing between; absence stresses that the earlier one has gone to make room.
  • the lack of a quality. A citta free of hatred is described by what it contains, not by absence condition. Absence condition is always a ceased citta helping the next.
