Condition · 6 of 24

Conascence

sahajāta

States that arise together help one another to arise, as a flame and its light: a citta and its factors, the four great elements.

Direction
Mind & matter → both
Group
Mind & matter → both
Manual
VIII §§11–28

In depth

Conascence condition, sahajāta-paccayasahajāta · conascence support between things arising at the same moment, like a lamp and its light, is help by arising together. The conditioning state, on arising, makes the conditioned states arise at the same moment with it. The Manual gives three kinds, and its Guide refines them into five. They are: each citta and factor for the other mental states with it; mental states for the matter arising with them; each great element for the other three; the great elements for the derived matter in their group; and, at rebirth, the heart-base and the mental aggregates for each other.

The matter that arises together with a citta is of two kinds, as the Manual points out at the end of its account. In the course of life it is mind-born matter. At rebirth-linking it is kamma-born matter, since the rebirth citta does not itself produce matter.

Conascence need not run both ways. The great elements condition derived matter, but derived matter does not condition them. A citta conditions its mind-born matter, but that matter does not condition the citta. Where help does run both ways, mutuality is added. Conascence is the head of a family: support of the conascent kind, presence and non-disappearance of the conascent kind, all share its states.

What conditions
all cittas and mental factors; the four great elements; and at rebirth in the five-aggregate planes, the mental aggregates and the heart-base
What is conditioned
the cittas and factors arising with them and the matter arising with them; the other great elements and the derived matter of their group; and at rebirth, the heart-base and mental aggregates for each other

An example. A flash of anger: the citta of anger and its factors arise together and help each other into being, and the heat and tension of mind-born matter in the face arise with them.

When a lamp is lit, its flame brings its light, colour and heat into being at the same moment.

Simile, after CMA VIII §20 Guide

Not to be confused with

  • mutuality. Mutuality is conascence that runs both ways at once. Every mutuality is conascence, but not every conascence is mutual.
  • association. Association is only between mental states. Conascence also includes matter.

Manual: VIII §20; VIII §27; Table 8.3

Which way it works

  • mind → mind. A citta and its factors arise together and help one another to arise.
  • mind → matter. A citta helps the matter that arises with it: mind-born matter in the course of life, kamma-born matter at rebirth.
  • matter → matter. The four great elements arise together and help each other and the derived matter arising with them.
  • matter → mind. At rebirth, the heart-base and the rebirth-linking citta arise together and help each other.
