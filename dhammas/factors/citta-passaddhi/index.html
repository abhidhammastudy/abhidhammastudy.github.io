Tranquillity of consciousness

In depth

Tranquillity of consciousness, citta-passaddhicitta-passaddhi · tranquillity of consciousness Tranquillity of consciousness calms the citta itself, as tranquillity of factors calms its companions., quiets the disturbance in the citta itself, as its partner quiets the disturbance in the factors. The two are defined alike in every respect and differ only in what they work on (II §5). It is present in every beautiful citta.

Why name one quality twice? The Manual explains only that one member extends to the mental body, the group of factors, and the other to consciousness itself (II §5). Six qualities, tranquillity, lightness, malleability, wieldiness, proficiency and rectitude, are each given this double form, while the other beautiful universals are not. Each pair targets a family of defilements: here, restlessness and worry, the states that stir up distress (II §5). Together the two form the enlightenment factor of tranquillity, which among the thirty-seven requisites occurs only once (VII §§29, 33).

The point newcomers miss is that tranquillity is not the absence of thought. A beautiful citta may be quick, busy with reflection or full of joy, and still be tranquil in this sense, because nothing in it is fretting. It is the quiet of a mind with nothing to defend, not of a mind with nothing to do.

Not to be confused with

  • tranquillity of factors. The same calm working on the citta's companion factors rather than on the citta itself.
  • worry. Worry frets over past deeds and comes with displeasure; tranquillity of consciousness is its direct opposite.

In your own experience. While working on something absorbing, notice that the mind can be fast without being frantic. That unfretful quality under the activity is tranquillity of consciousness.

Manual: II §5; II §15; VII §29; VII §33 · Suttas: SN 12.23, MN 118
