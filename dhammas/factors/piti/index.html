Mental factor

Zest

pīti

Zest is delight in the object, the gladness that refreshes body and mind. The commentaries describe five grades, from a slight thrill to an uplifting rapture. It occurs only with joyful feeling.

Occurs in
35 of 89 cittas (51 of 121)
Group
Occasionals
Manual
II §§1–9

In depth

Zest, pītipīti · zest delight in the object; a jhāna factor up to the third jhāna, is delight in the object, the refreshing interest that lifts body and mind. It arises in fifty-one cittas on the fuller count of 121 (thirty-five of the 89). The Manual reaches the number by exclusion: zest is absent wherever feeling is displeasure or equanimity, from both kinds of body-consciousness, and from the fourth jhāna (II §11). So zest comes only with joyful feeling, but joyful feeling does not always bring zest.

That fourth-jhāna case is the instructive one. In the fivefold scheme the fourth jhāna keeps joy (sukhasukha · bodily pleasure pleasant bodily feeling: only in wholesome-resultant body-consciousness) but lets zest fade, because zest is the coarser of the two: it belongs among the formations, while happiness is a feeling. In the first three jhānas zest is a jhāna factor that holds off ill will. Among the requisites it is the enlightenment factor of zest, one of the three that rouse a sluggish mind (VII §29), and it is one of the two requisites that may be missing from a path citta (VII §33).

A point newcomers miss is that zest is ethically variable. The thrill of a greedy citta at a coveted object is zest just as much as the gladness of a generous gift. What differs is the company: greed and delusion in one case, faith and non-greed in the other.

A traveller worn out in the desert hears of a pool at the edge of a wood and is glad: that gladness is zest. Bathing and drinking there, he is content: that is happiness.

Simile, after Visuddhimagga IV

Not to be confused with

  • feeling. Joyful feeling is the pleasant tone of the experience; zest is the eager delight in the object. The first is in the feeling aggregate, the second among the formations.
  • greed. Zest delights; greed grasps. Zest can rejoice in a good deed without wanting anything from it.

In your own experience. Recall a moment of real gladness at someone's kindness. Notice the lift, almost bodily, that came before any contentment settled. That lift is zest.
