Mental factor · 1 · Universals

phassa

contact

The meeting of mind and object. Contact is what makes an experience happen at all: the coming together of a sense base, an object and consciousness. Feeling follows from it.

Occurs in
every citta
Its roles
a nutriment
Family
Universals

Not to be confused with

  • feeling. Contact is the meeting; feeling is the tone that meeting has. In dependent arising feeling is conditioned by contact, but in one citta the two arise together.
  • attention. Attention turns the mind's companions toward the object; contact is their actual meeting with it.

Mental factor · 2 · Universals

vedanā

feeling

The tone of an experience: pleasant, painful or neither. Feeling is said to "enjoy" the object, the way a king enjoys a meal that others have only tasted. Five kinds are counted: bodily pleasure and pain, joy, displeasure, and equanimity.

Occurs in
every citta
Its roles
a jhāna factor, a faculty
Family
Universals

Not to be confused with

  • zest. Zest is delight in the object and belongs to the formations aggregate; joyful feeling is the pleasant tone itself. Zest always comes with joy, but joy can be present without zest, as in the fourth jhāna of the fivefold scheme.
  • neutrality of mind. Equanimous feeling is a neutral tone. Neutrality of mind is a balancing quality present in every beautiful citta, even a joyful one.

Mental factor · 3 · Universals

saññā

perception

Perception marks an object so that it can be known again, as a carpenter marks timber. It recognises by signs, which is why it can also mislead, as a scarecrow fools deer.

Occurs in
every citta
Family
Universals

Not to be confused with

  • wisdom. Perception recognises by marks it has stored; wisdom penetrates the thing's nature. One can perceive a feeling as 'mine' a thousand times and never see it as impermanent.
  • mindfulness. Perception records and recognises; mindfulness keeps the mind from drifting off what is present. Strong perception supports mindfulness but is not the same factor.

Mental factor · 4 · Universals

cetanā

volition

Volition gathers the other factors and directs them on the object, like a foreman who works and keeps others working. In wholesome and unwholesome cittas it is kamma.

Occurs in
every citta
Its roles
a nutriment
Family
Universals

Not to be confused with

  • desire to act. Desire to act is the wish to do something, and it is absent from some cittas. Volition is present in every citta; it is the doing itself, coordinating the other factors.
  • decision. Decision settles on the object without wavering; volition works on it and sets the rest of the mind working.
Open the entry →

Mental factor · 5 · Universals

ekaggatā

one-pointedness

One-pointedness holds the citta on its single object without wavering. Present in every moment, developed it becomes concentration, the factor that defines jhāna.

Occurs in
every citta
Its roles
a jhāna factor, a path factor, a faculty, a power, a requisite of enlightenment
Family
Universals

Not to be confused with

  • mindfulness. One-pointedness holds the mind on one object; mindfulness keeps it from drifting and guards what is happening. A greedy citta can have the first but never the second.
  • sustained application. Sustained application keeps the mind moving over the object and drops away in the higher jhānas. One-pointedness grows steadier as it does.

Mental factor · 6 · Universals

jīvitindriya

life faculty

The life faculty sustains the mental factors it arises with for the span of their moment, as water keeps lotuses alive. Matter has a life faculty of its own.

Occurs in
every citta
Its roles
a faculty
Family
Universals

Not to be confused with

  • the life faculty of matter. The material life faculty is one of the twenty-eight kinds of matter and sustains kamma-born matter. This factor is mental and sustains only the mental states of its own moment. They share a name but are counted as two (VII §23).
  • volition. Volition drives and organises the other factors; the life faculty only keeps them present while they last.
Open the entry →

Mental factor · 7 · Universals

manasikāra

attention

Attention turns the mind toward the object, as a rudder steers a ship. Whether it turns wisely (yoniso) or unwisely decides much of what follows.

Occurs in
every citta
Family
Universals

Not to be confused with

  • initial application. Attention turns its companions toward the object; initial application lifts them onto it. Attention is in every citta; initial application is absent from sense-consciousness and from the higher jhānas.
  • mindfulness. Attention is ethically variable and present in unwholesome cittas too. Mindfulness is only beautiful.

Mental factor · 8 · Occasionals

vitakka

initial application

Initial application lifts the mind onto its object, like striking a bell. It is the first factor of the first jhāna and falls away in the higher ones; as wrong or right intention it is also a path factor.

Occurs in
55 of 89 cittas
Its roles
a jhāna factor, a path factor, a requisite of enlightenment
Family
Occasionals

Not to be confused with

  • sustained application. Initial application is the lift onto the object, like a bird spreading its wings to rise; sustained application is the continued work on it, like the bird gliding on outstretched wings.
  • attention. Attention turns the mind toward the object and is present in every citta. Initial application places the mind on it and is a specialised factor.
Open the entry →

Mental factor · 9 · Occasionals

vicāra

sustained application

Sustained application keeps the mind moving over its object, like the bell's resonance after the strike. It is subtler than initial application and lasts one jhāna longer in the fivefold scheme.

Occurs in
58 of 89 cittas (66 of 121)
Its roles
a jhāna factor
Family
Occasionals

Not to be confused with

  • initial application. Initial application strikes the object; sustained application lingers on it. Sustained application is subtler and survives one jhāna longer.
  • wisdom. Sustained application keeps the mind examining in the sense of staying with; it is in unwholesome cittas too. Wisdom, the investigation of states, actually discerns.
Open the entry →

Mental factor · 10 · Occasionals

adhimokkha

decision

Decision settles the mind on its object, like a firm pillar. It is absent from moments of doubt, since doubt is exactly the failure to settle.

Occurs in
78 of 89 cittas (110 of 121)
Family
Occasionals

Not to be confused with

  • faith. Faith is trust in what is worthy and clears the mind; it is only beautiful. Decision is mere settledness about the object, present in greed and hatred as readily as in generosity.
  • doubt. Doubt is the failure to settle; decision is settling. They exclude each other, which is why the doubting citta lacks decision.
Open the entry →

Mental factor · 11 · Occasionals

viriya

energy

Energy supports the other factors and keeps them from collapsing, like new timbers propping a sagging house. It serves wholesome and unwholesome states alike; rightly directed it is right effort.

Occurs in
73 of 89 cittas (105 of 121)
Its roles
a path factor, a faculty, a power, a predominant, a requisite of enlightenment
Family
Occasionals

Not to be confused with

  • desire to act. Desire to act wants to do something; energy holds the mind up while it is done. Either may lead the citta as predominant, but only one at a time.
  • restlessness. Restlessness is agitation without direction. Energy is support, and it is calmed rather than lost when tranquillity and concentration grow.

Mental factor · 12 · Occasionals

pīti

zest

Zest is delight in the object, the gladness that refreshes body and mind. The commentaries describe five grades, from a slight thrill to an uplifting rapture. It occurs only with joyful feeling.

Occurs in
35 of 89 cittas (51 of 121)
Its roles
a jhāna factor, a requisite of enlightenment
Family
Occasionals

Not to be confused with

  • feeling. Joyful feeling is the pleasant tone of the experience; zest is the eager delight in the object. The first is in the feeling aggregate, the second among the formations.
  • greed. Zest delights; greed grasps. Zest can rejoice in a good deed without wanting anything from it.
Open the entry →

Mental factor · 13 · Occasionals

chanda

desire to act

Desire to act is wanting to do something with the object. It is not greed: it can be the wish to give, to learn or to practise, and as a predominant it can lead the whole mind.

Occurs in
69 of 89 cittas (101 of 121)
Its roles
a predominant, a requisite of enlightenment
Family
Occasionals

Not to be confused with

  • greed. Greed sticks to its object and does not let go; desire to act only reaches toward a goal and can rest when it is reached. Greed is always unwholesome, desire to act is not.
  • volition. Volition is in every citta and does the organising; desire to act is the further wish to accomplish, absent from twenty cittas.

Mental factor · 14 · Unwholesome factors

moha

delusion

Delusion blinds the mind to the true nature of its object. It is the root of all unwholesome states and is present in every one of them.

Occurs in
12 of 89 cittas
Its roles
a root
Family
Unwholesome factors

Not to be confused with

  • wrong view. Delusion is not seeing; wrong view is seeing wrongly, actively adopting a false account. Delusion is in every unwholesome citta, wrong view only in four greed-rooted ones.
  • doubt. Doubt is a wavering that delusion makes possible; it occurs in one citta only, with delusion as its root.
Open the entry →

Mental factor · 15 · Unwholesome factors

ahirika

shamelessness

Shamelessness is the absence of disgust at wrongdoing, the lack of an inner conscience that would recoil from it.

Occurs in
12 of 89 cittas
Its roles
a power
Family
Unwholesome factors

Not to be confused with

  • fearlessness of wrongdoing. Shamelessness lacks the inner check of self-respect; fearlessness of wrongdoing lacks the outer check of regard for others and for consequences.
  • shame. Shame and shamelessness never meet in one citta. Where one is, the other is not, since no citta is both beautiful and unwholesome.
Open the entry →

Mental factor · 16 · Unwholesome factors

anottappa

fearlessness of wrongdoing

Fearlessness of wrongdoing is the absence of dread at the consequences of bad action, the lack of the outer conscience that would hold one back.

Occurs in
12 of 89 cittas
Its roles
a power
Family
Unwholesome factors

Not to be confused with

  • shamelessness. Shamelessness is the missing inner check; fearlessness of wrongdoing is the missing outer one. The two are never apart.
  • energy. Boldness in a good cause is energy with faith and wisdom. Fearlessness of wrongdoing is found only where the mind is unwholesome.
Open the entry →

Mental factor · 17 · Unwholesome factors

uddhacca

restlessness

Restlessness is the mind's agitation, like water whipped by wind. It is present in every unwholesome citta and gives its name to one of the two delusion-rooted cittas.

Occurs in
12 of 89 cittas
Family
Unwholesome factors

Not to be confused with

  • worry. Restlessness is agitation in the present; worry is remorse over what was done or left undone. Restlessness is in every unwholesome citta, worry only occasionally in the hate-rooted ones.
  • energy. Energy supports the mind; restlessness scatters it. Too much energy without concentration can tip into restlessness, but the two are different factors.
Open the entry →

Mental factor · 18 · Unwholesome factors

lobha

greed

Greed grasps its object and does not let go, like birdlime. It ranges from faint liking to raging lust and appears in more of the Manual's lists of defilements than any other factor.

Occurs in
8 of 89 cittas
Its roles
a root
Family
Unwholesome factors

Not to be confused with

  • desire to act. Desire to act wants to accomplish and can be wholesome. Greed wants to hold, and is always unwholesome.
  • zest. Zest is delight and occurs in generous cittas too; greed is the grasping that can ride on that delight.
Open the entry →

Mental factor · 19 · Unwholesome factors

diṭṭhi

wrong view

Wrong view is adherence to a mistaken interpretation, above all the view of a self. It accompanies only greed-rooted cittas, since holding to a view is itself a kind of grasping.

Occurs in
4 of 89 cittas
Its roles
a path factor
Family
Unwholesome factors

Not to be confused with

  • delusion. Delusion is not seeing; wrong view is seeing amiss and holding to it. All unwholesome cittas have delusion, only four have wrong view.
  • conceit. Wrong view misinterprets; conceit measures oneself against others. Both rest on greed, but they cannot share a citta.

Mental factor · 20 · Unwholesome factors

māna

conceit

Conceit is measuring oneself against others, as better, equal or worse. It arises only in greed-rooted cittas, never together with wrong view.

Occurs in
4 of 89 cittas
Family
Unwholesome factors

Not to be confused with

  • wrong view. Wrong view says what the self is; conceit compares it. A stream-enterer has abandoned the first but not the second.
  • shame. Low self-estimation is conceit, not shame. Shame recoils from wrongdoing; it does not rank the self.
Open the entry →

Mental factor · 21 · Unwholesome factors

dosa

hatred

Hatred is the mind's striking out against its object, from mild irritation to fury. It always comes with displeasure, and roots two cittas.

Occurs in
2 of 89 cittas
Its roles
a root
Family
Unwholesome factors

Not to be confused with

  • worry. Worry is remorse over one's own deeds and arises only in a hate-rooted citta, always with hatred. Hatred can arise without worry.
  • fear of wrongdoing. Anxious fear is a form of hatred, with displeasure. Fear of wrongdoing is a beautiful factor that holds back from harm without any such distress.
Open the entry →

Mental factor · 22 · Unwholesome factors

issā

envy

Envy is resentment at another's success or good fortune. It arises only in the hate-rooted cittas, and only occasionally.

Occurs in
2 of 89 cittas
Family
Unwholesome factors

Not to be confused with

  • greed. Greed wants the thing; envy resents another's having it. They never arise in the same citta.
  • avarice. Envy looks outward at others' success; avarice looks inward at one's own and does not want to share it.
Open the entry →

Mental factor · 23 · Unwholesome factors

macchariya

avarice

Avarice is the wish to keep one's own good fortune from others. It arises only in hate-rooted cittas, never with envy or worry.

Occurs in
2 of 89 cittas
Family
Unwholesome factors

Not to be confused with

  • greed. Greed clings to the object with liking. Avarice is the displeasure at the thought of others having a share in it, and comes with aversion.
  • envy. Avarice guards one's own good fortune; envy resents another's.
Open the entry →

Mental factor · 24 · Unwholesome factors

kukkucca

worry

Worry is regret over what was done or left undone, the mind going back over the past with distress.

Occurs in
2 of 89 cittas
Family
Unwholesome factors

Not to be confused with

  • restlessness. Restlessness is agitation in every unwholesome citta; worry is the specific distress of regret, occurring only occasionally with hatred.
  • shame. Shame recoils from wrongdoing without distress and is beautiful. Worry is distressed self-recrimination and is unwholesome.
Open the entry →

Mental factor · 25 · Unwholesome factors

thīna

sloth

Sloth is the mind's sluggishness, its loss of drive. It occurs only in the prompted unwholesome cittas, always with torpor.

Occurs in
5 of 89 cittas
Family
Unwholesome factors

Not to be confused with

  • torpor. Sloth is sluggishness of consciousness; torpor is heaviness of the mental factors. They always come as a pair, but are two factors.
  • energy. Sloth is the direct opposite of energy. Lightness is the beautiful factor that dissolves its heaviness.
Open the entry →

Mental factor · 26 · Unwholesome factors

middha

torpor

Torpor is the unwieldiness of the mental factors, their heaviness. With sloth it forms one of the five hindrances.

Occurs in
5 of 89 cittas
Family
Unwholesome factors

Not to be confused with

  • sloth. Sloth dulls the citta; torpor weighs down its factors. They are always together but are separate factors.
  • tranquillity of factors. Calm can look heavy from outside, but tranquillity is cool and alert; torpor is clouded and sinking.
Open the entry →

Mental factor · 27 · Unwholesome factors

vicikicchā

doubt

Doubt is the mind's indecision about the Buddha, the Dhamma, the path and the nature of things. It is not a root: it occurs in one citta only, the delusion-rooted citta accompanied by doubt, and stream-entry ends it.

Occurs in
1 of 89 cittas
Family
Unwholesome factors

Not to be confused with

  • decision. Decision settles the mind on its object; doubt cannot settle. Where one is present the other is absent.
  • wisdom. Wisdom investigates and sees; doubt investigates without arriving. Wisdom is what finally dissolves doubt.
Open the entry →

Mental factor · 28 · Beautiful factors

saddhā

faith

Faith is confidence in what deserves it, which clears the mind as a water-clearing gem settles mud. It is present in every beautiful citta and is the first of the five faculties.

Occurs in
59 of 89 cittas (91 of 121)
Its roles
a faculty, a power, a requisite of enlightenment
Family
Beautiful factors

Not to be confused with

  • decision. Decision settles the mind on any object, wholesome or not. Faith is confidence in what is worthy and occurs only in beautiful cittas.
  • greed. Devotion that clings to a teacher's person is greed dressed as faith. Faith clears the mind; clinging clouds it.
Open the entry →

Mental factor · 29 · Beautiful factors

sati

mindfulness

Mindfulness keeps the mind from floating away from its object, as a post anchors a boat. It is present in every beautiful citta and is the seventh path factor.

Occurs in
59 of 89 cittas (91 of 121)
Its roles
a path factor, a faculty, a power, a requisite of enlightenment
Family
Beautiful factors

Not to be confused with

  • attention. Attention turns the mind toward anything, wisely or not, and is in every citta. Mindfulness is present only in beautiful cittas.
  • one-pointedness. One-pointedness unifies; mindfulness guards and keeps present. Unwholesome cittas can have the first, never the second.
  • perception. Perception remembers by marks; mindfulness keeps the present in view. Strong perception is mindfulness's proximate cause.
Open the entry →

Mental factor · 30 · Beautiful factors

hiri

shame

Shame is recoiling from wrongdoing out of self-respect: the inner guardian of the world, the commentaries call it.

Occurs in
59 of 89 cittas (91 of 121)
Its roles
a power
Family
Beautiful factors

Not to be confused with

  • fear of wrongdoing. Shame holds back out of self-respect; fear of wrongdoing out of regard for consequences and for others.
  • worry. Worry is distressed regret after the fact and is unwholesome. Shame is a clear recoil, often before the act, and beautiful.
Open the entry →

Mental factor · 31 · Beautiful factors

ottappa

fear of wrongdoing

Fear of wrongdoing is recoiling from it out of regard for its consequences and for others: the outer guardian of the world.

Occurs in
59 of 89 cittas (91 of 121)
Its roles
a power
Family
Beautiful factors

Not to be confused with

  • shame. Shame recoils from within, out of self-respect; fear of wrongdoing from without, out of regard for consequences and for others.
  • hatred. Anxious fear is a form of hatred. Fear of wrongdoing is beautiful and comes with joy or equanimity, never with displeasure.
Open the entry →

Mental factor · 32 · Beautiful factors

alobha

non-greed

Non-greed is not clinging to the object, like a drop of water on a lotus leaf. It shows as generosity and renunciation.

Occurs in
59 of 89 cittas (91 of 121)
Its roles
a root
Family
Beautiful factors

Not to be confused with

  • neutrality of mind. Non-greed does not cling; neutrality of mind keeps the citta and its factors evenly balanced. Both are in every beautiful citta, doing different work.
  • feeling. Non-greed is not indifference or a flat neutral tone. It can come with great joy, as when one gives gladly.
Open the entry →

Mental factor · 33 · Beautiful factors

adosa

non-hatred

Non-hatred is the mind's not opposing its object: friendliness and patience, not only the absence of ill will. Developed toward beings it becomes loving-kindness.

Occurs in
59 of 89 cittas (91 of 121)
Its roles
a root
Family
Beautiful factors

Not to be confused with

  • compassion. Non-hatred, as loving-kindness, wishes beings well. Compassion is a separate factor that wishes their suffering removed, and arises only when suffering is the object.
  • greed. Affection that wants something back is greed, the near enemy of loving-kindness. Non-hatred asks nothing for itself.
Open the entry →

Mental factor · 34 · Beautiful factors

tatramajjhattatā

neutrality of mind

Neutrality of mind keeps the citta and its factors in balance, as a charioteer watches well-trained horses. Developed toward beings it becomes the equanimity of the illimitables.

Occurs in
59 of 89 cittas (91 of 121)
Its roles
a requisite of enlightenment
Family
Beautiful factors

Not to be confused with

  • feeling. Equanimous feeling is a neutral tone; neutrality of mind is a balancing quality and occurs with joyful feeling as readily as with neutral.
  • delusion. Not caring because one does not see is delusion. Neutrality of mind sees fully and stays even.
Open the entry →

Mental factor · 35 · Beautiful factors

kāya-passaddhi

tranquillity of factors

Tranquillity of the mental factors calms their disturbance; it is the opposite of restlessness and worry.

Occurs in
59 of 89 cittas (91 of 121)
Its roles
a requisite of enlightenment
Family
Beautiful factors

Not to be confused with

  • tranquillity of consciousness. The same calm, applied to the citta itself rather than to its companion factors. The two always arise together.

Condition · 1 · Mind → mind & matter

hetu

root

The six roots, greed, hatred, delusion and their opposites, make the states they arise with firm, like the roots of a tree.

Direction
Mind → mind & matter

Not to be confused with

  • decisive support. Root works within a single moment. The influence of past greed or past faith on the present is decisive support.
  • cause in general. In the suttas hetuhetu · root one of six factors that steady a citta: greed, hatred, delusion, or their opposites often means any cause. In the Paṭṭhāna it means only these six roots.

Condition · 13 · Mind → mind & matter

kamma

kamma · ★ One of the four master conditions

A volition helps its result: at the same moment, by organising the citta, and across time, as past kamma that ripens later.

Direction
Mind → mind & matter

Not to be confused with

  • result. Kamma is the volition that ripens. Result is the help that the ripened states give each other, by being passive.
  • decisive support. Strong past kamma also supports its results by natural decisive support. Kamma condition names its specific force, the volition producing its fruit.

Condition · 14 · Mind → mind & matter

vipāka

result

Resultant states help one another by their passivity, effortlessly, as fruit ripens.

Direction
Mind → mind & matter

Not to be confused with

  • kamma. Kamma condition is the past volition producing a result. Result condition is the help the resultant states give one another, now.
  • functional cittas. Functional cittas are also not kamma, but they are active; they are not resultant, and do not condition by result.

Condition · 17 · Mind → mind & matter

jhāna

jhāna

The jhāna factors help by making the mind look closely at its object, in ordinary moments as well as in meditation.

Direction
Mind → mind & matter

Not to be confused with

  • jhāna as attainment. The jhānas of meditation are states in which these factors become strong and steady. The condition is the same five factors at work in any citta.
  • path. Jhāna factors make the mind look closely. Path factors make it a road toward a destination, good or bad.

Condition · 18 · Mind → mind & matter

magga

path

The path factors lead the mind toward a destination, a good one or a bad one.

Direction
Mind → mind & matter

Not to be confused with

  • jhāna. Jhāna factors fix the mind on its object. Path factors move it toward a destination.
  • the noble eightfold path. The supramundane path is path condition at its fullest. Path condition itself includes the mundane path and the wrong one.

Condition · 11 · Mind → matter

pacchājāta

postnascence

Postnascence is help from something that arises later, to matter already there: later cittas sustain the body, as later rain helps plants that have already grown.

Direction
Mind → matter

Not to be confused with

  • prenascence. Its mirror image. In prenascence matter that arose earlier helps later mind; in postnascence later mind helps matter that arose earlier.
  • mind-born matter. Mind-born matter arises together with its citta, by conascence. Postnascence helps matter already standing, from any origin.

Condition · 10 · Matter → mind

purejāta

prenascence

Prenascence is help from something that arose earlier and still exists: a sense base or an object already present when the citta arises.

Direction
Matter → mind

Not to be confused with

  • object condition. Object condition covers past, future and timeless objects. Object prenascence is only present concrete matter, arisen before the citta.
  • postnascence. Its mirror: in postnascence a later citta helps matter that arose earlier.

Condition · 2 · Anything → mind

ārammaṇa

object · ★ One of the four master conditions

Anything at all that a citta takes as its object helps it simply by being known: a colour, a thought, a concept, Nibbāna.

Direction
Anything → mind

Not to be confused with

  • object prenascence. Object condition covers objects of all times and none. Object prenascence covers only present concrete matter that arose before the citta.
  • object predominance. Every object is an object condition; only an object held in high regard is a predominance.

Condition · 9 · Anything → mind

upanissaya

decisive support · ★ One of the four master conditions

Decisive support is strong help, often from far away: a good friend, a climate, a past habit, a resolve, even Nibbāna as an object.

Direction
Anything → mind

Not to be confused with

  • support. Support is a present foundation, such as the eye for seeing. Decisive support is a strong reason, usually from earlier.
  • repetition. Repetition strengthens the next javana in the same process. A habit built over years is natural decisive support.

Condition · 3 · Mind & matter → both

adhipati

predominance

A predominant leads the mind, as a king leads ministers: desire, energy, consciousness or investigation in its conascent kind, or a highly esteemed object in its object kind.

Direction
Mind & matter → both

Not to be confused with

  • faculty. Faculties are like ministers, each controlling one domain, and many act together. A predominant rules the whole citta, one at a time.
  • object decisive support. The same objects, seen from another side: as predominance they attract and dominate; as decisive support they strongly help the mind to arise.

Condition · 6 · Mind & matter → both

sahajāta

conascence

States that arise together help one another to arise, as a flame and its light: a citta and its factors, the four great elements.

Direction
Mind & matter → both

Not to be confused with

  • mutuality. Mutuality is conascence that runs both ways at once. Every mutuality is conascence, but not every conascence is mutual.
  • association. Association is only between mental states. Conascence also includes matter.

Condition · 7 · Mind & matter → both

aññamañña

mutuality

Mutuality is help in both directions at once, like the legs of a tripod: each state supports and is supported by the others.

Direction
Mind & matter → both

Not to be confused with

  • conascence. Conascence can be one-way, as from great elements to derived matter. Mutuality is always both ways.
  • association. Association links only mental states, by shared arising, ceasing, object and base. Mutuality also links great elements, and at rebirth the heart-base with mind.

Condition · 8 · Mind & matter → both

nissaya

support

Support is help by being a foundation, as the earth supports trees: the great elements for derived matter, the eye for seeing.

Direction
Mind & matter → both

Not to be confused with

  • decisive support. Support is a foundation present now. Decisive support is strong dependence, often across time, on friends, habits, climate or past deeds.
  • conascence. Conascence support is the same as conascence. Support is wider, because it also includes the bases that arose before.

Condition · 15 · Mind & matter → both

āhāra

nutriment

The four nutriments sustain what they feed: edible food the body; contact, volition and consciousness the states they arise with.

Direction
Mind & matter → both

Not to be confused with

  • nutritive essence. Ojā is a kind of matter. Nutriment condition is the force with which it, and three mental factors, sustain what they feed.
  • faculty. A faculty controls its domain. A nutriment sustains and strengthens. Consciousness is both a faculty and a nutriment, in two different roles.

Condition · 16 · Mind & matter → both

indriya

faculty

A faculty controls its own domain, as a governor rules a province: the sense faculties, the life faculties, and the mental faculties such as faith and wisdom.

Direction
Mind & matter → both

Not to be confused with

  • predominance. Several faculties control at once, each in its own domain. A predominant leads the whole citta, and only one can do so at a time.
  • the sex faculties. They are faculties but not faculty conditions: they shape the body without producing, supporting or maintaining anything.

Condition · 20 · Mind & matter → both

vippayutta

dissociation

Dissociation is help between mind and matter, which cannot be associated but still support each other.

Direction
Mind & matter → both

Not to be confused with

  • association. Association links mental states into one unit. Dissociation links mind with matter, which stay distinct.
  • prenascence and postnascence. Prenascent and postnascent dissociation have the same states as prenascence support and postnascence; the name stresses that the two sides differ in kind.

Condition · 21 · Mind & matter → both

atthi

presence · ★ One of the four master conditions

Presence is help by being there at the same time, without which the other could not stand.

Direction
Mind & matter → both

Not to be confused with

  • conascence. Conascence requires arising together. Presence requires only overlap, so a body arisen earlier can be helped by a later citta.
  • absence. Absence helps by being gone; presence helps by still being here.

Condition · 24 · Mind & matter → both

avigata

non-disappearance

Non-disappearance is help by not yet having vanished, as a base or a meal still sustains what depends on it; it is presence under another name.

Direction
Mind & matter → both

Not to be confused with

  • presence. The same states and the same five kinds; non-disappearance stresses that the helper has not yet vanished.
  • disappearance. Its opposite: help by having vanished, from a citta to the next.
