Matter

Space

ākāsa

Space: the gap that separates one group of matter from another. It is not a thing but the boundary between things.

Origins
kamma, mind, temperature, nutriment
Kind
a quality of matter
Group
Limiting
Manual
VI §§2–15

In depth

The space element, ākāsa-dhātuākāsa · space Space: the gap that separates one group of matter from another., is the gap between groups of matter. It is not geometric extension, and not a container. It is the boundary that keeps one material group from merging with the next, so that groups can be told apart. It marks off one group from another, makes their edges evident, and appears as the borders of matter and the holes and gaps between things.

Space is non-concrete matter. It has no nature of its own beyond the groups it separates, and so it is not an ultimate reality. For the same reason it is not counted as a member of any material group: it lies between groups, not inside them. Yet it is said to arise from all four origins, derivatively, because it is the space between groups that each origin produces. There is kamma-born space between kamma-born groups, mind-born space between mind-born groups, and so on.

The suttas speak of internal space as the openings of the body: the ear-holes, nostrils, mouth and passages for food. The Abhidhamma refines this to the gaps between every group, however small. A newcomer often confuses this with the boundless space of the first immaterial attainment, which is something else entirely.

Not to be confused with

  • infinite space in meditation. The base of boundless space is the object of an immaterial jhāna: a concept reached by removing the kasiṇa. The space element is a mere boundary between groups of matter.
  • emptiness. Space is a material boundary. Emptiness in the teaching means being empty of self, which applies to all dhammas, space included.

In your own experience. Spread the fingers and look at the gaps between them. What is seen is colour; the gap is understood as the edge where one thing ends and another begins.
