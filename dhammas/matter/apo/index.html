Matter

Water

āpo

The water element: cohesion and fluidity. It holds the other elements together and cannot be touched directly.

Origins
kamma, mind, temperature, nutriment
Kind
concrete
Group
Great elements
Manual
VI §§2–15

In depth

The water element, āpo-dhātuāpo · water element cohesion, which holds matter together; known by mind, not by touch, is cohesion: the quality that binds the particles of matter together so that they do not scatter. The commentaries describe it by its flowing, by the way it intensifies the matter around it, and by its binding of that matter into one piece. It is concrete matter and one of the four great elements.

Like the other great elements it is one of the eight inseparables, present in every material group, and it can come from any of the four origins. A stone has it as surely as a river does; the stone simply holds together more stiffly.

What newcomers most often miss is that water cannot be touched. The tangible is made of only three of the great elements: earth, fire and air. When we put a hand in water, the Abhidhamma says, what body-consciousness actually meets is coolness (fire), softness (earth) and pressure (air); the cohesion itself is known only by the mind, by inference. For the same reason water is counted among the subtle kinds of matter, not among the twelve that strike the senses.

Not to be confused with

  • the earth element. Earth is felt as hardness or softness. Water is never felt at all; it is what keeps the hard or soft thing in one piece.
  • liquid. A liquid is a mixture of all four elements in which cohesion and motion are prominent. The water element is one quality within it, and also within ice and stone.

In your own experience. Touch water and note what is actually felt: cool, soft, moving. Then notice that the feeling of wetness is assembled from these; the binding itself is understood, not touched.
