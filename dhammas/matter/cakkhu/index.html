Eye-sensitivity

Eye-sensitivity: the sensitive matter in the eye that visible form can strike. It is the base and door of seeing.

In depth

Eye-sensitivity, cakkhu-pasādacakkhu · eye-sensitivity Eye-sensitivity: the sensitive matter in the eye that visible form can strike., is a fine, clear matter in the eye that visible form can strike. It is not the eyeball. What we call the eye is a composite of many kinds of matter; the sensitivity is one of them, and the commentaries place it in the dark centre of the eye, soaked through its layers like oil through cotton. It is concrete, and born of kamma alone, kamma that the commentaries trace to a desire to see.

It occurs in the eye-decad: the eight inseparables, the life faculty and the sensitivity itself. For beings of spontaneous birth this decad can arise at conception; for the womb-born it appears later in the course of life. It is found in the sense world and in the fine-material world, and is lacking in those born blind.

Eye-sensitivity has several roles at once. It is a base, the physical support of eye-consciousness and of nothing else; a door, through which the whole eye-door process reaches its object; and a faculty, governing how keenly seeing sees. It takes its object without touching it. Its condition for seeing is prenascent: the sensitivity has to have arisen a moment before, and still stand, when seeing arises.

The suttas compare the senses to six animals tied by one rope, each straining toward its own haunt. The eye pulls toward visible things as a snake pulls toward its anthill.

Simile, after SN 35.247

Not to be confused with

  • the composite eye. The eyeball, with its colour, fluids and warmth, is many groups of matter. Only the sensitivity in it is the base of seeing.
  • eye-consciousness. The sensitivity is matter and knows nothing. Seeing is a citta that depends on it.

In your own experience. You cannot see the eye-sensitivity; you know it only through seeing. Close the eyes and open them, and notice that seeing begins at once, with no effort of yours.

Manual: VI §3; VI §7; VI §17; VI §23; III §20; VIII §24 · Suttas: SN 35.247, MN 43
