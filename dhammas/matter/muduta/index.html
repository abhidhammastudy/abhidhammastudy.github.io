Matter

Malleability

mudutā

Malleability of matter: its pliancy, the opposite of stiffness.

Origins
mind, temperature, nutriment
Kind
a quality of matter
Group
Mutable
Manual
VI §§2–15

In depth

Malleability of matter, rūpassa mudutā, is pliancy: the absence of rigidity. It removes stiffness, and it shows as a body that resists no action. It is non-concrete matter, one of the mutable kinds, and it arises only with lightness and wieldiness as the lightness triad.

Like its companions it comes from three origins: mind, temperature and nutriment, never kamma. It occurs in the same groups as the triad: the undecad of the lightness triad, the dodecads with bodily intimation or with sound, and the tridecad with vocal intimation and sound. It is not present at the moment of conception, and it is found wherever these groups occur, in the fine-material world and among non-percipient beings as well as in the sense world.

The newcomer's common mistake is to equate malleability with softness. Softness is the earth element felt at low strength; a soft body can still be stiff, as anyone with a cramp in soft muscle knows. Malleability is the body's compliance with what is asked of it, a quality that a calm mind, a mild climate and good food bring forth, and that fear, cold and poor food take away.

The commentaries compare it to a well-tanned hide, pliant enough to be worked into any shape.

Simile, after Visuddhimagga XIV

Not to be confused with

  • softness. Softness is a degree of the earth element, known by touch. Malleability is non-rigidity, a mode of the group, inferred from how the body responds.
  • malleability of the mental body. A beautiful mental factor with the same name. That one is mind; this one is matter.

In your own experience. Notice the jaw or shoulders during a moment of irritation, then after a few calm breaths. Their yielding when the mind settles is mind-born malleability.

Manual: VI §4; VI §14; VI §18; VI §29
