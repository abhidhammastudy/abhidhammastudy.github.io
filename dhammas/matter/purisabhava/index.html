In depth

Masculinity, purisabhāvapurisabhāva · masculinity Masculinity: the kamma-born faculty that shapes the marks and manner of a male body. or purisindriya, is the sex faculty of a male body. Like femininity it is concrete matter, born of kamma alone, and it occurs in the male decad: the eight inseparables, the life faculty and masculinity. It is said to pervade the whole body.

It shows maleness: it is what lies behind the male structure and features of the body and, in the old commentarial phrasing, behind typically male work and bearing. It is one of the eight material faculties, governing its domain, but like femininity it is not a faculty condition, because it neither produces, supports nor maintains anything.

Two points help a newcomer. First, sex here is kamma-born matter, not a choice or a role; and yet, because kamma-born matter is renewed at every moment, the commentaries can tell of a change of sex within one life, as in the story of Soreyya, who became a woman and later a man again. Second, the sex decad is not universal. The Manual notes that it can be missing at conception, and in the fine-material world there is no sex decad at all.

Not to be confused with

  • femininity. Its counterpart. Each occurs in its own decad; the commentaries say a being has only one at a time.
  • body-sensitivity. Both pervade the body, but body-sensitivity is a base and door of touch; masculinity is neither base nor door.

Manual: VI §3; VI §7; VI §17; VI §23; VIII §24
