Ear-sensitivity

Ear-sensitivity: the matter in the ear that sound can strike. It is the base and door of hearing.

In depth

Ear-sensitivity, sota-pasādasota · ear-sensitivity Ear-sensitivity: the matter in the ear that sound can strike., is the matter in the ear that sound can strike. The commentaries place it deep in the ear-hole, in a small ring-shaped place lined with fine brown hairs. Like all five sensitivities it is concrete matter, born of kamma alone, and it occurs in its own decad: the eight inseparables, the life faculty, and ear-sensitivity.

It is a base for ear-consciousness, a door for the whole ear-door process, and a faculty that governs how well hearing hears. With the eye it is classed as taking an object that does not touch it, in contrast to nose, tongue and body. The ear is found in the sense world and the fine-material world, though not in those born deaf.

Hearing has a special place in the teaching. The Guide to III §21 notes that the first path, stream-entry, cannot arise in the immaterial realms: it depends on hearing the Dhamma, and hearing needs an ear. Newcomers sometimes picture the ear as a passive opening; in this account each moment of hearing needs an ear-sensitivity that has already arisen and still stands, and a sound that has done the same.

Not to be confused with

  • the ear as an organ. The visible ear and canal are composite matter. Only the sensitive matter within is the base of hearing.
  • sound. Sound is the object that strikes. Ear-sensitivity is what is struck. Neither one hears; ear-consciousness does.

In your own experience. Sit with sounds as they come. Notice that hearing simply happens when a sound is there; the ear is known only through the hearing that depends on it.

Manual: VI §3; VI §7; VI §17; III §20; III §21 · Suttas: MN 43
