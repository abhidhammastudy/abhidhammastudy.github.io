The fourth path factor · Virtue

Right action sammā-kammanta

Holding back from killing, stealing and sexual misconduct.

In brief

  • Right action is abstaining from killing, stealing and sexual misconduct (SN 45.8).
  • In Abhidhamma terms it is an abstinence (virati), the deliberate refraining from wrong bodily action.
  • Before the path it reaches only the wrong at hand: refraining from taking a life is abstinence from killing, not from stealing.
  • At the path it cuts the disposition to every wrong deed.

What it means

SN 45.8 defines right action as abstaining from killing living beings, from stealing and from sexual misconduct (sn45.8:6.1–6.3). Like the other abstinences, before the path it is not a general good intention but a specific restraint that arises when an opportunity for the wrong act actually presents itself (Guide to CMA II §6).

In ordinary life it arises only in a sense-sphere wholesome citta, occasionally and alone, and only in part, matching the wrong at hand (CMA II §15 and Guide). At the path all three arise together, each complete, cutting the disposition to every wrong of its kind; in the fruition moments after it they return, as the purity the path has secured (Guide to II §15).

Rescuing a life or giving a gift is not right action in this narrow sense. Those deeds are wholesome, but the factor itself is only the refraining; good positive deeds are carried by volition, non-greed, non-hatred and the rest.

Its training: Virtue · sīla

Right speech, right action and right livelihood make up the training of virtue (MN 44, mn44:11.3): what we hold back from.

In Abhidhamma terms

An abstinence (virativirati · abstinences right speech, right action, right livelihood; singly in mundane cittas, together in a path) (CMA II §6; Guide to VII §30). Among the ten courses of wholesome action, the three bodily ones are this abstinence together with the volition arising with it (Guide to V §24).

Not to be confused with

Right livelihood. Right action refrains from bodily wrongs in general; when the same wrongs would be done for a living, it is right livelihood that refrains (AtthasālinīAtthasālinī · The Expositor Buddhaghosa's commentary on the Dhammasaṅgaṇī; source of the mango simile, CS §301, commentarial).

A story

The brahmins of Sālā: The brahmin householders of Sālā asked why some beings go to bad destinations after death and some to good ones. The Buddha named ten ways to act wrongly, three by body, four by speech and three by mind, and the ten ways to act rightly that are their opposite.
