The sixth path factor · Concentration

Right effort sammā-vāyāma

Working to drop what is unwholesome and keep it away, and to grow what is wholesome and keep it.

In brief

  • Right effort is the four supreme efforts: to abandon and prevent unwholesome states, to arouse and increase wholesome ones.
  • In Abhidhamma terms it is the mental factor energy (viriya): one factor doing four jobs.
  • With right mindfulness and right concentration it makes up the training of concentration (MN 44).
  • Energy is not straining: too much tips into restlessness, as too little tips into sloth.

What it means

SN 45.8 defines right effort by four efforts (sn45.8:8.1–8.6), which the Manual counts as the four supreme efforts: abandoning and preventing evil states, arousing and increasing good ones (CMA VII §25 and Guide). They are one factor, energy, doing four jobs.

Energy supports the other factors and keeps them from collapsing, like new timbers propping a sagging house (Guide to CMA II §3; commentarial). It serves wholesome and unwholesome states alike: in an unwholesome citta it is wrong effort (CMA VII §17). Among the thirty-seven requisites it occurs nine times, more than any other factor (CMA VII §33).

MN 117 sets right effort beside each of the first five factors: one knows the wrong as wrong, strives to drop it and take up the right, and stays mindful while one does (mn117:9.3, 15.3, 21.3, 27.3, 33.3). Its job is to support, not to strain; too much of it, unbalanced by concentration, tips into restlessness, as too little tips into sloth (Guide to CMA VII §§27–28).

Its training: Concentration · samādhi

Right effort, right mindfulness and right concentration make up the training of concentration (MN 44, mn44:11.4): how we train the mind.

In Abhidhamma terms

The mental factor energy (viriyaviriya · energy effort that upholds the other factors; also a faculty and a power), working as the four supreme efforts (Guide to CMA VII §30; VII §32 and Table 7.3). It arises in seventy-three cittas, missing only from sixteen rootless cittas too weak and passive for it: five-door adverting, the ten sense consciousnesses, receiving and investigating (CMA II §11 and Guide).

The mental factor’s own entry: energy (viriya) ›

Not to be confused with

Restlessness. Restlessness is agitation without direction. Energy is support, held in balance by concentration (Guide to CMA VII §§27–28).

A story

Soṇa's lute: The monk Soṇa walked up and down in meditation until his feet bled, and still his mind was not freed, so he thought of going home. The Buddha asked him about his lute: did it play when the strings were too tight, or too slack? Only when evenly tuned. So too with energy, he said.
