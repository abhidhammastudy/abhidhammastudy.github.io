The noble eightfold path › The second path factor

The second path factor · Wisdom

Right intention sammā-saṅkappa

Turning the mind toward letting go, goodwill and not harming.

In brief

  • Right intention is the intention of renunciation, of goodwill and of harmlessness (SN 45.8).
  • In Abhidhamma terms it is initial application (vitakka), the factor that directs the mind onto its object.
  • With right view it makes up the training of wisdom (MN 44).
  • In a path above the first jhāna level it is absent, and the path has seven factors.

What it means

SN 45.8 defines right intention by its three aims: renunciation, goodwill and harmlessness (sn45.8:4.1–4.3).

In the suttas the word vitakkavitakka · initial application lays the mind onto its object; a factor of the first jhāna only often just means thinking, but the Abhidhamma uses it in a narrow sense: the factor that mounts the citta and its companions on the object, like striking a bell (Guide to CMA II §3; the bell, Vism IV §71, commentarial). Directed toward renunciation, goodwill and harmlessness, it is right intention; in unwholesome cittas the same factor is wrong intention (CMA VII §§17, 30).

Initial application is missing from the second jhāna upward, because meditation has outgrown it. So a path at the level of the second jhāna or above has no right intention in it, and the eightfold path is, in that moment, sevenfold (CMA II §19; Guide to VII §§32–33). Intention does its work on the way there, not always at the moment of arrival. One who reaches the path by bare insight, without jhāna, has a path at the first-jhāna level, and so, the commentaries say, all eight factors (Guide to I §§30–31; Vism XXI §798).

Its training: Wisdom · paññā

Right view and right intention make up the training of wisdom (MN 44, mn44:11.5): how we see, and what we aim at.

In Abhidhamma terms

Initial application (vitakkavitakka · initial application lays the mind onto its object; a factor of the first jhāna only), here directed toward renunciation, goodwill and harmlessness (Guide to CMA VII §30). It arises in fifty-five cittas: every sense-sphere citta except the ten sense-consciousnesses, and the eleven cittas of the first jhāna (CMA II §11).

The mental factor’s own entry: initial application (vitakka) ›

Not to be confused with

Sustained application. Initial application is the lift onto the object, like a bird beating its wings to take off; sustained application is the continued work on it, like the bird gliding once it is aloft (Vism IV §71; Guide to CMA I §§18–20; commentarial).
