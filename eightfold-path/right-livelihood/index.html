The noble eightfold path › The fifth path factor

The fifth path factor · Virtue

Right livelihood sammā-ājīva

Holding back from earning a living in ways that harm or deceive.

In brief

  • Right livelihood is giving up wrong ways of earning a living (SN 45.8; MN 117).
  • In Abhidhamma terms it is the third abstinence (virati), a mental factor of its own.
  • If it were only right speech and right action done for money, the path would have seven factors, not eight.
  • As a mental factor it is a moment: the specific refusal, in an ordinary working day, to earn by harming.

What it means

SN 45.8 defines right livelihood as giving up wrong livelihood (sn45.8:7.1–7.3); MN 117 names deceit among the wrong ways (mn117:29.2). The Guide gives trade in poisons, intoxicants, weapons, slaves and animals for slaughter as examples (Guide to II §6).

Livelihood has work of its own. The AtthasālinīAtthasālinī · The Expositor Buddhaghosa's commentary on the Dhammasaṅgaṇī; source of the mango simile explains: killing or lying done for a living, as by a hunter or a bribed witness, is wrong livelihood, and abstaining from it is right livelihood; the same deeds done for sport are wrong action or wrong speech (CS §301, commentarial). The elder Mahāsīva drew the line elsewhere: for him wrong livelihood proper is getting one's requisites by trickery.

It follows the pattern of the abstinences. In ordinary life it arises only on occasions of actual restraint, and never together with the other two; in the path it is present with right speech and right action, all three together, and it ends the disposition to wrong livelihood for good (CMA II §§15, 17 and Guide).

Its training: Virtue · sīla

Right speech, right action and right livelihood make up the training of virtue (MN 44, mn44:11.3): what we hold back from.

In Abhidhamma terms

An abstinence (virativirati · abstinences right speech, right action, right livelihood; singly in mundane cittas, together in a path) (CMA II §6; Guide to VII §30). Among the thirty-seven requisites of enlightenment it occurs once, as the fifth factor of the eightfold path (CMA VII §33).

The mental factor’s own entry: right livelihood (sammā-ājīva) ›

Not to be confused with

Non-greed. Non-greed is present in every beautiful citta as freedom from clinging. Right livelihood is the specific abstinence from wrong earning, arising, before the path, only when there is an occasion for it.
