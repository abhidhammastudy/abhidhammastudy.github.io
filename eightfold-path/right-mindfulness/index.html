The seventh path factor · Concentration

Right mindfulness sammā-sati

Staying present with what is here, in body, feelings, mind and mental objects, without drifting off.

In brief

  • Right mindfulness is the four foundations of mindfulness: body, feelings, mind and mental objects (SN 45.8).
  • In Abhidhamma terms it is the mental factor mindfulness (sati), which keeps the mind from floating away from its object.
  • It is present in every beautiful citta, and never in an unwholesome one.
  • So the Abhidhamma counts no wrong mindfulness, though the suttas use the phrase.

What it means

SN 45.8 defines right mindfulness by the four foundations (sn45.8:9.1–9.6): the one factor, mindfulness, applied to body, feeling, mind and mental objects (CMA VII §§24, 31).

The word is related to remembering, yet as a factor it means being present: the mind not drifting from what is here. The commentaries contrast a pumpkin bobbing on water with a stone that stays where it is (CMA II §5, Guide; commentarial). In the balancing of the faculties, faith is balanced with wisdom and energy with concentration, while mindfulness is always needed, since it watches over the rest (Guide to CMA VII §§27–28).

The Manual lists wrong view, wrong intention, wrong effort and wrong concentration, but no wrong mindfulness, because mindfulness never occurs in an unwholesome citta (CMA VII §17 and Guide). The suttas do use the phrase (MN 117; MN 8); the commentaries take it as a name for the unwholesome states together, not for one factor (Papañcasūdanī on MN 8, CS §83; commentarial).

Its training: Concentration · samādhi

Right effort, right mindfulness and right concentration make up the training of concentration (MN 44, mn44:11.4): how we train the mind.

In Abhidhamma terms

The mental factor mindfulness (satisati · mindfulness not floating away from the object; a beautiful universal factor), as the four foundations (Guide to CMA VII §30; VII §32 and Table 7.3). It is a beautiful universal, present in every beautiful citta, from a moment of generosity to the supramundane path (CMA II §§5, 15).

Not to be confused with

Attention. Attention turns the mind toward anything, wisely or not, and is in every citta; mindfulness is present only in beautiful cittas. A sniper's steady aim is one-pointedness and energy; it is not mindfulness.

A story

Ānanda lies down: The First Council was to begin the next day, and Ānanda, who would recite the suttas, was not yet an arahant. He spent the night in mindfulness of the body. Near dawn he decided to lie down, and as he leaned back, before his head reached the pillow and after his feet had left the ground, his mind was freed.
