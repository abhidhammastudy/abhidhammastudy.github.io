The noble eightfold path › The first path factor

The first path factor · Wisdom

Right view sammā-diṭṭhi

Seeing things as they are, starting from the four truths: suffering, its cause, its ending and the way.

In brief

  • Right view is the first factor of the path, and it leads: right view comes first, says MN 117.
  • The suttas define it as knowledge of suffering, its origin, its cessation and the way to its cessation (SN 45.8).
  • In Abhidhamma terms it is the mental factor wisdom (paññā).
  • With right intention it makes up the training of wisdom (MN 44).

What it means

SN 45.8 defines right view as knowledge of suffering, its origin, its cessation and the way to its cessation (sn45.8:3.1–3.2). So the path begins by knowing the four truths, and it is itself the last of them.

MN 117 says why it comes first. Right view knows wrong view as wrong and right view as right, and so for intention, speech, action and livelihood (mn117:4.1–4.3, 10.3, 16.3, 22.3, 28.3). Beside each of those first five factors run right view, right effort and right mindfulness: one sees the wrong, strives to drop it, and stays mindful while one does (mn117:9.3, 15.3, 21.3, 27.3, 33.3). The same sutta calls right view twofold: one kind is tainted and ripens in further existence; the other is noble, taint-free and supramundane, a factor of the path (mn117:6.3–6.4).

Wisdom is not cleverness or learning. It is the factor that sees, and it can be present in a simple person's quiet moment of seeing a feeling pass. The commentary on MN 117 counts five kinds of right view: insight, the view that beings own their kamma, and the right views of the path, of the fruit and of reviewing (Papañcasūdanī, CS §142; commentarial).

Its training: Wisdom · paññā

Right view and right intention make up the training of wisdom (MN 44, mn44:11.5). The list starts from view, though the training starts from virtue: the Manual's seven purifications begin with purification of virtue (CMA IX §§22, 28, with the Guide to §22). The list follows what leads; the training follows what supports what.

In Abhidhamma terms

The mental factor wisdom (paññāpaññā · wisdom understanding things as they are; also called non-delusion and the faculty of wisdom), which the Abhidhamma also calls knowledge and non-delusion (Guide to CMA VII §30; Guide to II §8). It arises in forty-seven cittas: the twelve sense-sphere beautiful cittas associated with knowledge, and all thirty-five sublime and supramundane cittas (CMA II §15). So every path moment has it.

Not to be confused with

Wrong view. They are not two ends of one factor: wrong view is a different mental factor, and the two are never found together. Nor is right view cleverness: a quick mind can still hold a wrong view.

A story

Assaji's four lines: The wanderer Upatissa saw the monk Assaji on alms round and asked for his teacher's doctrine. Assaji gave it in one verse: “Of things that arise from a cause, the Tathāgata has told the cause, and also their cessation.” Upatissa, later Sāriputta, gained the eye of Dhamma on hearing it.
