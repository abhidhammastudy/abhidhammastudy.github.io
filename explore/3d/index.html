Level 1 · Foundations

The categories, mapped

The suttas describe experience with overlapping lists: five aggregates, twelve bases, eighteen elements, four noble truths. The Abhidhamma shows that they are four ways of cutting the same cloth. The top bar holds every kind of ultimate reality, sized by count; each row below cuts that same bar differently. Read straight down to see where any dhamma falls in every list. Choose any slice to read it.

drag to turn

The four ultimate realities as four pillars, cut four ways: into the aggregates, the bases, the elements and the noble truths. Choose a cut to see which reality each item is made of.

Five aggregates

Rūpa is all 28 kinds of matter. Feeling and perception are one mental factor each, and formations are the other 50. Consciousness is all 89 cittas. Nibbāna belongs to no aggregate.

Twelve bases

Ten bases are matter: five sense organs and five objects. The mind base is all 89 cittas. The mental-object base gathers the rest: the 52 factors, the 16 subtle kinds of matter, and Nibbāna.

Eighteen elements

As the bases, but the mind base splits into seven: five sense-consciousness elements (10 cittas), the mind element (3: five-door adverting and the two receiving cittas) and the mind-consciousness element (the other 76).

Four noble truths

Suffering is all the mind and matter of the three mundane planes, except craving. Its origin is craving, which the Abhidhamma identifies with the factor greed. Cessation is Nibbāna. The path is the eight path factors, mental factors that arise together in path consciousness (seven in paths of the second jhāna and above, which lack right intention). The path citta's other states and the four fruitions belong to no truth (CMA VII §40).

Gross and subtle matter: the five organs and seven objects (touch counting as earth, fire and air) are the 12 gross kinds of matter; the other 16 are subtle and are known only at the mind door.

Sources: Bodhi, CMA VII §§34–40 (compendium of the whole); Nārada, Manual VII

Level 2 · Elements

Elements in 3D

Three models of the elements of experience: all 89 cittas as a stupa, the 52 factors gathered round a single citta, and the inside of a kalāpa of matter.

drag to turn

Each cube is one citta. The tiers rise from the 54 sense-sphere cittas, through the 15 fine-material and 12 immaterial, to the 8 supramundane cittas around Nibbāna at the top. Coloured by feeling, most of the stupa is joy or equanimity; the four deep-indigo and light-blue cubes are the only cittas with bodily pain, bodily pleasure or displeasure.

Level 3 · Process

Process in 3D

Two views of the same seconds of life. Above, one cognitive process as a track of moments, with what each moment does to kamma and to the body. Below, the body itself as a clock: one turn of the pointer is the 51 sub-moments that a unit of matter lives, so each ring of matter stays full while every unit in it is replaced.

The bar above the track is the object's life: seventeen moments for a very great object. Indigo seeds rise from the javanas, where kamma is made. The small cubes below are mind-born matter, produced as each citta arises, except the sense consciousness, which is too weak to produce matter.

Level 4 · Relations

Relations in 3D

The wheel of twelve links opened into a helix that climbs through three lives, to step through link by link; then the 24 conditions, each shown running from the states that condition to the states conditioned.

drag to turn · point at a link

The links climb upward through time. Between them, arrows show which way each conditions the next; the spine inside the helix carries the four groups. At rebirth, the Visuddhimagga says, mentality supports the mind base, the sixth of the six bases, in at least seven ways: conascence, mutuality, support, association, result, presence and non-disappearance.

The 24 conditions, by direction

drag to turn · point at a strand, bead or shape

On the left, what gives the help; on the right, what receives it. Each bead is one of the 24 conditions, numbered as in the Paṭṭhāna. A strand runs from each kind of state that can be the condition and ends, with an arrowhead, at each kind it conditions, so the strands show the ways each condition actually works. Within “mind & matter → both”, not every condition runs every way: dissociation, for instance, only crosses between mind and matter. Point at any strand for what it means there, or choose a direction to list its conditions.

Sources: Bodhi, CMA VIII; Visuddhimagga XVII

Level 5 · Lives and worlds

Thirty-one planes as a world-mountain

The traditional cosmos, stacked. The woeful states lie below the human world. Mount Sineru rises from it: the Four Great Kings dwell on its slopes and the Thirty-three on its summit. The higher heavens and the Brahmā worlds rest above it in space, and the four immaterial planes, drawn as empty rings, have no matter at all.

drag to turn

Floors of mind

Each floor of the Brahmā worlds corresponds to a jhāna; beings are reborn there by the kamma of that jhāna. The higher the floor, the longer the life and the subtler the mind.

Mind without matter

In the four immaterial planes there is no body and no base: cittas arise with no physical support. Among the non-percipient beings, the reverse: a body with no mind at all.

Ring widths

Disc widths are schematic, not measured. For the actual lifespans, from nine million human years to 84,000 aeons, see the reading Thirty-one planes.

Sources: Bodhi, CMA V; commentaries on Sineru

Level 6 · Practice

Practice as a climb

Calm rises through stacked floors of absorption, the first five each with fewer factors than the one below; the immaterial floors keep the fifth jhāna's two. Insight rises beside them: it can start from any floor, or from none, and only insight passes the top of calm into the supramundane, where four paths, each taking Nibbāna as object, cut the ten fetters.

drag to turn · choose a stage

The 37 requisites are 14 states

Reading the climb

A factor's post ends on the last floor that has it: initial and sustained application go first, then zest, then happiness, which gives way to equanimity; one-pointedness runs to the top. The immaterial floors keep the fifth jhāna's two factors and change only the object; drawn as empty frames, they are still planes of existence (D5). Dashed links: insight may start from any floor; dry insight starts from the ground with momentary concentration.

The table: each dot is one place a state appears among the 37; energy appears nine times, mindfulness eight, wisdom five, concentration four, faith twice, the other nine once. Thirteen are mental factors; one, as a base of success, is citta itself.

Sources: Bodhi, CMA I (jhāna factors), VII (requisites), IX (calm, insight, paths); Nārada, Manual VII

Level 7 · Synthesis

One moment, every lens

The mango moment (seeing a ripe mango and wanting it) as five transparent layers on one time axis of seventeen mind-moments: matter, citta, its factors, the conditions that link them, and kamma. Everything the site teaches happens at once; the layers show it lined up. Choose a moment to cut through all five.

What lines up

Wire-outline blocks appear in the factors only at the javanas, and the same seven moments are the only ones that send kamma forward; every other moment of the process is a result arriving or a functional step. The object's matter lasts exactly as long as the process, seventeen moments, and every citta but seeing adds mind-born matter.

How to use it

Cut at each moment and say all five lenses aloud before reading the caption. The readings follow ideas one at a time; this model takes the opposite cut: one moment, every idea at once.

Sources: Bodhi, CMA II (factors), III–IV (process), V (kamma), VI (matter), VIII (conditions)
