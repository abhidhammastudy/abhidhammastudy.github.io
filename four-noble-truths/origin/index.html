The second noble truth

The origin of suffering samudaya

Its cause is craving: the thirst for pleasant things, for being, and for not being, which keeps life after life going.

In brief

  • The second truth names the cause of suffering: craving (taṇhā).
  • Craving is of three kinds: for sense pleasures, for existence and for non-existence (SN 56.11).
  • Its task is to be abandoned: of the four truths it is the one to be abandoned.
  • In the Abhidhamma craving is one mental factor, greed, in its three aspects; on the Manual's count it alone is the origin.

What it means

In the sermon the origin is craving, which brings renewed existence: craving for sense pleasures, for existence and for non-existence (SN 56.11, sn56.11:4.4–4.5).

Why craving, of all the causes of suffering? The Vibhāvinī answers that craving is the chief cause: it makes kamma varied and keeps kamma company (CS §52). Ledi Sayadaw takes up the harder objection, that ignorance is the root of the whole round. In dependent origination, he says, ignorance stands first in the chain of conditions; in the teaching of the truths craving is the owner and chief that produces the aggregates, as a park made by a gardener from ground and seed is called the gardener's (Paramatthadīpanī, CS §165). Both are later commentary on the Saṅgahasaṅgaha · combination reading down: which factors each citta contains (also, elsewhere, “compendium”), not canon.

The suttas themselves tie this truth to dependent origination: AN 3.61 gives the origin of suffering as that chain in its forward order, and its cessation as the same chain ceasing, link by link (an3.61:13.6–17.4).

Its task: to be abandoned · pahātabba

Craving is the one truth to be abandoned (SN 56.11, sn56.11:6.2). The suttas say where: wherever something is pleasing, in the six-door process itself, in the eye and the sight, the seeing, the contact, the feeling and the rest, sixty places in all (DN 22, dn22:19.4–19.67). It is not abandoned all at once. Each of the four paths abandons only its share of the defilements: the first cuts wrong view and doubt, and craving is gone entirely only at the fourth (CMA IX §§38–41 with the Guides).

In Abhidhamma terms

One mental factor: greed (lobhalobha · greed grasping at an object; roots eight cittas; as craving, a root of the wheel), as craving in its three aspects (Guide to CMA VII §38). It is the one exception the first truth leaves out: everything else of the three mundane planes is suffering.

Not to be confused with

The widened origin of the Vibhaṅga's Abhidhamma method. There the word "noble" is dropped and the origin grows step by step: craving; then all the defilements; then all unwholesome states; then the wholesome roots that still carry taints; then all tainted wholesome states (Vibh 4). The Manual's count matches the sutta method, with craving alone as the origin.

A story

Mālukyaputta's teaching in brief: In old age Mālukyaputta asked for a teaching in brief, to take into solitude. The Buddha asked: could desire arise for sights you have never seen and never will? No. Then let the seen be only the seen. Mālukyaputta answered in verses: lust grows not from seeing a form, but from attending to its pleasing sign with mindfulness lost.
