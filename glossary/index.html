Glossary

309 terms, in Pāli alphabetical order without diacritics. Search finds the English as well as the Pāli.

1ABCDEGHIJKLMNOPRSTUVY

1

121 · the cittas counted in detail
the 89, with each path and fruition counted at five jhāna levels.

A

abhidhamma
the “higher teaching”: experience analysed into ultimate realities, dhammas.
Abhidhamma Piṭaka
the third basket of the Pāli Canon: seven books of analysis.
Abhidhammattha Saṅgaha
Anuruddha's manual of the whole Abhidhamma in nine chapters; Bodhi's CMA translates it.
abhiññā · direct knowledge
a mundane power worked from the fifth jhāna, such as the divine eye or recalling past lives; its citta runs for one moment.
abyākata · indeterminate
neither wholesome nor unwholesome: resultants, functionals, matter and Nibbāna.
accuta · not passing away
not passing away, often rendered deathless; a name of Nibbāna (VI §32).
āciṇṇa · habitual kamma
what one does again and again; next in line after death-proximate kamma.
adhimokkha · decision
resolves on the object; absent from doubt and from sense consciousness. The entry
adhipati · predominance
the one factor leading a citta: desire, energy, citta or investigation; or, as object predominance, an object held in high regard. The entry
āgantuka-bhavaṅga · adventitious life-continuum
an equanimous investigating citta that bridges displeasure in the javanas and a joyful life-continuum.
āhāra · nutriment (four)
edible food, contact, volition and consciousness, each sustaining its results. The entry
ahetuka · rootless
a citta with none of the six roots; there are 18.
ahirika · anottappa
shamelessness and fearlessness of wrongdoing: no self-respect, no dread of evil's results. The entry
ahosi · defunct kamma
kamma whose chance to ripen has passed.
ākāra · mode
one of twenty factors in the three-life reading: five for each group.
akusala · unwholesome
unhealthy, blameworthy, bringing painful results; rooted in greed, hatred or delusion.
ālaya-vijñāna · storehouse consciousness
Yogācāra's eighth consciousness, said to hold the seeds of past action.
alobha · adosa · amoha
non-greed, non-hatred, non-delusion: the three wholesome roots; non-delusion is wisdom. The entry
amata · the deathless
the deathless; a name of Nibbāna in the suttas (SN 43.25).
anāgāmī · non-returner
has cut sensual desire and ill will; reborn only in higher planes.
ānantariya · heinous deeds
killing mother, father or an arahant, wounding a Buddha, splitting the Saṅgha.
anārammaṇa · without object
without object; said of Nibbāna in Ud 8.1, which the Udāna commentary reads as: unlike a mind, it takes no object (cf. Kv XIX.6).
animitta · signless
without sign; an aspect of Nibbāna (VI §31), and the emancipation reached through the contemplation of impermanence.
anipphanna · non-concrete matter
the 10 qualities and modes of concrete matter, such as space and decay.
aniṭṭha · undesirable
of an object, undesirable; met through unwholesome kamma.
anuloma · conformity
the javana that fits the mind to what follows, just before change-of-lineage.
anupādisesa · without residue
the Nibbāna element without residue: named by the absence of aggregates after an arahant's last citta (It 44; VI §31 with the Guide; Vism XVI §561).
anusaya · latent tendencies
seven (six factors) that lie latent, unabandoned and gone to strength, ready to arise when conditions meet; only the paths remove them.
apāya · woeful planes
hell, animals, ghosts and titans: the four unhappy destinations.
āpo · water element
cohesion, which holds matter together; known by mind, not by touch. The entry
appamaññā · illimitables (factors)
compassion and appreciative joy as mental factors, taking suffering or happy beings as object.
appamaññā · illimitables (meditation)
loving-kindness, compassion, appreciative joy and equanimity, toward all beings: the divine abidings.
appanā · absorption
the jhāna or path javana itself, after access; the third stage of concentration.
appaṇihita · desireless
without hankering; an aspect of Nibbāna (VI §31), and the emancipation reached through the contemplation of suffering.
arahant
one who has cut all ten fetters; makes no new kamma and ends rebirth.
ārammaṇa · object
whatever a citta knows: a sight, a sound, a thought, a concept, Nibbāna. The entry
ārammaṇa-purejāta · object prenascence
a present object of matter, arisen earlier, helping the cittas that take it.
ārammaṇādhipati · object predominance
an object held weighty, which dominates the mind that takes it.
ariya · noble one
anyone who has reached a path or fruition, from stream-entry up.
ariya aṭṭhaṅgika magga · noble eightfold path
right view, intention, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness and concentration; in the strict sense, eight mental factors arising together in a supramundane path citta. The concept
ariya-sacca · noble truth
suffering, its origin, its cessation, the path: the four noble truths. The concept
arūpāvacara · immaterial sphere
the 12 cittas of formless jhāna and the 4 formless planes.
asaṅkhārika · unprompted
arising spontaneously, without urging by oneself or another.
asaṅkhata-dhātu · unconditioned element
Nibbāna, the one element not made by conditions.
asaṅkheyya · incalculable (the Bodhisatta's career)
in the Bodhisatta's story, a countless number of great aeons.
asaṅkheyya-kappa · incalculable aeon (Plate 11)
a quarter of a great aeon, one phase of a world-cycle.
āsanna · death-proximate kamma
done or recalled just before death; it ripens next if no weighty kamma does.
asaññasatta · non-percipient beings
a fine-material plane of matter with no mind, for 500 great aeons.
āsava · ogha · yoga
taints, floods, bonds: sense desire, desire for existence, views, ignorance.
asura · titan
a being of the woeful plane of the asuras, or, in another sense, of the warring titans.
atīta-bhavaṅga · past life-continuum
the life-continuum moment during which the object arises, before it registers at the door; counted first of the 17.
attha-paññatti · meaning-concept
a concept as what is made known: the meaning a name conveys, such as the idea of a chariot.
aṭṭhaka · navaka · dasaka
kalāpa sizes: octad eight kinds, nonad nine, decad ten, undecad eleven.
Atthasālinī · The Expositor
Buddhaghosa's commentary on the Dhammasaṅgaṇī; source of the mango simile.
avijjā · ignorance
not knowing the four noble truths; delusion as a root of the wheel.
avinibbhoga · inseparables
the eight kinds in every kalāpa: four elements, colour, smell, taste, nutritive essence.
āyatana · base (the twelve)
one of twelve bases: six internal (five sense organs and the mind base) and their six objects.
ayoniso manasikāra · unwise attention
attention that turns the mind the wrong way, toward greed, hatred or confusion; the opposite of wise attention.

B

bala · power
a state unshaken by its opposite; nine, among them faith, energy and wisdom.
bhaṅga-ñāṇa · dissolution
insight knowledge that attends only to the ending of formations.
bhava · kamma-becoming
link 10, active side: volition and the deeds that lead to rebirth.
bhava · rebirth-becoming
link 10, passive side: the new life's resultant mind and kamma-born matter.
bhavaṅga · life-continuum
the resultant citta, of the same kind as rebirth-linking, that runs between processes. The concept
bhavaṅga-calana · vibration of the life-continuum
the life-continuum moment that is shaken when an object comes into range; the next is the arrest.
bhavaṅgupaccheda · arrest of the life-continuum
the last life-continuum moment before a process; after it the stream stops and adverting arises.
bhūmi · plane
one of 31 planes of existence, each matched to a kind of mind.
bodhipakkhiyā dhammā · 37 requisites
the suttas' 37 aids to awakening, which reduce to 14 states.
Bodhisatta
a being bound for Buddhahood; the Buddha in his past lives and before awakening.
bojjhaṅga · enlightenment factor
seven: mindfulness, investigation of states, energy, zest, tranquillity, concentration, equanimity.
Brahmā
a being of the fine-material planes, reborn there through jhāna.

C

carita · temperament
a person's dominant tendency, such as lustful or faithful, guiding the choice of subject.
cetanā · volition
the willing that organises the other factors; as kamma, it bears results. The entry
cetasika · mental factor
a mental factor: arises and ceases with a citta, sharing its object and base.
chanda · desire-to-do
the wish to act or attain; not greed, and can be wholesome. The entry
citta · consciousness
one moment of knowing an object; 89 kinds, or 121 counted in detail.
cittakkhaṇa · mind-moment
the life of one citta, in three sub-moments: arising, presence, dissolution.
cittaviprayukta · dissociated formations
a northern category of things neither mind nor matter, such as life force.
cuti · death consciousness
the last citta of a life, of the same kind as its life-continuum.

D

deva
a being of the six sense-sphere heavens, above the human world.
dhamma
an ultimate reality, bearing its own nature; not a mere name or concept.
Dhammasaṅgaṇī
the first Abhidhamma book: lists every kind of citta and its factors.
dhātu · element (the eighteen)
one of eighteen elements: the twelve bases, with the mind base split into seven.
diṭṭhi · wrong view
a mistaken belief, such as a lasting self; joins four greed-rooted cittas. The entry
domanassa · displeasure
painful mental feeling; only in the two hate-rooted cittas.
dosa · hatred
aversion, from irritation to rage; root of two cittas, always with displeasure. The entry
duhetuka · two-rooted
a being reborn by a great resultant citta without knowledge; jhāna and the path are closed to them in that life.
dukkha · bodily pain
painful bodily feeling: only in unwholesome-resultant body-consciousness.
dukkha · suffering
the unsatisfactoriness of all that is conditioned; first noble truth and second mark; three kinds: pain, change, conditioned existence.
dvāra · door
the channel by which a process meets its object: five senses and mind.
dvāravimutta · door-freed
rebirth-linking, life-continuum and death: cittas that arise without any door.

E

ekaggatā · one-pointedness
unifies the mind on its object; when strong it is concentration (samādhi). The entry

G

gantha · tie, knot
four ties of the mental and physical body: covetousness, ill will, rites and rituals, the dogma 'this alone is true'.
garuka · weighty kamma
jhāna kept to death, a heinous deed or fixed wrong view; ripens first.
gati-nimitta · destiny sign
at death, an image of the coming birth.
gotrabhū · change-of-lineage
the javana that leaves the sense-sphere lineage for jhāna, or turns to Nibbāna.

H

hadaya-vatthu · heart-base
in beings with bodies, the matter supporting every citta except sense consciousness.
hasituppāda · smile-producing
an arahant's rootless functional citta that causes smiling at sense-sphere things.
hetu · root
one of six factors that steady a citta: greed, hatred, delusion, or their opposites. The entry
hiri · ottappa
moral shame and moral dread: recoiling from evil from self-respect, and fear of results. The entry

I

iddhipāda · means to accomplishment
desire, energy, consciousness or investigation, when it leads the mind toward the goal of the teaching.
indriya · faculty
a dhamma that governs its own domain; 22 faculties, and condition 16. The entry
issā · macchariya · kukkucca
envy, avarice and worry: three factors found only in hate-rooted cittas. The entry
iṭṭha · desirable
of an object, desirable (moderately, or extremely, ati-iṭṭha); met through wholesome kamma.

J

janaka … upaghātaka · kamma by function
productive, supportive, obstructive or destructive: what a kamma does.
Jātaka
a birth story of the Bodhisatta, ending by naming who was who.
jāti · birth
the arising of the aggregates in a new existence; the eleventh link, and the characteristic of matter called production.
javana · impulsion
the active phase of a process, usually seven alike cittas; here kamma is made.
jhāna · absorption
deep concentration fixed on one object: five fine-material levels, then four immaterial. The entry
jhānaṅga · jhāna factors
initial and sustained application, zest, one-pointedness and feeling: seven in CMA VII, five distinct states.
jīvita · life faculty (material)
sustains kamma-born matter in its kalāpa while it lasts. The entry
jīvitindriya · life faculty (mental)
sustains the mental factors it arises with, for as long as they last. The entry

K

kalāpa
the smallest group of matter: eight or more kinds arising and ceasing together.
kālavimutta · free of time
free of time: said of Nibbāna, which never arises or passes, and of concepts, which have no nature of their own (III §17 with the Guide).
kāmacchanda · sensual desire
the first hindrance: greed for sense objects; suppressed by one-pointedness.
kāmāvacara · sense-sphere
the 54 cittas, and 11 planes, where sense desire holds sway.
kamma
wholesome or unwholesome volition, which later ripens as results. The concept · The entry
kamma-bhava · kamma-process becoming
becoming as the active side: the wholesome and unwholesome volitions that make for a new existence.
kamma-nimitta · kamma sign
at death, an object linked with a past deed, such as offered flowers.
kammapatha · course of action
one of ten typical deeds of body, speech or mind, unwholesome or wholesome; complete, it can produce a rebirth.
kammaṭṭhāna · meditation subject
one of forty objects for calm; literally “place of work”.
kasiṇa
a device for concentration, such as a disc of earth or a colour.
kaṭattā · reserve kamma
any act done once, which ripens if nothing stronger is there.
kāya · body (two senses)
the physical body; or, in factor names like kāya-passaddhi, the mental factors. The entry
khandha · aggregate
one of the five heaps into which the suttas sort all experience.
khaṇika-samādhi · momentary concentration
concentration that holds each passing event in turn; the term is in the Visuddhimagga; the Guide reckons it as purification of mind for the pure-insight meditator.
khīṇāsava · one whose taints are destroyed
the arahant, in whom the four taints have ended.
kicca · function (of a citta)
one of fourteen jobs a citta can do, such as seeing or javana.
kilesa · defilement
one of ten states that torment the mind, from greed to fearlessness of wrongdoing.
kiriya · functional
a citta that neither makes kamma nor results from it, such as adverting.
kusala · wholesome
healthy, blameless, bringing pleasant results; rooted in non-greed and non-hatred.

L

lakkhaṇa · characteristic
a dhamma's own nature: the first part of its identity card.
lakkhaṇa-rūpa · characteristics of matter
production, continuity, decay and impermanence: marks of matter's arising and passing.
lobha · greed
grasping at an object; roots eight cittas; as craving, a root of the wheel. The entry
lokiya · mundane
within the round of rebirth: all cittas but the eight supramundane, and all matter.
lokuttara · supramundane
beyond the world: the four paths, four fruitions and Nibbāna.

M

magga · path (citta)
a supramundane citta that arises once, cutting defilements, with Nibbāna as object. The entry
maggaṅga · path factors
twelve: eight right factors and four wrong ones; the eight right ones in path consciousness are the truth of the path.
mahābhūta · great elements
earth, water, fire and air: the primary matter all other matter depends on.
mahākappa · great aeon
one cycle of a world's arising and passing; four incalculable aeons.
mahākiriya · great functional
the eight beautiful sense-sphere functional cittas: the arahant's counterparts of the great wholesome, which make no kamma.
mahākusala · great wholesome
the eight beautiful sense-sphere wholesome cittas, numbers 31–38 on Plate 3.
māna · conceit
measuring oneself as better, equal or worse; may join greed without wrong view. The entry
manasikāra · attention
turns the citta and its factors toward the object; present in every citta. The entry
mano · mind (base and door)
as a base, all 89 cittas; as the mind door, the life-continuum.
mano-dhātu · mind element
the three cittas of five-door adverting and receiving.
manodvāra-vīthi · mind-door process
a process at the mind door alone: adverting, seven javanas, and two registrations when the object is clear and the conditions for registration hold.
manodvārāvajjana · mind-door adverting
citta 29: opens mind-door processes, and does determining in five-door ones.
manoviññāṇa-dhātu · mind-consciousness element
the 76 cittas that are neither sense consciousness nor mind element.
mātikā · matrix
the Dhammasaṅgaṇī's opening lists, 22 triads and 100 dyads, that sort every dhamma.
mettā · loving-kindness
the wish that beings be well; in the Abhidhamma, the mental factor non-hatred taking beings as object.
moha · delusion
mental blindness to an object's true nature; present in every unwholesome citta. The entry
momūha · sheer delusion
delusion with neither greed nor hatred: the two delusion-rooted cittas, one with doubt and one with restlessness.
muñcitukamyatā-ñāṇa · desire for deliverance
insight knowledge that wants release from the whole field of formations.

N

nāma · mind
mind: citta with its factors, which “bend” toward objects; the word also means “name”.
nāma-paññatti · name-concept
a concept as what makes known: the name or designation itself, the word “chariot”.
nāmarūpa · mentality-materiality (link 4)
mind and matter. As link 4 of the wheel: the resultant mental factors and kamma-born matter of a new life; in the suttas, feeling, perception, volition, contact and attention with the elements; in the Abhidhamma, citta with its factors, and the 28 kinds of matter.
nāmarūpa-pariccheda · defining mind and matter
discerning mind and matter as they are, without a self; the purification of view, first of the insight knowledges.
ñāṇa · knowledge
knowledge, understanding; in the Abhidhamma, the factor wisdom (paññā).
nānākkhaṇika-kamma · asynchronous kamma
a past volition helping its results at a later time; kamma condition across a gap.
ñāṇasampayutta · with knowledge
accompanied by wisdom that understands the object as it is.
nibbāna
the unconditioned: the ending of craving; object of path and fruition. The concept
nibbidā · disenchantment
turning away from formations, seen as fearful and dangerous; an insight knowledge.
nikanti · attachment
the subtle liking for insight's bright experiences; the tenth imperfection.
nimitta · meditation sign
the preliminary, learning or counterpart image of the meditation object.
nippapañca · non-proliferation
freedom from proliferation, the spreading out of craving, conceit and views (the commentaries' gloss); a manifestation of Nibbāna (Vism XVI §556) and a sutta name of it (SN 43.23).
nipphanna · concrete matter
the 18 kinds with a nature of their own, fit objects for insight.
nirodha · cessation (third truth)
the cessation of suffering, the third noble truth, which is Nibbāna, to be realised; not the attainment of cessation.
nirodha-samāpatti · attainment of cessation
the temporary stopping of citta and mental factors, open only to non-returners and arahants with all eight jhānas.
nissaya · support (condition 8)
a foundation, as the earth is for trees; arising together or before. The entry
nissaya · word-by-word text
a study text giving each Pāli phrase, then its meaning; a Burmese teaching form. The entry
nīvaraṇa · hindrances
obstacles to jhāna and to wisdom: sensual desire, ill will, sloth and torpor, restlessness and worry, doubt; the Abhidhamma adds ignorance.
niyāma · order, law
a fixed order of nature; the commentaries count five, among them the order of mind, which the cognitive process follows.
niyata · aniyata · fixed and unfixed
fixed factors always arise in their cittas; the eleven unfixed only sometimes.

O

ogha · flood
the four taints seen as floods that drag beings into the sea of becoming: sensual desire, existence, views, ignorance.
ojā · nutritive essence
the essence in food, and in every kalāpa, that sustains matter. The entry

P

paccavekkhaṇa · reviewing
after attainment, knowledge looking back on path, fruition and Nibbāna, and usually, not always, on the defilements abandoned and those that remain.
paccaya · condition
a dhamma that helps another to arise or persist, in a specific way.
paccayuppanna · conditioned state
the dhamma that a condition helps to arise or persist.
pacchājāta · postnascence
support from later cittas to a body that arose before them. The entry
paccupaṭṭhāna · manifestation
how a dhamma shows itself in experience: the third part of its card.
padaṭṭhāna · proximate cause
what a dhamma most directly depends on: the fourth part of its card.
pakatūpanissaya · natural decisive support
strong help from what came before: earlier lust or faith, pleasure or pain, a person, food, climate, lodging, past kamma.
pakiṇṇaka · occasionals
six ethically variable factors that join some cittas but not all.
pañcadvārāvajjana · five-door adverting
citta 28: turns toward an object arriving at one of the five sense doors.
paññā · wisdom
understanding things as they are; also called non-delusion and the faculty of wisdom.
paññatti · concept
a concept: a name, or what a name refers to, such as “chariot”.
paramattha · ultimate
what remains after analysis: citta, cetasika, matter and Nibbāna.
parikamma · preliminary
the first stage of concentration; also the first javana leading into absorption.
pariññā · full understanding
the task of the first truth, which is to be fully understood, not abandoned.
pariyuṭṭhāna · obsession
a defilement risen into the mind and taking it over, but not yet in deed; the middle of the commentaries' three levels.
pasāda · sensitivity
the matter of eye, ear, nose, tongue or body that receives sense impacts.
passaddhi … ujjukatā · the six pairs
tranquillity, lightness, malleability, wieldiness, proficiency, rectitude: of the factors and of the mind.
pathavī · earth element
hardness or softness; the foundation for other matter. The entry
paṭicca-samuppāda · dependent origination
the twelve links by which suffering arises, and by whose ending it ceases.
paṭigha · aversion
“striking against”: the Manual's name for hatred in the two hate-rooted cittas, from rage to slight irritation; it always comes with displeasure.
paṭisandhi · rebirth-linking
the first citta of a life: a resultant that links it to the last.
paṭisaṅkhā-ñāṇa · reflection
insight knowledge that re-examines formations by the three marks, as the means of release.
Paṭṭhāna
the seventh Abhidhamma book: relates all dhammas by 24 conditions. The concept
peta · ghost
a being of one of the woeful planes, tormented by hunger and thirst.
phala · fruition
the supramundane resultant citta that follows its path, also taking Nibbāna as object.
phalasamāpatti · attainment of fruition
a noble one's absorption in their own fruition, with Nibbāna as object.
phassa · contact
the meeting of object, base and consciousness; the first universal factor. The entry
pīti · zest
delight in the object; a jhāna factor up to the third jhāna. The entry
pubbabhāga-magga · preliminary path
a commentarial name for the path factors as developed in ordinary moments, before the supramundane path.
pubbaṅgama · forerunner
what goes before: the mind as forerunner of states, as in the first verse of the Dhammapada.
purejāta · prenascence
support from matter that arose earlier and still exists, such as the eye. The entry
puthujjana · worldling
an ordinary person who has reached no path.

R

rasa · function (of a dhamma)
what a dhamma does: the second part of its identity card. The entry
rūpa · matter
matter: the third reality, 28 kinds; as an aggregate it is called form.
rūpa · visible form
visible form: colour, the object of seeing; one of the 28 kinds of matter.
rūpāvacara · fine-material sphere
the 15 cittas of form-jhāna and the 16 planes they lead to.

S

sa-upādisesa · with residue
the Nibbāna element with residue: the defilements ended while an arahant's aggregates remain (It 44; VI §31).
sabbacittasādhāraṇa · universals
the seven factors present in every citta, from contact to attention.
sabhāva · own nature
the nature a dhamma bears by itself, which makes it an ultimate reality rather than a concept.
sacca · truth
truth, and what is real: the four noble truths; in another sense also the two truths, conventional and ultimate.
saddhā · faith
confidence that clarifies the mind, like a gem clearing muddy water. The entry
sahajāta · conascence
support between things arising at the same moment, like a lamp and its light. The entry
sakadāgāmī · once-returner
has weakened sensual desire and ill will; returns to this world once.
saḷāyatana · six bases (link 5)
link 5: the five sense organs and the mind, as results of kamma.
samādhi · concentration
the mind collected on one object; in the Abhidhamma, the factor one-pointedness developed.
samatha · calm
meditation that develops concentration, up to the jhānas. The concept
sammā-diṭṭhi · right view
the first path factor, the wisdom factor knowing the four noble truths.
sammā-saṅkappa · right intention
the second path factor, initial application directed to renunciation, goodwill and harmlessness.
sammappadhāna · supreme effort
energy in four tasks: discard arisen evil, prevent unarisen evil, develop unarisen good, increase arisen good; together, right effort.
sammasana-ñāṇa · comprehension
the first insight knowledge, assigning the three marks to formations gathered in groups.
sammuti · convention
conventional truth: speech about persons and things; true, but not ultimate.
sampaṭicchana · receiving
the citta that takes up the object just after sense consciousness.
sampayoga · association
reading across: which cittas each mental factor joins.
samuccheda-pahāna · abandoning by cutting off
the path's way of abandoning a defilement: for good, never to arise again.
samudaya · origin
the origin of suffering, the second noble truth: craving, for sense pleasures, for existence and for non-existence.
samuṭṭhāna · origin of matter
what produces matter: kamma, citta, temperature or nutriment.
saṃyojana · fetter
one of ten defilements that bind to rebirth; the four paths cut them.
sandhi · connection
one of three joints between the groups of causes and effects.
saṅgaha · combination
reading down: which factors each citta contains (also, elsewhere, “compendium”).
saṅkhāra · all conditioned things
widest sense: everything conditioned, as in “all formations are impermanent”.
saṅkhāra · formations (aggregate)
formations, the fourth aggregate: the fifty factors besides feeling and perception.
saṅkhāra · formations (link 2)
kamma-formations, link 2: wholesome and unwholesome volition in the past life.
saṅkhārupekkhā · equanimity about formations
insight knowledge that looks on all formations evenly, with neither fear nor delight.
