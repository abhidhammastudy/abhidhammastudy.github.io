𑀧𑀸𑀞pāṭha · a text

Readings

Essays written to be read slowly, in the order of the Manual. Each ends with a question to take away.

New here? Where to begin suggests a short first path and maps the whole journey.

What there is

The realities the analysis ends in.

  1. 1. The librarySeven books that take experience apart and put it back together, and the long shelf of commentary that grew around them. · about 7 minutes
  2. 2. A chariot of partsWhere the Abhidhamma begins, with a nun's answer to Māra, and how a person is taken apart without being called a lie. · about 7 minutes
  3. 3. The All, counted twiceTwelve bases and eighteen elements: the Buddha's "all" as six doors and what comes through them, and why the same ground was counted three ways. · about 6 minutes
  4. 4. A map of the eighty-nineEvery kind of consciousness the Abhidhamma knows, sorted by plane, by root and by feeling, and what the sorting reveals. · about 8 minutes
  5. 5. The twelve unwholesome cittasEight rooted in greed, two in hatred, two in delusion alone: what each label means, the scenes the old commentaries chose to show each one, and where the teachers disagreed about prompting. · about 11 minutes
  6. 6. Rootless and beautiful cittasThe eighteen cittas with no root at all, the arahant's smile among them; the twenty-four beautiful cittas of the sense sphere and who has them; and, briefly, the jhāna, formless and supramundane cittas, with how eight became forty. · about 13 minutes
  7. 7. An ensemble of fifty-twoSeven factors present in every moment of mind, two families that never meet, and why the Abhidhamma thinks of a mind as a chord. · about 8 minutes
  8. 8. Which factors meet which cittasOne table read two ways: across, the cittas a factor joins; down, the factors a citta takes. How the Abhidhamma assembles a moment of mind. · about 5 minutes
  9. 9. What each citta doesFrom what a citta is to what it does: fourteen functions, six doors, six bases, and the few cittas that do the most work. · about 6 minutes
  10. 10. Feeling, roots and objectsThree more ways to sort the eighty-nine cittas: by the feeling each one carries, by the roots that make it firm, and by what it is able to know. · about 10 minutes
  11. 11. How matter is madeFour great elements, twenty-four kinds of matter derived from them, four ways matter is produced, and the smallest groups in which it ever occurs. · about 8 minutes
  12. 12. Mind and matter togetherThe nāma–rūpa model. Two currents that hold each other up, moment by moment: matter gives mind its bases and objects, mind makes and sustains matter, and at conception the two arise together. · about 14 minutes
  13. 13. Names and the namedIf a chariot is only a name, what is a name? The Manual's last word on what there is: concepts, the things they make known, and the six ways of naming, from "feeling" to "king's son". · about 8 minutes
  14. 14. Nibbāna, the unconditionedThe fourth ultimate reality, the one the analysis does not take apart: what the suttas and the Manual say it is, why the commentaries deny that it is mere absence, and which few moments of mind ever take it as their object. · about 12 minutes
  15. 15. The commentators' picture-bookA heap of coins, a struck bell, a bird gliding: how the old teachers made a list of dhammas memorable, and what each picture is pointing at. · about 7 minutes

How it unfolds

Moments, processes, conditions and lives.

  1. 16. The mango and the momentA man asleep under a mango tree, a fruit that falls, and the seventeen moments in which seeing happens and kamma is made. · about 9 minutes
  2. 17. When the object is weakNot every sight or sound gets the full seventeen moments. How late an object comes into range decides where the process stops: after registration, after javana, at determining, or before it has begun. · about 6 minutes
  3. 18. Other processesThe mind door, the last process of a life, absorption, the path, fruition and cessation: how the building blocks of the mango moment make every other kind of process. · about 13 minutes
  4. 19. Who has which cittasThe object decides what is seen; the person decides what is done with it. Which of the eighty-nine cittas can arise depends on how a being was reborn, what they have attained, and the world they live in. · about 9 minutes
  5. 20. The web of twenty-four conditionsThe Abhidhamma's last book relates every dhamma to every other. How one thing helps another to arise, and why the help is never a cause acting alone. · about 8 minutes
  6. 21. One moment, twenty-four questionsTwo moments of the mango process, seeing the fruit and wanting it, put to each of the twenty-four conditions in turn: which hold, what gives them, and why the others do not. · about 14 minutes
  7. 22. Taints, floods, bonds, tiesNine lists of what binds the mind, and why the tradition kept all their names: each name has its own reach, a latent tendency is a defilement not yet abandoned, and the paths do not end every name at the same time. · about 12 minutes
  8. 23. The lists reduced to their factorsTaints, floods, bonds, clingings, hindrances, fetters: chapter VII's many names for what binds, and the fourteen factors they all come down to. · about 6 minutes
  9. 24. The wheel across three livesTwelve links, three periods, twenty modes and three rounds: how the Abhidhamma reads dependent origination, and where the wheel can be stopped. · about 8 minutes
  10. 25. Kamma, death and rebirthFour ways to sort kamma, the signs that appear to a dying mind, and the hinge between two lives, where nothing passes over and yet nothing is lost. · about 9 minutes
  11. 26. Ten ways to actKamma is made through three doors, body, speech and mind, in ten typical ways for ill and ten for good. Which root drives each, when an intention becomes a full course of action, and the ten bases of merit. · about 8 minutes
  12. 27. From kamma to its resultsHow each kind of kamma is matched to the rebirth it can give, why the quality of a good deed matters as much as the deed, and what ripens within a life. · about 6 minutes
  13. 28. Thirty-one planesThe worlds of the Theravāda cosmology, from the hells to the formless realms, and how each one corresponds to a kind of mind. · about 7 minutes
  14. 29. Who is reborn whereNineteen cittas can begin a life, and each belongs to its own kind of world. Where a being can go next depends on where it dies and with what, and for the noble ones there is no going down. · about 8 minutes

The way out

Calm, insight, and the moment of the path.

  1. 30. The four noble truthsThe first sermon's frame, read the Abhidhamma way: the realities sorted under the truths, with a remainder left outside, each truth with its own task, and all four seen in a single moment. · about 9 minutes
  2. 31. Forty meditation subjectsThe *Manual*'s catalogue of calm, after the *Visuddhimagga*: subjects matched to temperaments, the signs that arise on the way, and how far each subject can take the mind. · about 8 minutes
  3. 32. The noble eightfold pathEight factors named as the mental factors they are: scattered over many moments in ordinary practice, and together, in one citta, at the moment of the path. · about 9 minutes
  4. 33. The tree of purificationThe path of insight pictured as a tree: the soil, the roots, a trunk of seven purifications, the knowledges that climb it, and the single moment at its crown. · about 9 minutes
  5. 34. How insight is practisedWhat the texts tell a meditator to do: the ground to stand on, what to watch and how, how to keep the balance, what begins to show, what can go wrong, and what the texts leave to teachers. · about 15 minutes
  6. 35. How insight is taught todayThe living methods side by side: what a beginner is asked to do in each, how much calm comes first, which map of stages is used, how closely each follows the texts it cites, and how to choose and practise safely. · about 16 minutes
  7. 36. Ten knowledges, one by oneWhat insight looks at, the groundwork it needs, and the ten knowledges the Manual lists, from comprehension to conformity, each with what it sees and why it gives way to the next. · about 11 minutes
  8. 37. Thirty-seven requisites, fourteen statesThe Buddha gathered his teaching on practice into seven sets: thirty-seven items in all. The Abhidhamma counts what they are made of and finds fourteen states, with energy and mindfulness doing most of the work. · about 9 minutes
  9. 38. Four noble personsStream-enterer, once-returner, non-returner, arahant: what each of the four paths cuts away for good, the kinds of person on each step, and the two attainments that only the noble know. · about 10 minutes

Looking back

One moment through every lens, and other schools' maps.

  1. 39. One moment, every lensThe mango moment once more, read through ten of the Abhidhamma's frameworks in turn, to see how the whole system bears on a single sight. · about 8 minutes
  2. 40. Two moments, side by sideThe same ripe mango, seen twice: once wanted, once given away. Identical up to determining, the two processes part at the javanas, and everything downstream parts with them. · about 5 minutes
  3. 41. Other maps of the same groundOther Indian schools also took experience apart into elements, and reached different counts. Comparing the maps shows which choices were open, and why the Theravāda made the ones it did. · about 7 minutes
