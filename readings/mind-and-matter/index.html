Mind and matter together

The nāma–rūpa model. Two currents that hold each other up, moment by moment: matter gives mind its bases and objects, mind makes and sustains matter, and at conception the two arise together.

About 14 minutes

In brief

  • Every moment of life in this world is mind and matter (nāmarūpa); together they are everything conditioned, and Nibbāna is not one of the two currents.
  • Matter supports mind as its bases, the sense organs and the heart-base, and as its objects, such as colour and sound.
  • Mind supports matter: most kinds of consciousness produce matter such as posture and speech, and past volition produced the living core of the body.
  • Temperature and food renew the body on their own, and only at conception do mind and matter arise together as equal, mutual supports.
  • Mind and matter keep different time: a moment of mind is brief, while a unit of matter lasts seventeen such moments.
  • In the Visuddhimagga, discerning mind and matter is purification of view, the first step of insight: hearing seen as sound, ear and hearing, no one behind.

The readings so far have taken the Abhidhamma’s lists one at a time: the eighty-nine cittas, the fifty-two mental factors, the twenty-eight kinds of matter. Experience never comes in lists. Every moment of a life in this world is mind and matter at once, each holding the other up. The tradition calls the pair nāmarūpanāmarūpa · mentality-materiality (link 4) mind and matter. As link 4 of the wheel: the resultant mental factors and kamma-born matter of a new life; in the suttas, feeling, perception, volition, contact and attention with the elements; in the Abhidhamma, citta with its factors, and the 28 kinds of matter, mind and matter, and this reading is about how they fit together. It is the hinge of the whole study: after it, and two shorter readings that round off the first part, the readings turn from what there is to how it unfolds.

Two words, two depths

The word is older than the Abhidhamma. In the suttas, name-and-form is the fourth link of dependent origination, and the Buddha defines it there: “name” is feeling, perception, volition, contact and attention; “form” is the four great elements and the matter derived from them (SN 12.2). Consciousness is not part of it; it is the link just before, on which name-and-form depends.

The Abhidhamma keeps the pair but takes it down to its ultimate terms. Mind, nāmanāma · mind mind: citta with its factors, which “bend” toward objects; the word also means “name”, is citta together with its mental factors; matter, rūparūpa · matter matter: the third reality, 28 kinds; as an aggregate it is called form, is the twenty-eight kinds. Together they are everything conditioned. The fourth ultimate reality, after citta, its factors and matter, is Nibbāna, and it is neither: nothing produces it, and it conditions only as an object (VIII §17, §19). The path and fruition cittas take it, and so, briefly, do change-of-lineage and reviewing (IX §34).noteThe Dhammasaṅgaṇī, sorting all dhammas into the pair “nāma and rūpa”, puts Nibbāna on the side of nāma, because, like mind, it is not matter and the mind bends towards it as an object. In the model here it stands apart, since it is not one of the two currents.

Two currents that hold each other up

In the worlds that have both, mind cannot arise without a physical base, and much of the body is produced and kept going by mind. Each side feeds the other, and the arrows run both ways.

Matter feeds mind in two ways. Every citta rests on a vatthuvatthu · physical base the matter a citta arises from: a sense organ or the heart-base, a base: the five sense organs for the five kinds of sense consciousness, and the heart-base for all the rest. And the five kinds of sense object, colour, sound, smell, taste and the tangible, are all matter.

Mind feeds matter in two ways too. Seventy-five of the eighty-nine kinds of citta produce matter, each at the moment it arises, from the first life-continuum after rebirth on: posture, gesture, speech, a smile. And volition, which is kamma, produced the living core of this body from conception on: the sense organs, the heart-base, the life faculty.

Meanwhile temperature and food keep renewing the body without asking the mind. The fire element in every group of matter gives rise to new groups, and the nutritive essence of food does the same.

Nine ways they meet

The model below draws nine of these relations as moving threads. On the left is mind, a single citta with its factors circling it; on the right, a body drawn as a cloud of tiny groups of matter, with the heart-base and the eye inside it. Around them are the outside sources: a visible object, past kamma, temperature and food.

Each relation is made of several of the Paṭṭhāna’s twenty-four kinds of condition working at once (they are introduced in The web of twenty-four conditions). The heart-base, for instance, helps a citta in five ways at once: as its base (support), by having arisen before it (prenascence), as matter helping mind (dissociation), and by being present and not yet gone (presence, non-disappearance). Choose a relation to see which.

Who supports whom

Seen from the other side, the Paṭṭhāna’s twenty-four conditions can be sorted by which way they run. Here is every condition that crosses between mind and matter, or runs within matter, with what it means in that direction.

  • 13. kamma Past volition also produces kamma-born matter, from rebirth onwards; present volition supports the matter its citta produces.
  • 14. result Resultant cittas also support the matter arising with them, such as kamma-born matter at rebirth.
  • 15. nutriment The mental nutriments also sustain the matter that arises with them.
  • 16. faculty The mental faculties also govern the matter that their citta produces.
  • 17. jhāna The jhāna factors also support the matter that their citta produces.
  • 18. path The path factors also support the matter that their citta produces.
  • 20. dissociation Mind helps matter, a different kind of thing: the matter its citta produces, and, later, the body arisen earlier.
  • 21. presence Present cittas sustain matter, both what arises with them and the body arisen earlier.
  • 24. non-disappearance Cittas that have not disappeared sustain matter.

Matter → matter · 7

  • 6. conascence The four great elements arise together and help each other and the derived matter arising with them.
  • 7. mutuality The four great elements support one another, each by the others.
  • 8. support The great elements are the support of all derived matter.
  • 15. nutriment Edible food nourishes this body.
  • 16. faculty The physical life faculty sustains the kamma-born matter it is part of. The two sex faculties are not faculty conditions: they shape the body but do none of a condition's three works, producing, supporting or maintaining (Guide to VIII §24; Vism XVII §609).
  • 21. presence Present great elements, food and the life faculty sustain matter.
  • 24. non-disappearance Great elements, food and life faculty, while they last, sustain matter.

Mind → mind · 21

  • 1. root The roots, such as greed or non-greed, make firm the citta and the factors they arise with.
  • 2. object Any citta or factor, past, present or future, can be the object of a later citta, as when one recollects.
  • 3. predominance Conascent predominance: desire, energy, consciousness or investigation leads the citta and factors it arises with; only one at a time, and only in javanas with two or three roots.
  • 4. proximity Each citta, as it ceases, conditions the very next one: the life-continuum gives way to adverting, adverting to seeing, and so on.
  • 5. contiguity The same relation stressed as seamless: no gap between one citta and the next.
  • 6. conascence A citta and its factors arise together and help one another to arise.
  • 7. mutuality A citta and its factors hold each other up, like the three sticks of a tripod.
  • 8. support A citta and its factors are each other's support, as the earth supports trees.
  • 9. decisive support Faith, a past resolve, or a habit of anger strongly supports later states; each citta also strongly supports the next.
  • 12. repetition Each mundane javana repeats and strengthens the next of its kind, like a lesson made firm by practice. A path citta does not, since a fruition follows it, not another path.
  • 13. kamma Volition coordinates the citta and factors it arises with; past volition, ripening later, produces resultant cittas.
  • 14. result Resultant cittas and factors, arising without effort, support one another.
  • 15. nutriment The three mental nutriments, contact, volition and consciousness, sustain the mind they arise with.
  • 16. faculty The mental faculties, such as mindfulness and wisdom, each govern their own domain in the citta.
  • 17. jhāna The jhāna factors, such as initial application and one-pointedness, fix the citta closely on its object: in every citta but the ten sense consciousnesses.
  • 18. path The path factors, right or wrong, lead the mind in their direction: in every citta but the eighteen rootless ones.
  • 19. association A citta and its factors are associated: they arise and cease together, with one object and one base.
  • 21. presence A citta and its factors help each other by being present together.
  • 22. absence A citta helps the next by ceasing: its absence gives the next room to arise.
  • 23. disappearance The same as absence, seen from the side of disappearing.
  • 24. non-disappearance The same as presence, seen from the side of not disappearing: a citta and its factors, while they last.

The same ways are drawn as strands in the 3D model of the 24 conditions.

Notice how much more traffic runs from mind to matter than the reverse. Matter helps mind mainly by being there first, as base and object. Mind helps matter in many ways at once, through its roots, its volition, its jhāna and path factors, its nutriments and faculties. And only once in a life do the two hold each other up as equals: at conception, when the rebirth-linking citta and the heart-base arise together, each the mutual support of the other.

Where they meet: the six doors

Every kind of knowing needs an object, a door it comes through, and a base it rests on. At five of the six doors, everything but the knowing is matter. The eye-door is the eye-sensitivity, its object is colour, its base is the same eye-sensitivity, and its consciousness is eye-consciousness. The mind-door is different: its door is a citta, the life-continuum, though the mind still rests on the heart-base. The six doors are drawn in The All, counted twice.

Matter in time

Mind and matter do not keep the same time. A citta lasts one mind-moment, with three sub-moments: arising, presence and dissolution. A unit of matter lasts seventeen mind-moments, fifty-one sub-moments. That is why a sense object that strikes a door lasts exactly long enough for one complete process, the seventeen moments traced in The mango and the moment.

It also means that at any instant, dozens of generations of matter are alive in the body at once. In the model below, each bar is one unit of matter from its arising to its ceasing, in four lanes for its four origins; the row of discs is the stream of cittas.

Kamma makes new matter at every sub-moment; a citta makes matter at its arising. Kamma-born matter stops arising seventeen mind-moments before death, so that it ends together with the death consciousness. Temperature-born matter alone outlasts death: the matter of a corpse is temperature-born.

The smallest groups of matter

Matter never arises alone. It comes in small groups, kalāpakalāpa the smallest group of matter: eight or more kinds arising and ceasing togethers, built on the same core: the four great elements and the four kinds of matter that always go with them, colour, smell, taste and nutritive essence. These eight are called the inseparables. Kamma, mind, temperature and nutriment each add their own members. The model shows the twenty-one groups, with the eight inseparables at the corners of a cube.

The four great elements hold up all the rest. The Visuddhimagga defines each by what it is, what it does and how it shows itself:

Inside the mind side

On the side of mind the unit is a citta with its factors. A factor arises with its citta, ceases with it, takes the same object and rests on the same base. The citta is the knowing; the factors colour it. Choose a citta to see which factors make it up.

Mind makes matter

Not every citta can produce matter, and those that can do different things with it. The ten kinds of sense consciousness are too weak; the four immaterial resultants arise where there is no matter. A smaller set can move the body and the voice, and a smaller set again can make a smile.

Not every world has both

Which bases a being has depends on its plane. In the immaterial worlds there is mind with no matter at all, and the citta needs no base. Among the non-percipient beings there is matter with no mind. Everywhere else the two currents run together, and the groups of matter present at the moment of rebirth-linking differ by plane.

Why this matters

For practice, all of this comes down to one first step. The Visuddhimagga makes the discerning of mind and matter, nāmarūpa-paricchedanāmarūpa-pariccheda · defining mind and matter discerning mind and matter as they are, without a self; the purification of view, first of the insight knowledges, the purification of view: the first knowledge of insight, on which the rest are built (see The tree of purification). To see a moment of hearing as sound, ear and the hearing, and nothing over and above them, is the beginning of seeing that there is no one behind the hearing. The model on this page is a map of what such seeing finds.

Reflect and discuss

Opening question

Sit still for a minute and notice one moment of seeing, or of hearing. What in it is matter, and what is mind? Where exactly do they meet?

One way to see it, after you have tried

The colour and the eye are matter; the seeing, and the liking or not liking that follows, are mind. They meet at the door: the eye-sensitivity is the base of the seeing, the colour is its object, and neither of them knows anything. What knows is the citta, and it lasts a single moment. The commentaries call this discerning of mind and matter the purification of view, the first of the insight knowledges.
