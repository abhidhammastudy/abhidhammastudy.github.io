One moment, every lens

The mango moment once more, read through ten of the Abhidhamma's frameworks in turn, to see how the whole system bears on a single sight.

About 8 minutesManual Review: I, II, III §§8–22, IV §6, VI §§9–22, VIII

In brief

  • This page takes one small moment, a man seeing a ripe mango and wanting it, and reads it through ten of the Abhidhamma's frameworks in turn.
  • In ordinary speech "I see a mango and want it"; in ultimate terms there are moments of mind, their factors, an eye and a colour, with no owner.
  • Each framework, from the seventeen-moment process to the conditions and kamma, describes the whole moment completely in its own terms.
  • Most of them point to the same small place, the moments in which the mind acts (javana), as where something new is done.
  • None needs a self to finish its account; the lists exist so that a single moment can be seen clearly enough to be let go of.

A man sees a ripe mango and wants it. That moment has run through these readings since The mango and the moment. Here it is read once more, lens by lens, to see the whole system at work on one small event.

Two truths. Conventionally, “I see a mango and want it”. Ultimately: cittas, mental factors, an eye-base and a visible form, with no owner behind them. Both statements are true. The second is what the rest of the lenses examine.

Cittas. Seven kinds of citta run in the active part of the process: five-door adverting, eye-consciousness, receiving, investigating, determining, the javana, and registration. The javana here is greed with joy, unprompted, without wrong view. Each has its number on the map of the eighty-nine.

Factors. In each javana, the seven universals and the six occasionals are present, with the four unwholesome factors that come with every unwholesome citta, and greed. Conceit may join them; wrong view does not, in this citta. None of the beautiful factors is present.

Matter. The eye’s sensitivity and the colour of the mango are matter, each in its group of eight or more. The colour’s group lasts seventeen mind-moments, exactly the length of the process. If the man reaches for the fruit, mind-born matter moves the hand: a citta willed it. The Vibhāvinī adds that the moving comes from javanas of the mind-door processes that follow, since five-door javanas are too weak to produce intimation.

The process. Three moments of the life-continuum, five that turn to, see and settle the object, seven javanas, two of registration: the seventeen moments of a strong object at the eye door. Only the javanas make kamma.

Conditions. The mango helps the seeing as object. The eye helps it as support and as a prenascent base. Each citta helps the next by proximity. The factors of each citta help one another by conascence and mutuality. Past habits of liking mangoes help the javanas by decisive support. And each javana but the last helps the next by repetition.That is eight of the twenty-four conditions in one glance; a fuller reading, in One moment, twenty-four questions, finds twenty or more for a single javana. The Manual gathers all twenty-four under four: object, decisive support, kamma and presence.

Dependent origination. Contact, feeling, craving: three links of the wheel in a single process. The Vibhaṅga teaches the whole series within one moment of citta, and the mango moment shows why that is not a forced reading. The feeling of the sight is a result; the wanting is new.

Kamma. The volition in the greed-rooted javanas is unwholesome kamma. It is small, and it is reserve kamma, unlikely to ripen at death unless repeated until it becomes habitual. Repeated, it becomes a habit, and that habit is a decisive support for the next sight of a mango.

Planes. The cittas are all of the sense sphere. A Brahmā of the fine-material worlds has eyes and could see the mango, but the commentators say its nose, tongue and body lack sensitivity, so it could not smell, taste or touch it. A being of the immaterial worlds, with no body at all, would not have this process. The man’s plane sets what his mind can meet.

The path. The same seventeen moments are where practice works. Seeing is given. At adverting and determining, where attention turns, wise attention could have led to wholesome javanas: seeing the colour as colour, the wanting as wanting. In an arahant the javanas would be functional, merely seeing, and leave no result.

What the lenses share

Laid side by side, the ten readings agree on a few things. Every one of them describes the moment completely in its own terms. None of them needs a self to finish the account. And most of them point to the same small place, the javanas, as where something new is done.

That is what the Abhidhamma is, seen whole: not ten theories, but ten ways of looking at the same ground, each sharpening the others. The lists exist so that a single moment, like this one, can be seen clearly enough to be let go of.

Reflect and discuss

Opening question

Take one ordinary moment from today, a sound, a craving, a flash of irritation, and read it through three lenses from this essay. What does each show that the others miss?

One way to see it, after you have tried

The two truths show that "I was irritated" is a convention for cittas and factors. The process shows where in the sequence the irritation arose, in the javanas, after a given result. The conditions show what fed it: an object, a habit as decisive support, a body that was tired. Each lens adds something, and none of them ever finds the one who was irritated.
