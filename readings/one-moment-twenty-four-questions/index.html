One moment, twenty-four questions

Two moments of the mango process, seeing the fruit and wanting it, put to each of the twenty-four conditions in turn: which hold, what gives them, and why the others do not.

About 14 minutesManual VIII §§11–27 with the Guides and Tables 8.2–8.4 (the conditions one citta receives: eye-consciousness and a greed-rooted javana)

In brief

  • Of any moment one can ask which of the twenty-four conditions it receives, and from what. Ledi Sayadaw's Paṭṭhānuddesa-dīpanī does this citta by citta, and a check against Table 8.3 of the Manual bears its counts out.
  • Eye-consciousness seeing the mango receives eighteen kinds of condition, from six sources: the eye, the colour, the adverting just ceased, past kamma, strong earlier states such as climate and food, and the citta with its seven factors.
  • A greed-rooted javana wanting the mango receives twenty, or twenty-one if a predominant leads it; the later javanas add repetition, for twenty-one or twenty-two.
  • Between seeing and wanting there is a gap in time: the Visuddhimagga lets resultant feeling help craving only as a decisive support, never by arising with it.
  • When the Manual says matter helps mind "in one way", it can be read as saying that prenascence is the only condition running from matter to mind alone; the eye in fact helps seeing in six ways, and the colour in four.
  • Asked of the twelve links, the same questions show which joints are held by many conditions and which by one; feeling to craving is held by one.

The web of twenty-four conditions named the kinds of help the Paṭṭhāna distinguishes. This reading puts them to work. It takes two moments from the mango and the moment: the fifth, in which eye-consciousness sees the fruit, and one of the seven javanas in which the mind wants it. Of each it asks twenty-four questions. Is it helped by a root? By an object? By a predominant? And so on down the list: for each condition that holds, what gives it; for each that does not, why not.

The exercise is an old one. The Burmese teacher Ledi Sayadaw, in his Paṭṭhānuddesa-dīpanī, counts the conditions each type of citta receives (in the section Paccayaghaṭanānaya), and checked against Table 8.3 of the Manual row by row they hold. Two cautions. The counts are of kinds of condition, not of helpers: one helper can give several kinds, and one kind can come from several helpers. And Ledi’s figures are ceilings for a type; a single moment may fall short, as the first javana will.

The setting is the mango reading’s: a human being, an object strong enough to run all seventeen moments, and a pleasant sight, so that the seeing ripens from some past wholesome kamma. The colour of the mango and the eye-sensitivity that sees it both arose with the first of the seventeen moments, the past life-continuum, and both last through the process (Guide to IV §6; Guide to VIII §24 puts the sensitivity’s arising at that moment’s static phase).

Seeing: eighteen of twenty-four

Eye-consciousness here is a wholesome-resultant citta with neutral feeling, and it has no roots. It receives eighteen kinds of condition (Ledi, Paṭṭhānuddesa-dīpanī, on the sense consciousnesses), and they come from six places.

From the eye. The eye-sensitivity arose four mind-moments before and is still standing. It helps the seeing in six ways: as its base, which is support; by having arisen first, which is vatthu-purejātavatthu-purejāta · base prenascence a physical base, arisen earlier and still standing, helping the cittas that rest on it, base prenascence; as a faculty, whose rule sharp sight and dim sight show; as matter helping mind, which is dissociation; and by being present and not yet gone, which are presence and non-disappearance (Vism XVII §641; Table 8.3 rows 8, 10, 16, 20, 21).

From the colour. The visible form helps in four ways: as object; by having arisen first, which is ārammaṇa-purejātaārammaṇa-purejāta · object prenascence a present object of matter, arisen earlier, helping the cittas that take it, object prenascence; and by presence and non-disappearance (Paccayaniddesa §§2, 10, 21, 24; Vism XVII §642 says the same of eye-contact; Table 8.3 row 10). It does not dominate the seeing, nor lean on it heavily. Object predominance, and the object decisive support that goes with it, reach only certain javanas, and sense consciousness is not among them (Table 8.3 rows 3, 9).

From the moment before. Five-door adverting has just ceased. Its ceasing gives the seeing proximity, contiguity, proximity decisive support, absence and disappearance: five conditions with the same giver and the same receiver (§13 with the Guide; Guide to §17). The Paṭṭhāna’s definition fixes the next steps of the order itself: eye-consciousness for the mind element that receives, and that for the mind-consciousness element that investigates (Paccayaniddesa §4, and its general clause, 16.1). The commentary fills in the rest by the same method.

From past kamma. The volition of a past wholesome act helps the seeing across time, as asynchronous kamma, nānākkhaṇika-kammanānākkhaṇika-kamma · asynchronous kamma a past volition helping its results at a later time; kamma condition across a gap (§14; Table 8.3 row 13). The Manual adds, closing its account of decisive support, that kamma is also a natural decisive support for its results (§17), and the Paṭṭhāna gives the same case: wholesome kamma for its result (Pañhāvāra, CS §423).

From further back. Climate, food, lodging and persons, and the ignorance and craving behind the old kamma, help as natural decisive support (Ledi, Paṭṭhānuddesa-dīpanī, on the sense consciousnesses; Paccayaniddesa §9).

From within. The citta arises with the seven universal factors, eight states in all, and they hold each other up by conascence, mutuality, conascent support, association, presence and non-disappearance, and, since all eight are results, by result. Contact, volition and the citta itself feed the rest as nutriment. Volition organises them as conascent kamma. The citta, the feeling and the life faculty each govern their share as faculties; those are the three the Dhammasaṅgaṇī names for this citta (Dhs §§431–442).

That makes eighteen. Six do not hold, each for a plain reason. There is no root, because the citta is rootless, and therefore no path, which reaches only rooted cittas (Vism XVII §611; Table 8.3 rows 1, 18). There is no jhāna: the sense consciousnesses are the one class the jhāna factors do not reach (Vism XVII §§610–611; Table 8.3 row 17). There is no predominance: a conascent predominant leads only in javanas with two or three roots (Guide to §19), and the colour, as said, cannot be an object predominance here. There is no repetition, which passes only from javana to javana (Guide to §13). And there is no postnascence, which never helps mind at all, only a body that arose before (§15).

What the seeing gives is soon told. To receiving it gives proximity and its four companions. To its own factors it gives the conascent set. To the body already standing it gives postnascence, for every citta from the first life-continuum on supports matter that arose earlier and is still present (Table 8.3 row 11). That body includes the eye-sensitivity on which the seeing rests: the eye helps the seeing by prenascence, and the seeing helps the eye by postnascence. Both rows are in the Table; that they meet on one piece of matter is our application of them. What the seeing does not do is produce matter. The ten sense consciousnesses are too weak to (VI §11 with the Guide), so the seeing gives nothing to matter by conascence.

Wanting: twenty, or twenty-one

In the mango reading the javanas are greed with joy, without wrong view, unprompted. Ledi’s ceiling for the greed-rooted cittas is twenty-two, everything except postnascence and result (Paṭṭhānuddesa-dīpanī, on the unwholesome cittas). A single javana sits a little below it.

From the roots. Greed and delusion are the two roots of this citta. By root condition each helps the other, the rest of the citta, and the matter the citta produces (Pañhāvāra, CS §402; Table 8.3 row 1).

From the heart-base. The eye is no longer the base: every citta of the process except the seeing rests on the heart-base. It helps in five ways: support, prenascence, dissociation, presence and non-disappearance (Vism XVII §641; Guide to §16). The faculty that the eye gave the seeing has no counterpart here, for the heart-base is not a faculty.

From the colour. The javana takes the same colour, still present, and receives from it object, object prenascence, presence and non-disappearance. Object prenascence reaches beyond the sense consciousness to the sense-sphere cittas that take a present object (Pañhāvāra CS §424, patthana1.2:816.1; Table 8.3 row 10(2); the Vibhāvinī on Saṅgaha VIII §19 (CS), which is CMA §16, extends it to mind-door processes).

From the moment before. For the first javana, determining has just ceased and gives proximity and its four companions (Pañhāvāra, CS §417). From the second javana to the seventh the giver is the javana before, and it gives one thing more: repetition, āsevanaāsevana · repetition Repetition is the way each javana strengthens the next, as a lesson is learned by reciting it., the help a state gives the next of its own kind, making it more practised, as a lesson is by recitation (Guide to §13; Pañhāvāra CS §426, patthana1.2:822.1). The first javana receives none, since determining is not a javana, and repetition passes only between javanas of one kind (Table 8.3 row 12).

From within. The citta and its factors give one another the conascent set, nutriment and conascent kamma, as in the seeing. What is new comes in three groups that the seeing lacked. As faculties: energy, concentration, the mind, joy and life. As jhāna factors: initial and sustained application, zest, joy and one-pointedness. As path factors: wrong intention, wrong effort and wrong concentration. That is three, because this citta has no wrong view; the greed cittas with view have it as a fourth (Dhs §§400–401; the variant with view, §§365, 398; Table 8.3 rows 16–18). A path factor here is a road to a bad destination, not the noble path (Guide to §14).

From far away. Natural decisive support, pakatūpanissayapakatūpanissaya · natural decisive support strong help from what came before: earlier lust or faith, pleasure or pain, a person, food, climate, lodging, past kamma, is the widest condition in the list, and the javana takes it from many places: earlier greed, an old liking for mangoes, an earlier pleasure, the weather, food, company. The Manual names lust and faith, pleasure and pain, persons, food, season and lodging among such supports (§17). The Paṭṭhāna gives greed as the decisive support of later states, and bodily pleasure, climate, food and lodging as supports of unwholesome ones (Pañhāvāra, CS §423).

So the first javana receives root, object, proximity, contiguity, conascence, mutuality, support, decisive support, prenascence, kamma, nutriment, faculty, jhāna, path, association, dissociation, presence, absence, disappearance and non-disappearance: twenty. Each later javana adds repetition, for twenty-one. Result is missing because an unwholesome citta is not a result, and postnascence because it never helps mind.

Possibly, predominance. Two kinds of predominance could raise the count by one. A conascent predominant, desire, energy or the citta itself, may lead the javana, but only one at a time, and only on some occasions (Guide to §19; Guide to VII §20). The Paṭṭhāna commentary makes a predominant certain only in unwholesome cittas fixed in wrong view, and in the others present only when it leads; it also rules investigation out of the unwholesome altogether (on Paccayaniddesa §3). Ledi says the predominants of the greed cittas operate kadāci, “sometimes”. Investigation could not be one here in any case, for this citta has no wisdom, and no source we have read says whether an ordinary passing want has a predominant at all. The colour too may become an object predominance, ārammaṇādhipatiārammaṇādhipati · object predominance an object held weighty, which dominates the mind that takes it, and with it an object decisive support, if it is made weighty. The Paṭṭhāna gives exactly this case, a visible form made weighty and relished, and lust arising (Pañhāvāra, CS §416), and its commentary allows matter to be weighty in this way only for greed. Whether the javanas of a single glance can make the colour weighty, or only the mind-door processes that follow, no source read decides. We mark both kinds possible, not necessary, so the count is twenty or twenty-one for the first javana and twenty-one or twenty-two for the rest, never a fixed twenty-one.

What the javana gives. To the next javana it gives proximity’s five and repetition; the seventh gives registration proximity’s five alone, since registration is a result and not of its kind (Table 8.3 row 12). To the matter it produces it gives much of what it gives its own factors: root, a predominant if there is one, conascence, support, kamma, nutriment, faculty, jhāna, path, dissociation, presence and non-disappearance, but never mutuality or association (§14; §§19–26). To the body standing from earlier moments it gives postnascence. And to the future it gives two things. Its volition becomes asynchronous kamma, which can ripen as unwholesome results and kamma-born matter (Pañhāvāra CS §427, patthana1.2:828.1), and its greed becomes a natural decisive support for later states (Pañhāvāra, CS §423), the nearest the conditions come to describing a habit. Ledi sets a limit worth keeping: the Paṭṭhāna maps only the chief result. A long chain, from greed to work to wealth to gifts, belongs to the suttas’ method of “this being, that is”, not to the twenty-four (Paṭṭhānuddesa-dīpanī, closing section).

The seam between seeing and wanting

The mango reading asked where a sight becomes a want. The twenty-four give an exact answer, and the Visuddhimagga states it when it reads the links of dependent origination through the conditions. Contact helps the feeling in eye-consciousness in eight ways, arising with it and sharing its moment; but for the feelings of receiving, investigating and registration, which come later, contact is a decisive support and nothing more (Vism XVII §643). And feeling helps craving by decisive support alone, because the feeling meant in that link is resultant feeling (§644). In the mango process, then, the neutral feeling of the seeing and the joy of investigating do not make the greed. They help it from a moment or more away, strongly, but not as part of it.

Inside the javana it is different. The joy and the greed arise together in one citta and help each other by conascence, mutuality and association. The Vibhaṅga, in its Abhidhamma section, reads the whole series of links within one such citta (Vibh §243 ff.; the greed-rooted citta from §248). The two readings do not contradict each other. One follows the links across moments and lives, the other within a single moment: across the seam, feeling helps craving from a distance; inside the want, once it has arisen, joy and greed hold each other up. That the distance is where restraint can work is the site’s reading of §644, not the text’s claim.noteWhere teachers differ. Proximity and contiguity. The Guide calls the two identical in meaning and then gives them different emphases: nothing comes between, and the next arises in its fixed order (Guide to VIII §13). The Visuddhimagga puts the fixed order under proximity itself, reads sam- as “well”, that is, without a seam, and rejects a view of some teachers that the two differ in meaning (Vism XVII §598). How many faculties in seeing? The Dhammasaṅgaṇī names three in eye-consciousness: mind, feeling and life (Dhs §§431–442). Ledi has one-pointedness govern there as a faculty too (Paṭṭhānuddesa-dīpanī, on the sense consciousnesses); we have not found his reason. The count of eighteen kinds is the same either way. Can one glance make the mango weighty? The Paṭṭhāna illustrates object predominance with a visible form relished (Pañhāvāra, CS §416); its commentary limits weighty matter to greed; Ledi counts object predominance among the conditions of the greed cittas. None says whether a five-door javana can do it. Does decisive support ever reach matter? Table 8.3 and Ledi say no: only mind is decisively supported, and past kamma builds the body by kamma condition alone. The Visuddhimagga once says that the kamma-forming consciousness helps kamma-born matter by decisive support, but it marks this as the suttas’ way of speaking, not the Paṭṭhāna’s (Vism XVII §640).

How mind and matter hold each other up

The Manual does not walk through the twenty-four in order. It sorts them by what helps what, in six kinds, and its verse gives the count of each (VIII §12). Mind helps mind in six ways: proximity, contiguity, absence, disappearance, repetition and association (§13). Mind helps mind and matter together in five: root, jhāna, path, kamma and result (§14). Mind helps matter in one, postnascence (§15). Matter helps mind in one, prenascence (§16). Concepts, mind and matter help mind in two, object and decisive support (§17). And mind and matter help mind and matter in nine: predominance, conascence, mutuality, support, nutriment, faculty, dissociation, presence and non-disappearance (§18). Six, five, one, one, two and nine make twenty-four, and on our count each condition is placed exactly once.

That is where a reader can trip. “Matter helps mind in one way” sounds as if the eye did little for the seeing, yet above it helped in six ways and the colour in four (Vism XVII §641; Paccayaniddesa §§2, 10, 21, 24). In effect, each condition sits under the widest kind of relation it can serve. Support, faculty, dissociation, presence and non-disappearance can run between mind and matter in either direction, so they sit under §18; object sits under §17. What §16 says is that prenascence is the one condition that runs only from matter to mind. The same holds the other way: postnascence is the one condition that runs only from mind to matter (§15), though a javana helps the matter it produces in eleven ways, or twelve with a predominant.

The heart-base shows the whole pattern in one piece of matter. In the course of a life it helps every citta but the sense consciousnesses in the five ways met above (Vism XVII §641). At rebirth it cannot be prenascent, for it arises at the same moment as the rebirth citta. Then the two help each other by conascence and mutuality, with support, dissociation, presence and non-disappearance, and that is the only moment in a life when mind and matter help each other by mutuality (Vism XVII §641; §§20–21, 25; Table 8.3 rows 6–8; Guide to §16). From the first life-continuum on, the help also runs back: each later citta supports the body that arose before it, the heart-base included, by postnascence (§15 with the Guide). The Paṭṭhāna never names the heart. It speaks only of “the matter in dependence on which the mind element and the mind-consciousness element occur”, their support (§8), prenascent to the mind element and to the mind-consciousness element at some times only (§10); and it says material states are conascent with mental states at some times (§6), which the Visuddhimagga takes as the heart-base (Vism XVII §599). Identifying it with the heart is the commentaries’ work, and the conditions say nothing more about where the mind is.

Matter can also help mind from far off, but only in one direction. Food, climate and lodging are natural decisive supports for states of mind (§17; Paccayaniddesa §9). The reverse does not happen in the Paṭṭhāna’s sense: only mind is decisively supported, and past kamma helps the body it builds by kamma condition alone (Table 8.3 rows 9, 13).

Between the links

The same questions can be put to the twelve links of the wheel, and the Visuddhimagga puts them, reading the links across three lives as the wheel across three lives does (Vism XVII §§616–651). Some joints are held by many conditions at once, some by one.

  • Ignorance helps meritorious formations of the sense and fine-material spheres as object and decisive support, and the imperturbable formations of the formless spheres as decisive support only. Demeritorious formations it helps in many ways, since it arises within them as a root (§616).
  • Formations help consciousness by asynchronous kamma and decisive support (§636).
  • Resultant consciousness helps resultant mentality in nine ways; at rebirth it helps the heart-base in nine and other matter in eight (§640).
  • The eye-base helps eye-contact in six ways, and the visible form helps it in four (§642).
  • Contact helps the feeling of its own citta in eight ways, and later feelings only as decisive support (§643).
  • Feeling helps craving as decisive support only (§644).
  • Craving helps sensual clinging as decisive support only, and the clingings of view in seven ways, or eight (§645).
  • Clinging helps the becoming it shares a citta with by the conascent conditions, and other becoming as decisive support (§650).
  • Becoming, here kamma, helps birth by kamma and decisive support; birth helps ageing-and-death as decisive support (§651).

The joints held by decisive support alone are the ones that work across a gap in time, and feeling to craving is the one the mango process crosses. The Vibhaṅga’s single-moment reading ties the same links by conascence instead, so a table of conditions between the links has to say which reading it follows; these counts belong to the reading across three lives.

Ledi draws the point of the exercise at the end of his grouping of the conditions. Every conditioned dhamma depends on conditions, none on anyone’s will, and none arises from a few conditions, only from many (Paṭṭhānuddesa-dīpanī, Paccayasabhāga; compare Vism XVII §617). Eighteen kinds of help for one glance, twenty or more for one want: the count is the argument. There is no one in the list who sees or wants, only what helps, and how.

Reflect and discuss

Opening question

The seeing receives eighteen kinds of condition and the wanting twenty or more, yet the feeling in the seeing helps the wanting in only one way, and from a distance. If the feeling does not make the greed, what in the javana's list made the greed likely?

One way to see it, after you have tried

Read down the list. The object was a desirable colour, still present. Before it came adverting and determining, which the commentaries name as where attention turns wisely or unwisely (Atthasālinī, Vipākuddhārakathā, on Dhs §498). Behind it stood natural decisive supports: earlier greed, an old liking for mangoes, bodily ease, the weather, company. The feeling of the sight bears on the greed too, but only as one more decisive support (Vism XVII §644). None of these makes the greed alone; together they make it likely. The inner ones, earlier greed and liking, were laid down by earlier javanas of the same kind, and a reader may draw the conclusion the site draws, though the texts do not state it: they can be laid down otherwise.
