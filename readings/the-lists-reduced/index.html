The lists reduced to their factors

Taints, floods, bonds, clingings, hindrances, fetters: chapter VII's many names for what binds, and the fourteen factors they all come down to.

About 6 minutes

In brief

  • A chapter of the Manual gathers many lists of what binds the mind, such as the hindrances and fetters, and asks which mental factor each item is.
  • The dozens of names come down to fourteen unwholesome factors; greed alone appears in all nine groups, under sixteen different names.
  • One reason to keep the names: they differ in reach: the commentaries give the taint of sensual desire a narrower range than the tie of covetousness, and the paths end them at different times.
  • Six of the chapter's mixed lists, such as the roots, are also kinds of condition, so the lists say both what factors are and how they help.
  • The thirty-seven requisites of enlightenment reduce the same way, to fourteen states, with energy and mindfulness appearing most often.

The seventh chapter of the Manual is a compendium of lists. It opens with nine lists of what binds the mind: the taints, the floods, the bonds, the bodily ties, the clingings, the hindrances, the latent tendencies, the fetters and the defilements (VII §§3–12). Most come from the suttas, though the taint of views and the sixth hindrance, ignorance, are not in the suttas’ usual counts. Read as they come, they make a dense thicket of names. The Abhidhamma’s contribution is to ask, of each item, which mental factor it really is.

The answer clears the thicket. Dozens of names come down to only fourteen factors, the unwholesome factors of the fifty-two (VII §§13–14).

Many names, few factors

Take the four taints, āsavaāsava · ogha · yoga taints, floods, bonds: sense desire, desire for existence, views, ignorance: sensual desire, attachment to existence, wrong views and ignorance. The first two are greed; the third is wrong view; the fourth is delusion. Three factors, four names. The four clingings, upādānaupādāna · clinging intense grasping: at sense pleasures, views, rites, a self; link 9 of the wheel, are greed once and wrong view three times. The six hindrances (six in the Dhammasaṅgaṇī’s count; the suttas have five, without ignorance) are eight factors: greed, hatred, sloth and torpor, restlessness and worry, doubt, and delusion as ignorance.

Count across all nine groups and a pattern appears. Greed is in every one of the nine groups (Table 7.1), in seventeen items if both lists of fetters are counted, under sixteen different names. Wrong view is in eight groups, delusion in seven. A handful of factors does almost all the binding.The atlas counts by factor: each circle on the plate gives the number of items in a list that are, ultimately, that factor, counting both lists of fetters.

Why keep the names, then? Not only because each has its own image. The commentaries give them different reach: the taint of sensual desire is lust for the five sense pleasures and ends at non-returning, while covetousness as a bodily tie is every kind of greed and ends only with arahantship (Atthasālinī on Dhs §§1102, 1140; Vism XXII §830). Each list, its images, what a latent tendency is, and which path ends which name are taken up in Taints, floods, bonds, ties.

Categories that are conditions

The chapter’s second section, its mixed categories (lists that hold wholesome, unwholesome and neutral factors together), holds a further surprise. Six of them are six of the twenty-four conditions. The six roots work as root condition. The jhāna factors work as jhāna condition. The path factors, the faculties, the predominants and the nutriments each have a condition of the same name. Only the nine powers have no condition of their own. Five of them (faith, energy, mindfulness, concentration and wisdom) are also counted as faculties; the other four are shame, fear of wrongdoing and their two opposites. All nine are called powers because their opposites cannot shake them (Guide to VII §19).

So the lists of chapter VII and the web of chapter VIII describe the same factors from two sides: what they are, and how they help.

The requisites

The chapter ends with the thirty-seven requisites of enlightenment: the foundations of mindfulness, the right efforts, the bases of success, the faculties and powers, the factors of awakening, and the eightfold path. Reduced the same way, thirty-seven items are fourteen states (a different fourteen from the defilements). Energy appears nine times, mindfulness eight, wisdom five, concentration four, faith twice. The path, too, is a few qualities under many names, each name marking a stage or a use. The seven sets and their fourteen states are drawn out in Thirty-seven requisites, fourteen states.

Reflect and discuss

Opening question

Greed turns up in every one of the nine groups of defilements, under sixteen different names. Why might the tradition have kept so many names for one factor?

One way to see it, after you have tried

One answer: the names are not synonyms. Each has its own image, and in the commentaries each has its own reach: the taint of sensual desire is lust for the five sense pleasures and ends at non-returning, while the tie of covetousness is every kind of greed and ends only with arahantship. Reduced to a factor, the two are the same; kept as names, they say how far each reaches and which path ends it.
