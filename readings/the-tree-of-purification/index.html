The tree of purification

The path of insight pictured as a tree: the soil, the roots, a trunk of seven purifications, the knowledges that climb it, and the single moment at its crown.

About 9 minutesManual IX §§22–44 (insight: purifications, knowledges, emancipations, individuals, attainments)

In brief

  • Buddhaghosa's Visuddhimagga pictures the path of insight as soil, roots and trunk: what is to be understood is the soil, virtue and concentration the roots, wisdom the trunk; we draw it as a tree.
  • Seven purifications, from a sutta's relay of chariots, frame the path; a critical one is seeing that attachment to bright experiences in meditation is not the path.
  • Ten insight knowledges climb the trunk, from comprehension and rise and fall, through dissolution and disenchantment, to equanimity about formations and, last, conformity.
  • Insight breaks through by whichever mark it dwelt on most, impermanence, suffering or not-self, but all three doors open onto the same Nibbāna.
  • At the crown a single moment of the path cuts certain bonds, called fetters; four such paths in turn cut all ten.
  • A path adds nothing: it removes the conditions for certain states of mind ever arising again.

The Visuddhimagga, Buddhaghosa’s path of purification, pictures the path of insight as something that grows. The things to be understood, the aggregates, bases, elements, faculties, truths and dependent origination, are the soil. Purification of virtue and purification of mind are the roots. The five purifications of wisdom are the trunk (Vism XIV §430). The Visuddhimagga stops there; the tree, and liberation at its crown, are this site’s way of drawing the image out.

The image is useful because it is organic. Nothing on the trunk can grow without the roots, and nothing at the crown without the trunk.

Seven purifications

The scheme of seven purifications comes from a sutta in which Sāriputta and Puṇṇa compare the path to a relay of seven chariots: each takes the traveller only as far as the next, and the last arrives (MN 24). The commentaries make it the frame for insight.

Purification of virtue is restraint of body and speech. Purification of mind is concentration, access or absorption, the work of calm. For one who goes by pure insight, without calm first, the Guide reckons momentary concentration, as steady as access, in its place (Guide to IX §29); the sub-commentary on the Visuddhimagga already pairs pure insight with momentary concentration; Ten knowledges, one by one has more. These two are the roots; the five that follow are the trunk.

Purification of view is seeing mind and matter, nāmanāma · mind mind: citta with its factors, which “bend” toward objects; the word also means “name” and rūpa, as they are, with no being or person behind them. Purification by overcoming doubt is seeing their conditions, in the present and in the past and future. Purification by knowledge and vision of what is and is not the path is the critical one: the meditator, seeing rise and fall, meets brilliant experiences, light, rapture, tranquillity, strong faith, and must recognise that attachment to them is not the path.noteThe Visuddhimagga lists ten such “imperfections of insight”. They are not bad in themselves; they are dangerous because they are so attractive that they stop the work.

Purification by knowledge and vision of the way is the run of insight knowledges below, from rise and fall to conformity. Purification by knowledge and vision is the path itself.

The knowledges

The Manual lists ten insight knowledges along the trunk. Comprehension sees dhammas in terms of the three marks, tilakkhaṇatilakkhaṇa · the three marks impermanence, suffering and non-self: the marks that insight sees. Knowledge of rise and fall sees each one arising and ceasing. Knowledge of dissolution sees only the ceasing. Then knowledges of fearfulness, of danger and of disenchantment: what dissolves so constantly cannot be a refuge. Then desire for deliverance, reflection, and equanimity toward formations, a balanced, unshakeable seeing of everything conditioned. Last comes conformity, which adapts the mind to the path. Ten knowledges, one by one takes each in turn, and notes that the Paṭisambhidāmagga counts fear, danger and disenchantment as one knowledge, and the next three as one.

Three doors

Insight that is about to break through dwells on one mark more than the others, and that mark is the door it enters by. Dwelling on impermanence, it enters by the signless. Dwelling on suffering, by the desireless. Dwelling on not-self, by the void, emptiness. The doors differ; what lies beyond them does not.

The crown

At the top of the tree a single process carries the change. After conformity comes change-of-lineage, gotrabhūgotrabhū · change-of-lineage the javana that leaves the sense-sphere lineage for jhāna, or turns to Nibbāna, the first citta in the process to take Nibbāna as its object, the moment that passes from the worldling’s lineage to the noble one’s. Before the three higher paths this moment is called cleansing, vodāna, since the meditator is already noble (Guide to IX §34). Then the path citta, maggamagga · path (citta) a supramundane citta that arises once, cutting defilements, with Nibbāna as object, for one moment only, with the factors of the noble eightfold path all present together; it cuts the fetters it is able to cut. Then two or three moments of fruition, phalaphala · fruition the supramundane resultant citta that follows its path, also taking Nibbāna as object, enjoying the same object: three in one of keen faculties, whose process skips the preparation moment, two in others (Guide to IV §22). Then the life-continuum resumes, and the person is different for good.

Four paths cut ten fetters, saṃyojanasaṃyojana · fetter one of ten defilements that bind to rebirth; the four paths cut them. Stream-entry cuts three: identity view, doubt, and clinging to rules and rites. Once-returning ends the gross forms of sensual desire and ill will, leaving only subtle ones (Paṭisambhidāmagga ps1.1:349; Vism XXII §830); in the Manual’s words, lust, hatred and delusion are attenuated (IX §39). Non-returning cuts sensual desire and ill will. Arahantship cuts the last five: desire for fine-material and immaterial existence, conceit, restlessness and ignorance. Each of the four kinds of person this makes is followed in Four noble persons.

One moment is enough because of what a path does. It does not add anything. It removes the conditions for certain cittas ever arising again, and from then on they simply do not come.

The ideas in this reading

  • Insight vipassanāThe knowledges that see mind and matter rise and fall.
  • Path and fruition four pathsThe four moments of awakening that cut defilements for good, each followed by its fruition.
  • Nibbāna unconditionedThe one unconditioned reality, known in the moments of awakening called path and fruition.

Reflect and discuss

Opening question

Insight is said to enter liberation by one of three doors, set by whichever mark it dwelt on most: impermanence, suffering or not-self. Which of the three is most alive for you, and what keeps the others at a distance?

One way to see it, after you have tried

For many people impermanence is the easiest to see and not-self the hardest to accept. The teaching is that the three are aspects of one seeing: whatever is impermanent is unsatisfactory, and whatever is impermanent and unsatisfactory is not fit to be regarded as self (SN 22.59). Any one door opens onto the same Nibbāna.
