The wheel across three lives

Twelve links, three periods, twenty modes and three rounds: how the Abhidhamma reads dependent origination, and where the wheel can be stopped.

About 8 minutesManual VIII §§2–10 (dependent origination: formula, periods, factors, modes, connections, groups, rounds, roots)

In brief

  • Dependent origination (paṭicca-samuppāda) is a chain of twelve links, each conditioning the next, that explains how suffering arises, continues and ends.
  • The Abhidhamma's commentaries read the twelve across three lives: past causes produce present results, and present causes produce future results.
  • Filled in, the chain gives twenty modes: five past causes, five present results, five present causes and five future results.
  • Defilements give rise to kamma, kamma to results, and results met with ignorance and craving to new defilements, so no beginning of the wheel can be found.
  • A wheel of conditions is held together only by its links, so one failing link stops it; insight teachers point to the joint between feeling and craving, and the paths cut craving for good.

“When this exists, that comes to be; with the arising of this, that arises.” The suttas state the principle in a sentence, and then apply it as a chain of twelve links: paṭicca-samuppādapaṭicca-samuppāda · dependent origination the twelve links by which suffering arises, and by whose ending it ceases, dependent origination. It explains how suffering arises and continues, and how it ends.

The twelve are these, each conditioning the next:

  1. Ignorance: not knowing the four noble truths, in the sutta explanation (Guide to VIII §3)
  2. Formations: volitions, wholesome and unwholesome
  3. Consciousness
  4. Mentality-materiality: mind and matter, the nāmarūpa of Mind and matter together
  5. The six sense bases
  6. Contact
  7. Feeling
  8. Craving
  9. Clinging
  10. Becoming
  11. Birth
  12. Ageing and death, with sorrow, lamentation, pain, grief and despair

Three lives

The Abhidhamma’s commentaries read the twelve across three lives.This is the commentarial reading, systematised in the Visuddhimagga and the Manual. The suttas themselves give several variants, and some modern teachers read the chain within a single life or a single moment. The Vibhaṅga itself, in its Abhidhamma section, applies the whole series to one moment of citta. Avijjāavijjā · ignorance not knowing the four noble truths; delusion as a root of the wheel, ignorance, and the formations it conditions belong to the past: they are the kamma of a previous life. Consciousness, mentality-materiality, the six bases, contact and feeling belong to the present as results: the rebirth-linking consciousness and the life it begins. Craving, clinging and becoming belong to the present as causes: the defilements and the kamma of this life. Birth and ageing-and-death belong to the future.

So the twelve links describe two turns of a wheel: past causes producing present results, and present causes producing future results.

Twenty modes

The commentaries then notice something about this arrangement. Wherever ignorance and formations are present, craving and clinging are present too, since one does not occur without the others. Becoming has two sides: kamma-becoming, the kamma that leads to a new existence, and rebirth-becoming, the existence it leads to. Wherever kamma-becoming is, ignorance and formations are. So each set of causes is really five. The results are five too: consciousness, mentality-materiality, the sense bases, contact and feeling; and birth and ageing-and-death in the future are those same five seen arising and decaying.

That gives twenty “modes”: five past causes, five present results, five present causes, five future results. The twelve links are a sketch; the twenty modes fill it in.

Three rounds, two roots

The links can also be sorted into three rounds, vaṭṭavaṭṭa · round one of three cycles turning in the wheel: defilements, kamma, results. The round of defilements is ignorance, craving and clinging. The round of kamma is formations and kamma-becoming. The round of results is everything else. Defilements give rise to kamma, kamma gives rise to results, and results, met with ignorance and craving, give rise to new defilements.

And the wheel has two roots. Ignorance is the root that runs from the past into the present, and its run ends in feeling; craving, taṇhātaṇhā · craving greed as thirst: for sense objects, for existence or for non-existence; a root, is the root that runs from the present into the future, and its run ends in ageing and death (VIII §9). Neither is a first cause. The Manual adds that ignorance itself arises from the taints (VIII §10; the Guide traces this to MN 9), so the wheel has no beginning that can be found.

Where it can be stopped

A wheel with no beginning sounds hopeless. The teaching is the opposite. A wheel of conditions is held together only by its links, and any one link that fails stops the whole.

Teachers often point to the joint between feeling and craving. Feeling is a result: as long as there is a body and a mind, there will be pleasant, painful and neutral feeling. Craving is not a result. It is a defilement arising in the javanas, where new kamma is made, and it can fail to arise. Insight teachers stress this joint: where feeling is seen clearly, as feeling, craving does not get its hold. The commentaries call this kind of release abandoning by the opposite factor (tadaṅga-pahāna): insight keeps a defilement away only while it lasts (Vism XXII §849, after the Paṭisambhidāmagga). Craving, with ignorance one of the wheel’s two roots (VIII §9), is cut for good only by the paths, the last of it by the path of arahantship (IX §38–41).

That is why the twelve links are taught at all. They are not a cosmology to be admired. They are a diagram of a machine, drawn so that you can see where to put your hand.

Reflect and discuss

Opening question

The wheel has no first link. Ignorance conditions formations, but ignorance is itself conditioned. Does a wheel without a beginning make liberation harder to imagine, or easier?

One way to see it, after you have tried

Easier, perhaps. A wheel with a first cause would need that cause undone. A wheel of conditions needs only one link to fail. The tradition points to the link between feeling and craving: feeling will keep arising as long as there is a body, but craving need not follow it.
