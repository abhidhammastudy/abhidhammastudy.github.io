Two moments, side by side

The same ripe mango, seen twice: once wanted, once given away. Identical up to determining, the two processes part at the javanas, and everything downstream parts with them.

About 5 minutes

In brief

  • The same ripe mango is seen twice, once wanted and once given to a hungry friend, and the two moments are compared step by step.
  • For the first eight moments the two run alike: seeing and the steps around it are results of past kamma or merely functional, and involve no choice.
  • They part at the javanas, the moments in which the mind acts: one run is rooted in greed, the other in non-greed and non-hatred, as generosity.
  • The feeling can be joy in both, so joy does not tell you which kind of moment you are in; the roots decide.
  • From the javanas on everything differs, the kamma made and what it conditions, so the practice is to watch those seven moments, not to stop seeing or enjoying.

The previous reading looked at one moment through many lenses. This one looks at two moments through one: the same mango, seen twice. In the first moment the man wants it. In the second, a friend is hungry, and he gives it away.

Contrast is how the categories become clear. Set the two processes side by side, moment by moment, and look for where they differ.

The same, up to determining

For eight moments the two run alike. The same mango strikes the same eye. The life-continuum vibrates and is arrested in the same way. Five-door adverting turns toward it; eye-consciousness sees it; receiving, investigating and determining do their work. Every one of these is a result or merely functional. None of them is a choice. The Atthasālinī places the turn of attention, wise or unwise, at adverting and determining; what it turns into first shows in the javanas.

Seeing is never the problem.

Where they part

At the javanas the two processes part. In the first, the javanas are rooted in greed: lobhalobha · greed grasping at an object; roots eight cittas; as craving, a root of the wheel with joy, the four unwholesome universals with it, delusion hiding what the mango is. In the second, they are rooted in non-greed and non-hatred, perhaps with understanding: generosity with joy, faith, mindfulness, and the nineteen beautiful factors present in every beautiful citta.

The feeling can be the same in both: joy. That is one of the lessons of the contrast. Joy goes with opposite qualities. The roots decide what the moment is.The figure below runs both processes through the lenses of One moment, every lens, row by row, and marks where they differ. Most rows change only from the javanas on.

Everything downstream

From the javanas on, everything differs. The kamma made is unwholesome in one case and wholesome in the other. What it conditions is different: one strengthens a habit of wanting, the other a habit of giving. The link from feeling to craving is made in one process and not in the other. Even the matter produced differs: the hand that reaches to take and the hand that reaches to give are moved by different cittas, in the mind-door processes that follow.

What the contrast teaches

Three things stand out. Seeing is the same in both; the first eight moments are given. Feeling is the same in both; joy does not tell you which kind of moment you are in. And kamma is made only where the two processes differ, in seven moments of javana.

That is much of the Abhidhamma’s practical teaching in miniature. It does not ask you to stop seeing mangoes, or to stop enjoying them. It points to the seven moments where the difference is made, and asks you to watch them.

Reflect and discuss

Opening question

Take one ordinary moment of liking from today and imagine its wholesome twin. Which parts of the experience would change, and which would stay exactly the same?

One way to see it, after you have tried

The sight, the sound, the pleasant feeling would stay the same; they are results and were given. What would change is the root of the javanas, the factors that come with it, the kamma made, and what that kamma conditions later. Usually the outward act changes least of all. Most of the difference is inside.
