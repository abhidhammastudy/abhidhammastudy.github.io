What each citta does

From what a citta is to what it does: fourteen functions, six doors, six bases, and the few cittas that do the most work.

About 6 minutesManual III §§5–7 (roots), §§8–11 (functions), §§12–15 (doors), §§16–19 (objects), §§20–22 (bases)

In brief

  • The Manual's third chapter sorts the eighty-nine kinds of consciousness (citta) by what they do, and counts fourteen functions.
  • Three functions lie outside any process, rebirth-linking, the life-continuum and death; the rest are the stations of a process, from adverting to registration.
  • Most cittas have one job only, but a few are versatile: one kind of citta can open a life, carry it through every pause, and close it.
  • Process cittas meet their object through one of six doors; in a being with a body, every citta stands on a sense organ or the heart-base.
  • Of the fourteen functions only javana, the run of moments in which the mind acts, makes kamma; every other is a result or merely functional.
  • So in a whole life of processes, the room for doing something new is a single station, seven moments long.

A map of the eighty-nine sorted the cittas by what they are. The third chapter of the Manual sorts them by what they do. It is the bridge from the map to the moments of a process: before you can follow a sight through seventeen moments, you need to know which cittas can fill each slot.

Fourteen jobs

The Manual counts fourteen functions, kiccakicca · function (of a citta) one of fourteen jobs a citta can do, such as seeing or javana. Three happen outside any process: rebirth-linking, the life-continuum, and death. The rest are the stations of a process: adverting; seeing, hearing, smelling, tasting and touching; receiving; investigating; determining; javana, the run of moments in which the mind acts; and registration, which takes up the same object once more after the javanas.

Most cittas have one job only. Sixty-eight of the eighty-nine do just one thing: seeing only sees; a greed-rooted citta only ever runs as javana. A few are versatile. The two equanimous investigating cittas each do five jobs: they investigate and register in ordinary processes, and in beings reborn with a rootless citta, in the lower worlds or with a severe impairment, they also serve as rebirth-linking, life-continuum and death.noteThe eight great resultant cittas do four jobs each: registration, rebirth-linking, life-continuum and death for human beings and gods of the sense sphere. The nine fine-material and immaterial resultants do three.

So a single kind of citta can open a life, carry it through every pause, and close it.

Six doors

A dvāradvāra · door the channel by which a process meets its object: five senses and mind, door, is where a process meets its object. Five are the sensitive matter of the senses. The sixth, the mind door, is the life-continuum itself, at the moment it is disturbed by an object.

Forty-six kinds of citta can arise through the eye door; sixty-seven through the mind door. Nineteen are “door-freed” when they serve as rebirth-linking, life-continuum and death: in those roles they arise outside any process and through no door. Ten of them also work inside processes, as investigating or registration; the other nine never do (III §§13–14). The counts overlap, because one citta can arise through more than one door.

Six bases

Every citta in a being with a body stands on a physical base, a vatthuvatthu · physical base the matter a citta arises from: a sense organ or the heart-base. The ten sense consciousnesses, a pair for each sense, stand on their own sense organ: five bases. Every other citta stands on the sixth, the heart-base, which the commentaries name as the physical support of mind. In the immaterial planes there is no matter and so no base; cittas there arise without one.

The base is not the same as the door. Seeing stands on the eye as its base, and the eye is also its door. But the javanas of that process arise through the eye door while standing on the heart-base.

Why this chapter matters

Read as a table, the chapter is dry. Read as a description of a mind, it says something precise about freedom. Of the fourteen functions, only one, javana, makes kamma. Every other function is either a result of past kamma or merely functional. A process has seventeen moments, a life has millions of processes, and in all of it the room for doing something new is a single station, seven moments long.

The same chapter sorts the cittas three more ways, by feeling, by roots and by object; those are in Feeling, roots and objects.

Reflect and discuss

Opening question

The same kind of citta that links a life to the last one also runs as the life-continuum and ends that life as death. What does it mean that a life begins, idles and ends in the same kind of mind?

One way to see it, after you have tried

It means a life has a background tone set at its start by one kamma, and that this tone does not change however much happens in the foreground. Character can change through the javanas of a lifetime, but the life-continuum stays what it was. One lesson to draw from this: the foreground is where change is possible.
