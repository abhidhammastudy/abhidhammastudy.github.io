Which factors meet which cittas

One table read two ways: across, the cittas a factor joins; down, the factors a citta takes. How the Abhidhamma assembles a moment of mind.

About 5 minutes

In brief

  • The Manual sets the fifty-two mental factors against the eighty-nine kinds of consciousness (citta) in one table, and reads it two ways.
  • Read across, a row shows which cittas a factor joins: the seven universals join every one, while doubt joins only one.
  • Some factors are unfixed: compassion joins only when a suffering being is the object; abstinences join great wholesome cittas only when one actually refrains.
  • Read down, a column is a recipe: seeing takes only the seven universals, an unwholesome citta at most twenty-one, a great wholesome one up to thirty-four at once.
  • The fourteen unwholesome factors and the twenty-five beautiful ones never arise in the same citta.
  • Every moment of mind is assembled anew from a fixed menu, by rules, so naming the kind of citta tells you everything that came with it.

The second chapter of the Manual asks two questions of one table. Put the fifty-two factors down the side and the eighty-nine cittas across the top. Read across a row, and you have the method of association: which cittas does this factor join? Read down a column, and you have the method of combination: which factors does this citta take?

It is the Abhidhamma’s way of assembling a moment of mind from its parts.

Reading across

Some rows are full. The seven universals appear in every column. Some rows are nearly empty: doubt appears in one citta only, the delusion-rooted citta that bears its name. Envy, avarice and worry share the two hate-rooted cittas, but never together, since each has a different object. Compassion and appreciative joy appear in twenty-eight cittas, and only occasionally, when a being in suffering or in success is actually the object.The Manual calls such factors “unfixed”: they may be present in a citta, but are not always. Eleven factors are unfixed in this way.

The three abstinences behave the same way. Right speech, right action and right livelihood join the great wholesome cittas only at the moment of actually refraining from a wrong. In path and fruition, all three are present together.

Reading down

A column is a recipe. The simplest cittas, the sense consciousnesses, take only the seven universals: seeing is contact, feeling, perception, volition, one-pointedness, life and attention, and nothing else. The first greed-rooted citta takes nineteen: the seven universals and six occasionals, the four unwholesome factors present in every unwholesome citta, then greed and wrong view. An unwholesome citta takes at most twenty-one factors at once. A great wholesome citta (one of the eight wholesome cittas of the sense sphere) with knowledge can count thirty-eight, though not all at once: at most thirty-four arise together, because the abstinences and the illimitables exclude one another.

Two families that never meet

The table has one rule that is visible at a glance. The fourteen unwholesome factors and the twenty-five beautiful ones never fall in the same column.

What the table is for

It is tempting to treat this chapter as a reference table and move on. But it carries a picture of mind that is worth absorbing slowly. Every moment is assembled anew, from a fixed menu, according to rules. Nothing carries over from one column to the next except by condition. Name the kind of citta, and the table tells you everything that came with it.

The ideas in this reading

  • Cetasika mental factorsThe mental factors that colour each citta.
  • Citta consciousnessConsciousness: one moment of knowing an object. The Abhidhamma counts 89 kinds.

Reflect and discuss

Opening question

An unwholesome citta takes at most twenty-one factors at once; a great wholesome one up to thirty-four. Is a good moment richer than a bad one, or only busier?

One way to see it, after you have tried

The tradition would say richer, because the additional factors are qualities, not noise: faith, mindfulness, tranquillity, lightness, malleability. A wholesome mind has more working parts and they all pull in one direction. An unwholesome mind is simpler and more unstable, held together by delusion and restlessness.
