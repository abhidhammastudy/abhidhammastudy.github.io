Who is reborn where

Nineteen cittas can begin a life, and each belongs to its own kind of world. Where a being can go next depends on where it dies and with what, and for the noble ones there is no going down.

About 8 minutes

In brief

  • Nineteen kinds of consciousness (citta) can begin a life, each belonging to its own kind of world; one rebirth, of the non-percipient beings, has none.
  • The citta that links a new life is the same that runs all life long as the life-continuum and finally ends it at death.
  • The woeful planes are entered by one citta, the human world and sense heavens by nine, and the fine-material and immaterial worlds by their own jhāna results.
  • An ordinary person's next birth can fall almost anywhere, since a meditative absorption (jhāna) can raise a being high and its fading can let it fall.
  • A noble disciple, who has reached at least the first path, dies with a citta rooted in non-greed, non-hatred and wisdom, and is never reborn lower.
  • So, on this reading, the path, not a high rebirth, is the only real safety; an arahant is not reborn at all.

Kamma, death and rebirth describes the last process of a life and the first moment of the next. Thirty-one planes describes the worlds. This reading joins them. It asks two questions: which citta can begin a life in which world, and, after a death in one world, where the next birth can fall. The answers show why, on this reading, the path, not a high rebirth, is the only real safety.

Four kinds of beginning

The Manual counts four kinds of paṭisandhipaṭisandhi · rebirth-linking the first citta of a life: a resultant that links it to the last, rebirth-linking (V §9). The citta that links a new life is the same citta that then runs all life long as the life-continuum and finally ends it as the death citta: one type, with one object, for the whole of a life (V §17).

  • In the woeful planes, one citta does it: the investigating citta with equanimity, born of unwholesome kamma (§10). Every hell-being, animal, ghost and asura begins this way.
  • In the human world and the sense heavens, nine cittas (§11). The eight great resultants, two- or three-rooted, are the usual ones. The ninth, the investigating citta born of wholesome kamma, is rootless, and it begins the lives of those born with a defect already in the kamma that made them: those blind, deaf or mentally impaired from conception, and some minor earth-deities and a class of “fallen” asuras distinct from the asuras of the woeful planes.noteThe Guide is careful: blindness from an accident in the womb is a different matter, and can happen to a being with a two- or three-rooted rebirth. Only a defect built into the generating kamma marks a rootless rebirth.
  • In the fine-material worlds, six modes (§13): the five fine-material resultants, each in the worlds of its jhāna, and, for the non-percipient beings, no citta at all, only matter.
  • In the immaterial worlds, four cittas (§15): each immaterial resultant in its own plane.

That makes nineteen rebirth cittas, and one rebirth with none.

Where a worldling can go

The Manual closes chapter V with a verse on the order of death and rebirth (V §40), and the Guide and Table 5.7 work it out world by world. For an ordinary person, a worldling (the roots counted here are the three wholesome ones: non-greed, non-hatred and non-delusion, or wisdom):

  • Dying in a woeful plane, or dying in the sense sphere with a rootless or two-rooted citta, one is reborn only in the sense sphere, woeful or happy.
  • Dying in the sense sphere with three roots, one can be reborn anywhere, since a three-rooted being can make any kind of kamma, including a jhāna.
  • Dying in a fine-material world, one is never reborn without roots: into the sense sphere with two or three roots, or into a fine-material or immaterial world by a jhāna one still has. The non-percipient beings return to the sense sphere.
  • Dying in an immaterial world, one is reborn in the same plane or a higher one, if the jhāna is kept; if it is lost, in the sense sphere, with three roots, by the force of access concentration (Guide to V §40).

Never lower

For noble disciples, those who have reached at least the first path, the rule is simpler and stricter (Guide to V §40). A “trainee” is a noble disciple short of arahantship. A noble disciple always dies with a three-rooted citta, since the path cannot be reached without three roots, and is never reborn into a lower world or with a lower citta. A trainee who dies in the sense sphere goes to the sense sphere or higher, never to a woeful plane; one who dies in a world of form or of formlessness goes to the same world or a higher one. The Pure Abodes receive only non-returners, who climb from one to the next and reach arahantship there at the latest. An arahant is not reborn at all.

So the grid has a shape. For worldlings it spreads in every direction, because a jhāna can raise a being high and its fading can let it fall. For the noble it only rises.

Reflect and discuss

Opening question

A being in the highest heaven of form can be reborn as a human, but a stream-enterer in the human world can never be reborn as an animal. What, in the Abhidhamma's terms, makes the difference?

One way to see it, after you have tried

The Brahmā's rebirth was won by a jhāna, which is a temporary attainment: when its force is spent, whatever other kamma the being carries can take over, and the being dies with whatever it has. The stream-enterer has seen something that cannot be unseen. The path has removed the defilements strong enough to lead to a woeful birth, and every noble disciple dies with a three-rooted citta. Height of world is not the same as security; the path is.
