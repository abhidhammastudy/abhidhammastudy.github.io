Ānanda lies down

The First Council was to begin the next day, and Ānanda, who would recite the suttas, was not yet an arahant. He spent the night in mindfulness of the body. Near dawn he decided to lie down, and as he leaned back, before his head reached the pillow and after his feet had left the ground, his mind was freed.

Where
Rājagaha, on the night before the First Council
Who
  • Ānanda, the Buddha's attendant, still a trainee when the Buddha died
  • Mahā Kassapa, the senior monk who called the council
Source
Vinaya, Cūḷavagga XI

The story

After the Buddha's death Mahā Kassapa chose the monks who would recite the teaching at Rājagaha: four hundred and ninety-nine arahants, and Ānanda, who had heard more of the Buddha's words than anyone but was still a trainee. He could not be left out, and he could not be counted with the others as he was.

On the night before the council, the Vinaya says, Ānanda spent much of the night mindful of the body. The commentaries add that he walked up and down his walking path, striving hard, and remembered that the Buddha had told him in his last days that he would soon be free. Seeing no result, he saw that he had pushed too hard, so that energy was tipping into restlessness, and he resolved to balance it.

Towards dawn he went into his dwelling to lie down. As he inclined his body, with his feet off the ground and his head not yet on the pillow, his mind was freed from the taints without clinging. The commentaries treasure the detail: he became an arahant in none of the four postures, neither walking, standing, sitting nor lying.

In the morning he took his seat in the assembly. The commentaries tell that he came by his power, rising up through the earth or through the air, so that the others would know without being told. Asked by Mahā Kassapa where each discourse was spoken and to whom, he recited the suttas, beginning “Thus have I heard”, after Upāli had recited the Discipline.

A reading · a study aid, not the tradition's own reading

The path process in “Other processes” runs at the mind door and needs no posture at all: preparation, access, conformity, cleansing (the name change-of-lineage takes for the higher paths), then path and fruition, in the space between standing and lying down.

Read with: Other processes · The tree of purification

The dhammas it shows

  • mindfulness sati: a whole night mindful of the body
  • energy viriya: energy pushed too hard, then balanced
  • path magga: the arahant path cuts the last fetters in one process

Where it is told

  • Vinaya, Cūḷavagga XI (the council of the five hundred)
  • Sumaṅgalavilāsinī, introduction (the commentary on the council)
  • DN 16, the Buddha's promise to Ānanda

The Vinaya tells the moment of release briefly; the over-striving, the phrase “in none of the four postures” and his arrival by psychic power belong to the commentaries.
