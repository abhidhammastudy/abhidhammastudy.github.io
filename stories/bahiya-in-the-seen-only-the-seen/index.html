Bāhiya: in the seen, only the seen

Bāhiya stopped the Buddha on his alms round, begging to be taught. The Buddha said: “In the seen there will be only the seen, in the heard only the heard, in the sensed only the sensed, in the cognized only the cognized.” Bāhiya became an arahant there and then, and was killed by a cow soon after.

Where
Suppāraka on the western coast, then the streets of Sāvatthī
Who
  • Bāhiya Dārucīriya, “Bāhiya of the bark cloth”, honoured at Suppāraka as an arahant
  • A deva, a former kinsman; in the commentary, a companion in the holy life long ago
  • The Buddha, on his alms round
Source
Ud 1.10

The story

Bāhiya lived at Suppāraka by the sea, dressed in bark, and people honoured him as an arahant. The commentary says he had been shipwrecked and came ashore naked, covered himself in bark, and was taken for a holy man. One day the thought came to him: am I one of those who are arahants? A deva who had once been his kinsman came to him and said: you are not, and you are not even on the way. There is one in Sāvatthī who is.

Bāhiya set out at once, and travelled, the commentary says, the whole way in one night. At Jetavana he was told the Buddha had gone into the city for alms. He hurried after him, fell at his feet in the street and begged to be taught. “It is not the time,” said the Buddha; “I am on alms round.” Bāhiya asked a second time, and a third: “It is hard to know how long the Blessed One will live, or how long I will.”

Then the Buddha taught him, standing there: “Bāhiya, train yourself thus: in the seen there will be only the seen, in the heard only the heard, in the sensed only the sensed, in the cognized only the cognized. When that is so, you will not be ‘with that’; when you are not ‘with that’, you will not be ‘in that’; then you will be neither here nor beyond nor between the two. This is the end of suffering.”

Bāhiya's mind was freed on the spot. Soon after the Buddha left, a cow with a young calf attacked and killed him. Returning from his round, the Buddha had the monks cremate the body and build a mound for it, and declared that Bāhiya had attained final Nibbāna. He later named him foremost among those who understand quickly.

A reading · a study aid, not the tradition's own reading

“The mango and the moment” shows where the instruction bites, and the Udāna commentary reads it the same way: let the javanas stay at the measure of eye-consciousness, which neither lusts, hates nor is deluded. Seeing itself, at moment 5, is a result and makes no kamma. Liking, disliking and “I” enter only in the javanas.

Read with: A chariot of parts · The mango and the moment

The dhammas it shows

  • visible form vaṇṇa: in the seen, only the seen
  • sound sadda: in the heard, only the heard
  • object ārammaṇa: the bare object, before javanas add liking or self

Where it is told

  • Ud 1.10 Bāhiya Sutta
  • Udāna commentary on Ud 1.10, CS ¶10 (the shipwreck, the one-night journey)
  • AN 1.216, foremost among those with swift insight
  • SN 35.95, the same teaching given to Mālukyaputta

The same short instruction appears in SN 35.95, given to the old monk Mālukyaputta, who asks for a teaching in brief and is given this one, with verses that unpack it.
