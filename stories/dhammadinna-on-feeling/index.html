Dhammadinnā on feeling

Questioned by the layman Visākha, the nun Dhammadinnā explained that lust lies latent in pleasant feeling, aversion in painful feeling and ignorance in neutral feeling. The Buddha said he would have answered in the same way.

Where
The Bamboo Grove at Rājagaha
Who
  • Dhammadinnā, a nun, formerly Visākha's wife
  • Visākha, a lay follower of Rājagaha, already a non-returner
  • The Buddha, who confirmed her answers
Source
MN 44

The story

The commentary tells the background. Visākha, a merchant of Rājagaha, heard the Buddha teach and in time became a non-returner. When he came home he no longer smiled at his wife as before. When she learned why, she asked to go forth, and soon became an arahant. Then Visākha came to put questions to her.

He asked her about identity: what the Buddha calls “personality”, how identity view arises and how it ends; about the noble path and the three trainings; about formations of body, speech and mind, and the attainment of cessation. Then he asked about feeling: pleasant, painful and neutral, and what lies latent in each.

Lust lies latent in pleasant feeling, she said, aversion in painful feeling, ignorance in neutral feeling; but not in every pleasant feeling. The pleasure of the first jhāna abandons lust; the pain of longing for liberation, a grief of the mind, abandons aversion; the neutral feeling of the fourth jhāna abandons ignorance.

He pressed on: what is the counterpart of pleasant feeling? of neutral feeling? of ignorance? of liberation? And what is the counterpart of Nibbāna? “You have gone too far, friend Visākha,” she said. “The holy life is lived with Nibbāna as its culmination.” Visākha reported all this to the Buddha, who said: “The nun Dhammadinnā is wise. If you had asked me, I would have answered in the same way.”

A reading · a study aid, not the tradition's own reading

This joins the colours of feeling in “A map of the eighty-nine” to the wheel in “The wheel across three lives”: feeling (link 7) is where craving (link 8) takes hold.

The dhammas it shows

  • feeling vedanā: pleasant, painful and neutral feeling, examined one by one
  • greed lobha: lust lies latent in pleasant feeling
  • hatred dosa: aversion lies latent in painful feeling
  • delusion moha: ignorance lies latent in neutral feeling

Where it is told

  • MN 44 Cūḷavedalla Sutta
  • Therīgāthā 1.12, her verse
  • AN 1.239, the list of foremost disciples: Dhammadinnā, foremost among nuns who teach the Dhamma

The Buddha's endorsement makes her answers the Buddha's word. MN 44 is one of the suttas the Abhidhamma tradition draws on for its account of feeling and the latent tendencies.
