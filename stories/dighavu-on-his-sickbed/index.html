Dīghāvu on his sickbed

The lay disciple Dīghāvu lay gravely ill, his pain growing, and the Buddha came to his house. Finding that he already had the faith and virtue of a stream-enterer, the Buddha gave him more to do: see all formations as impermanent, the impermanent as suffering, suffering as not self, and keep in mind giving up, fading and ceasing. Dīghāvu died soon after, and the Buddha declared him a non-returner.

Where
Dīghāvu's house in Rājagaha, while the Buddha was staying at the Bamboo Grove
Who
  • Dīghāvu, a lay disciple, gravely ill
  • Jotika, his father, a householder
  • The Buddha, who came to the house at Dīghāvu's request
Source
SN 55.3

The story

Dīghāvu was sick, in pain, gravely ill. He asked his father Jotika to go to the Buddha, bow at his feet in his son's name, and ask him to come to the house out of compassion. The Buddha agreed in silence, and came, and sat down by him. I hope you are bearing up, Dīghāvu, he said; I hope the pain is going down and not up. It is not, sir, said Dīghāvu. The pain is terrible and growing.

Then train like this, said the Buddha: I will have unwavering confidence in the Buddha, in the Dhamma, in the Saṅgha, and the virtue dear to the noble ones. Dīghāvu answered that these four factors of stream-entry were already found in him; he lived by them. Then, standing on these four, said the Buddha, develop six things further that have a part in true knowledge: live seeing impermanence in all formations, perceiving suffering in what is impermanent, not-self in what is suffering, and perceiving giving up, fading away and ceasing.

These too, said Dīghāvu, are found in me; I live so. But one thought troubles me: that when I am gone, my father Jotika will be distressed. His father answered: dear Dīghāvu, do not attend to that. Attend closely to what the Blessed One is telling you.

The Buddha gave this advice and left, and not long after, Dīghāvu died. Monks came to ask where he had been reborn. Dīghāvu was wise, said the Buddha; he practised in line with the Dhamma and did not trouble me over the Dhamma. With the ending of the five lower fetters he has been reborn spontaneously, and will reach final Nibbāna there, never to return from that world.

A reading · a study aid, not the tradition's own reading

The three contemplations of “Ten knowledges, one by one” (CMA IX §24) at a deathbed, in a layman: impermanence in all formations, suffering in the impermanent, not-self in suffering. Linking his six perceptions to the three marks and the contemplations is the site's reading; the commentary calls them insight. The canon's words are “contemplating” impermanence and “perceiving” the other five (anupassī, saññī); in the Abhidhamma's terms such seeing is the work of wisdom, not of perception alone. His pain is painful bodily feeling, which arises only in one result citta, unwholesome-resultant body-consciousness (CMA III §3); whether his mind grieved or saw depended on the javanas that followed. Virtue, here the noble ones' virtue of the stream-enterer, is the ground the Buddha tells him to stand on.

The dhammas it shows

  • faith saddhā: unwavering confidence in the Buddha, the Dhamma and the Saṅgha
  • feeling vedanā: pain growing, not fading
  • wisdom paññindriya: insight for the higher paths, the commentary says

Where it is told

  • SN 55.3 Dīghāvu Sutta (sn55.3:3.6–5.7)
  • Saṃyutta commentary on SN 55.3, CS §999: “all formations” are those of the three planes; this is insight for the three higher paths

The sutta is short, canonical and undramatic. It does not say when Dīghāvu became a non-returner, only what he was practising and what he was at death. The commentary adds one line that matters: the six things are insight for the three higher paths, given to a man already established in the first, and “all formations” means the formations of the three planes, the whole field of insight. The human centre is the father: the last tie the son names is his care for him, and the father himself lets him go.

Opening question

His last worry was for his father, and his father released him from it. What does that say about the ties insight has to loosen?
