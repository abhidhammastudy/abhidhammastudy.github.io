The Buddha's life · Level 4

“Do not say so, Ānanda”

Ānanda remarked that dependent origination, though called deep, seemed clear to him. The Buddha replied: “Do not say so, Ānanda. This dependent origination is deep and appears deep. Through not understanding it, this generation has become like a tangled skein.”

Where
Kammāsadhamma, a market town of the Kurus
Who
  • Ānanda, the Buddha's cousin and attendant
  • The Buddha, teaching at Kammāsadhamma
Source
DN 15

The story

Ānanda came to the Buddha and said: it is wonderful, venerable sir, how deep this dependent origination is, and how deep it appears; and yet to me it seems as clear as clear can be.

“Do not say so, Ānanda,” the Buddha answered. “It is deep and it appears deep. It is through not understanding and not penetrating this teaching that this generation has become like a tangled skein, a knotted ball of thread, like matted reeds and rushes, and does not pass beyond the plane of misery, the bad destinations, the lower world, saṃsāra.”

Then he took Ānanda through it again, link by link, asking at each step: is there a specific condition for aging and death? For birth? For becoming? In this telling the chain runs back to consciousness and name-and-form, which condition each other, and stops there: “to this extent one may be born, age and die.” A second chain shows how craving leads to seeking, gain, possessiveness and at last to quarrels and the taking up of weapons. The sutta ends with the ways people describe a self, and the eight liberations.

A reading · a study aid, not the tradition's own reading

“The web of twenty-four conditions” and “The wheel across three lives” make the structure visible, but reading a diagram is learning (suta). Seeing a link happen is development (bhāvanā).

Read with: The wheel across three lives

The dhammas it shows

  • delusion moha: not understanding, this generation is a tangled skein
  • mutuality aññamañña: consciousness and name-and-form condition each other
  • greed lobha: craving leads to seeking, gain and quarrels

Where it is told

  • DN 15 Mahānidāna Sutta
  • Bhikkhu Bodhi, The Great Discourse on Causation (1984), with the commentary

The commentary reads the exchange two ways: as praise, since only Ānanda's long practice could make the teaching seem clear to him, and as a warning that it is deep for everyone else.
