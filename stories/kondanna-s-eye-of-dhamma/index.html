The Buddha's life · Level 6

Koṇḍañña's eye of Dhamma

At the first sermon in the Deer Park, while the Buddha was speaking, Koṇḍañña understood: “Whatever is subject to arising is all subject to cessation.” The Buddha exclaimed, “Koṇḍañña has understood!”

Where
The Deer Park at Isipatana, near Bārāṇasī
Who
  • Koṇḍañña, one of the five ascetics
  • The Buddha, giving his first teaching
Source
SN 56.11

The story

In the Vinaya and MN 26, the five ascetics saw him coming and agreed not to rise or greet him: he had given up the struggle. As he came near they could not keep to it. He told them not to call him “friend”: he had found the deathless.

He taught them that there are two extremes not to be followed, the pursuit of sensual pleasure and the pursuit of self-torment, and a middle way between them: the noble eightfold path. Then the four noble truths, each in three turnings: this is suffering; it is to be fully understood; it has been fully understood. Only when his knowledge of all twelve was clear, he said, did he claim to be awakened.

While he was speaking, the dustless eye of Dhamma arose in Koṇḍañña: whatever is subject to arising is all subject to cessation. The earth devas raised a cry, and it passed from heaven to heaven up to the Brahmā world; the ten-thousandfold world system shook, and a great light appeared. Then the Buddha exclaimed: “Koṇḍañña has understood! Koṇḍañña has understood!” and so he came to be called Aññā Koṇḍañña.

The Jātaka introduction (Nidānakathā) says Koṇḍañña had been the youngest of the eight brahmins who examined the baby prince's marks, and the only one who said without hedging that he would become a Buddha. Over the following days the other four entered the stream; with the next teaching, on non-self, all five became arahants.

A reading · a study aid, not the tradition's own reading

“The four noble truths” reads this sermon the Abhidhamma way: each truth a set of realities with its own task, and all four seen in a single moment. The commentaries take Koṇḍañña's insight as stream-entry: path knowledge took Nibbāna as its object and the first three fetters fell away. The sutta says only that the eye of Dhamma arose. The Saṃyutta commentary takes it here as the first path alone, and has him established in the fruit of stream-entry (SN-a on SN 56.11, CS §1081); that fruition follows the path in the same process is the Manual's account (CMA IX §34).

Read with: The four noble truths · The tree of purification

The dhammas it shows

  • path magga: path knowledge takes Nibbāna as its object
  • doubt vicikicchā: doubt, one of the three fetters that fell
  • wrong view diṭṭhi: identity view, cut at stream-entry
  • wisdom paññindriya: whatever is subject to arising is subject to cessation

Where it is told

  • SN 56.11 Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta
  • Saṃyutta commentary on SN 56.11, CS §1081: the eye of Dhamma here as the first path; Koṇḍañña established in the fruit of stream-entry
  • SN 22.59, the teaching on non-self that followed
  • Vinaya, Mahāvagga I.6
  • MN 26, the meeting with the five

The day is kept as Āsāḷha Pūjā, the full moon of Āsāḷha, usually in July, across the Theravāda world.
