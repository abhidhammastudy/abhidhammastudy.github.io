Jātaka · Level 3

Makhādeva's first grey hair

King Makhādeva told his barber to report the first grey hair. When it came, the king held it in his hand, saw death approaching, handed the kingdom to his son and went forth.

Where
Mithilā, capital of Videha
Who
  • King Makhādeva, the Bodhisatta, a righteous king of very long life
  • The barber, Ānanda in a former life
  • The prince, his eldest son; Rāhula in a former life
Source
Ja 9

The story

The Buddha told this story when the monks were talking about his own going forth. Makhādeva lived in an age when lives were long: the tale gives him eighty-four thousand years as a prince, as many as viceroy, and as many as king.

One day he told his barber: “When you see a grey hair on my head, tell me.” Many years later the barber found one, drew it out with tweezers and laid it in the king's hand. The king looked at it and felt as if the king of death had come and was standing beside him. He called it a messenger of the gods (devadūta).

He gave the barber a village, called his eldest son, and handed him the kingdom, asking him to do the same when his own first grey hair appeared. Then he went forth in a mango grove near the city, cultivated the four divine abidings, and after death was reborn in the Brahmā world.

His line kept the custom for eighty-four thousand kings. The last of them was Nimi, whose story is told in Ja 541: the Bodhisatta born again at the end of his own line, to close the circle.

A reading · a study aid, not the tradition's own reading

A small object can carry a large process: the same grey hair can stir vanity or urgency (saṃvega), depending on how it is attended to (“The mango and the moment”). MN 83 adds the Buddha's own point: Makhādeva's good custom led to the Brahmā world; the custom he himself founded, the noble eightfold path, leads to Nibbāna.

Read with: The mango and the moment · The tree of purification

The dhammas it shows

  • decay jaratā: one grey hair: the decay of matter
  • attention manasikāra: wise or unwise attention (yoniso / ayoniso manasikāra) decides between urgency and vanity

Where it is told

  • Ja 9 Makhādeva Jātaka
  • MN 83 Makhādeva Sutta, the canonical telling

MN 83 tells the same story in the canon, and adds the line of kings down to Nimi, and how the custom was finally broken by Nimi's son.
