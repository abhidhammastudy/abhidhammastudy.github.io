Nun Vajirā's chariot

Māra asked the nun Vajirā who had created “this being”. She answered: “Why do you assume a being? This is a heap of formations. As the word ‘chariot’ is used when the parts are assembled, so ‘a being’ is the convention when the aggregates are present.”

Where
The Blind Men's Grove outside Sāvatthī
Who
  • Vajirā, a nun, sitting alone for the day's meditation
  • Māra, the tempter, trying to make her afraid
Source
SN 5.10

The story

In the morning Vajirā went into Sāvatthī for alms, and after her meal she went to the Blind Men's Grove to sit for the day under a tree. Māra wanted to make her afraid and break her concentration, and came to her with a question in verse: by whom was this being created? Where is the one who makes beings? Where does a being arise, and where does it cease?

Vajirā knew at once who was speaking. Her answer, in three verses, became one of the most quoted passages in the canon. Why do you assume “a being”? Is that your view, Māra? This is a mere heap of formations; no being is found here. Just as the word “chariot” is used when the parts are put together, so “a being” is the convention when the aggregates are there.

Her last verse goes further: it is only suffering that comes to be, suffering that stands and falls away. Nothing but suffering comes to be, nothing but suffering ceases. Māra, knowing he had been seen through, vanished, sad and disappointed.

Centuries later the monk Nāgasena used the same chariot to answer King Milinda: is the axle the chariot? the wheels? the pole? Neither the parts nor something apart from them, and yet “chariot” is a true name; and he quoted Vajirā's verse to seal the point.

A reading · a study aid, not the tradition's own reading

Her reply is the hinge of “A chariot of parts”: the five aggregates underneath, the convention “a being” on top of them.

Read with: A chariot of parts

The dhammas it shows

  • wrong view diṭṭhi: Māra assumes a being; is that your view?
  • wisdom paññindriya: she sees a heap of formations, not a being

Where it is told

  • SN 5.10 Vajirā Sutta
  • Milindapañha 3.1.1 (SuttaCentral numbering), the chariot simile
  • Kathāvatthu I.1, the debate on the person, where her verse is quoted

The Saṃyutta Nikāya's chapter on nuns (SN 5) has ten such meetings with Māra, each answered in verse. Vajirā's is the one quoted in the Kathāvatthu's debate on the person, by Nāgasena to King Milinda, and in the Visuddhimagga on the purification of view.
