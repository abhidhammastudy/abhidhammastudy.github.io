The Buddha's life · Level 3

Rāhula and the mirror

The Buddha asked his young son Rāhula what a mirror is for. “For reflection, sir.” “In the same way,” said the Buddha, “reflect before, during and after every action of body, speech and mind: does it lead to harm for myself, for others, or both?”

Where
Ambalaṭṭhikā, in the Bamboo Grove at Rājagaha
Who
  • Rāhula, the Buddha's son, a novice of about seven, the commentary says
  • The Buddha, his father and teacher
Source
MN 61

The story

Seeing the Buddha coming in the evening, Rāhula set out a seat and water for his feet. The Buddha washed his feet and left a little water in the vessel. “Do you see this little water left, Rāhula? Just so little is the recluseship of those who are not ashamed to tell a deliberate lie.” He threw the water away: just so is their recluseship thrown away. He turned the vessel upside down, and then upright, empty: just so overturned, just so empty.

Then an elephant: a royal tusker that goes into battle using its feet, its body, its tusks and its tail, but keeps its trunk back, has not yet given its life to the fight. When it uses its trunk too, there is nothing it will not do. So with one who is not ashamed to lie: there is no evil he will not do. “Train thus, Rāhula: I will not tell a lie even as a joke.”

Then the mirror. “What is a mirror for?” “For reflection, venerable sir.” So, before doing an action with the body, while doing it and after it, one should reflect: does this lead to my own affliction, to others', or to both? Is it unwholesome, with painful results? If so, do not do it; stop it; and if it is done, confess it to the teacher or a wise companion and restrain yourself in future. The same for speech; but a deed of mind already done is not confessed: one should be troubled by it, ashamed of it, and turn from it. If the act was good, abide happy and glad, training day and night.

A reading · a study aid, not the tradition's own reading

Reflection is a discipline for the javanas, where volition becomes kamma (“The mango and the moment” and “Kamma, death and rebirth”).

Read with: The mango and the moment · Kamma, death and rebirth

The dhammas it shows

  • shame hiri: ashamed to lie, even as a joke
  • fear of wrongdoing ottappa: fear of painful results, checked like a mirror
  • right speech sammā-vācā: never a deliberate lie, even in jest
  • volition cetanā: reflecting on intention before, during and after acting

Where it is told

  • MN 61 Ambalaṭṭhikārāhulovāda Sutta
  • Named by King Asoka, in his Bhabru edict, among the texts monks and lay people should hear often

Rāhula has three teachings named for him in the Majjhima: MN 61, 62 and 147. In the last, years later, he becomes an arahant.
