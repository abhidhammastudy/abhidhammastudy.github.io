The Buddha's life · Level 4

Rays from the Jewel House

In the fourth week after the awakening, the commentary says, the Buddha sat at the “jewel house” and contemplated the Abhidhamma. Through the first six books no rays appeared; when he reached the Paṭṭhāna, rays of six colours streamed from his body.

Where
The Ratanaghara, “jewel house”, near the Bodhi tree, in the fourth week after the awakening
Who
  • The Buddha, newly awakened, contemplating the Abhidhamma
Source
Atthasālinī, introduction

The story

The commentaries spend each of the seven weeks after the awakening at a different spot near the Bodhi tree. In the fourth he sat to the north-west of the tree, at the place called the jewel house. Not a house of jewels, the Atthasālinī says, but the spot where he contemplated the seven books, the jewels of the Abhidhamma; the Jātaka introduction has the devas build a jewelled house there. He sat and went over the Abhidhamma in his mind, book by book.

He went through the Dhammasaṅgaṇī, then the Vibhaṅga, the Dhātukathā, the Puggalapaññatti, the Kathāvatthu and the Yamaka, and nothing happened. Then he came to the Paṭṭhāna, with its twenty-four conditions and their numberless combinations. Here, the commentary says, his omniscient knowledge found room at last, like the great fish Timirapiṅgala turning in the ocean, eighty-four thousand yojanas deep.

As his mind moved in that vastness, rays of six colours streamed from his body: blue from his hair and the dark of his eyes, yellow from his skin, red from his flesh and blood, white from his bones, teeth and the whites of his eyes, crimson from various parts of his body, and a shining blend of all five. They spread through the universe. This was neither a power willed nor a power of meditation, the commentary says: as he contemplated, his blood, the heart-base and his skin grew clear, and colour born of mind stood around him.

In 1885 a committee in Colombo designed a flag from these six colours for the Vesak holiday, and the American Henry Steel Olcott helped adapt it for wider use. It is now the Buddhist flag across the world.

A reading · a study aid, not the tradition's own reading

The tradition marks the Paṭṭhāna, the book behind “The web of twenty-four conditions”, as the vastest of all. Its colours inspired the modern Buddhist flag.

The dhammas it shows

  • wisdom paññindriya: omniscient knowledge at last finds room in the Paṭṭhāna
  • visible form vaṇṇa: colour born of mind: his blood and skin grew clear as he contemplated

Where it is told

  • Atthasālinī, Nidānakathā; The Expositor, tr. Pe Maung Tin (1920)
  • Jātaka Nidānakathā and the Buddhavaṃsa commentary (the jewel house in the fourth week)

The Jātaka introduction makes the jewel house a building the devas made; the Atthasālinī reads the name as the place of the seven books. The rays and the Paṭṭhāna belong to the Atthasālinī's telling, which has every reason to honour that book.
