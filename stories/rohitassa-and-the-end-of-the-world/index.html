Rohitassa and the end of the world

A deva asked the Buddha whether one could reach the end of the world by travelling. In a former life, he said, he had walked for a hundred years at the speed of an arrow and died without finding it. The Buddha answered that the world, its arising and its ending are found in this fathom-long body, with its perception and mind.

Where
Jetavana at Sāvatthī, late at night
Who
  • Rohitassa, a young deva, in his former life a seer of that name, Bhoja's son
  • The Buddha, at Jetavana
Source
AN 4.45; SN 2.26

The story

Late one night a young deva named Rohitassa came to the Buddha at Jetavana, lighting up the whole grove, and asked: is it possible, by travelling, to know or see or reach the end of the world, where one is not born, does not age, does not die, does not pass away and arise again? No, said the Buddha: that end of the world cannot be reached by going.

The deva was delighted, and told him why. In a former life he had been a seer called Rohitassa, with the power to travel through the air. His speed was that of a light arrow shot by a skilled archer across the shadow of a palm tree, and his stride reached from the eastern ocean to the western. With that speed he had set out to reach the end of the world. Stopping only to eat, drink, sleep and relieve himself, he walked for a hundred years, and died on the way.

The Buddha answered: without reaching the end of the world there is no making an end of suffering. But it is in this very fathom-long body, with its perception and its mind, that I make known the world, its arising, its ending, and the way that leads to its ending. Then he spoke verses: the end of the world is never reached by travelling; the wise one, who knows the world, goes to its end with the holy life lived out.

In SN 35.116 the Buddha gives the same teaching in brief, and the monks ask Ānanda to explain it. That in the world by which one perceives the world and conceives the world, Ānanda says, is called the world in the noble discipline: the eye, the ear, the nose, the tongue, the body and the mind.

A reading · a study aid, not the tradition's own reading

The world that cannot be reached by walking is the world of “The All, counted twice”: six doors, what comes through them, and the knowing of it, inside a fathom-long body.

Read with: The All, counted twice

The dhammas it shows

  • perception saññā: the world is made known with perception and mind
  • eye-sensitivity cakkhu: the eye, first of the six doors that are the world
  • energy viriya: a hundred years of walking, energy without the right direction

Where it is told

  • AN 4.45 Rohitassa Sutta
  • SN 2.26 Rohitassa Sutta (the same exchange)
  • SN 35.116, Ānanda explains what “the world” means

The same conversation is told twice in the canon, among the devas' questions in the Saṃyutta and among the fours in the Aṅguttara. Ānanda's explanation in SN 35.116 is the bridge from this story to the six bases.
