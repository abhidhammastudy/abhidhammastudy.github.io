Selā and the seed

Māra tried to frighten the nun Selā out of her meditation: who made this puppet, where is its maker, where does it come from and where does it end? She answered that it was made neither by itself nor by another. It comes to be because of a cause and ceases when the cause breaks up, as a seed grows in a field from the soil and its moisture.

Where
The foot of a tree near Sāvatthī, during the day's meditation
Who
  • Selā, a nun, sitting alone for the day's meditation
  • Māra, who came to frighten her out of her concentration
Source
SN 5.9

The story

One morning the nun Selā went for alms, and afterwards sat down at the foot of a tree for the day's meditation. Māra, the text says, wanted to make her feel fear and dread and make her hair stand on end, and to shake her out of her concentration. He spoke a verse to her: who made this puppet? Where is the one who made it? Where has the puppet come from, and where does it cease?

Selā wondered who was speaking, a human being or not; then she knew it was Māra, come to frighten her and break her concentration. She answered him in verse. This puppet is not made by itself, nor is this misery made by another. It comes to be because of a cause, and when the cause breaks up it ceases. As a seed sown in a field grows by drawing on the earth's nourishment and on moisture, so the aggregates, the elements and the six sense bases come to be because of a cause, and when the cause breaks up they cease.

Māra understood that she knew him. Unhappy and downcast, he vanished on the spot.

A reading · a study aid, not the tradition's own reading

The purification by overcoming doubt (CMA IX §31) in two verses: mind and matter seen to have conditions, so that doubt about a maker falls away. In the Guide to §31 the meditator turns dependent arising on his own mind and matter: past ignorance, craving, clinging and kamma, not chance or a maker, account for them: “The wheel across three lives” turned on oneself. Matching her verse to this stage is the site's reading; the sutta names no stage. It pairs with Vajirā's chariot, the stage before it, discerning mind and matter with no being behind it.

The dhammas it shows

  • wrong view diṭṭhi: the views that the person is self-made, or made by another
  • wisdom paññindriya: seeing that what comes from a cause ends with its cause

Where it is told

  • SN 5.9 Selā Sutta (sn5.9:4.1–6.4)
  • Saṃyutta commentary on SN 5.9, CS §170 (the puppet and the misery as the person; the cause breaking up as conditions lacking)
  • SN 12.17, the four positions on suffering: made by oneself, by another, by both, or by neither
  • SN 5.10, Vajirā's answer to the same kind of question, which follows in the same chapter

We have only her verses; the commentary on them is a few words long. It takes the puppet (bimba) as the person, the whole individual existence, and the misery (agha) as the same person, called misery because it is the ground of suffering; the cause breaking up is the lack of conditions. The suttas reject four positions on where suffering comes from: made by oneself, by another, by both, or by neither, arising by chance (SN 12.17). Selā's verse rules out the first two and puts a cause in their place. A nun named Selā also has verses in the Therīgāthā (3.7), but the Therīgāthā commentary says that Selā was also called Āḷavikā and tells her story with the verses of SN 5.1; it does not connect her with this sutta, so the two should not be merged.
