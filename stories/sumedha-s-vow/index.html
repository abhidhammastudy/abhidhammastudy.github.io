Jātaka · Level 7

Sumedha's vow

Ages ago, the ascetic Sumedha lay down in the mud so that the Buddha Dīpaṅkara could walk across him, and resolved to become a Buddha himself. Dīpaṅkara foretold it. Four incalculable aeons and a hundred thousand aeons of perfections followed.

Where
The city of Amaravatī, and the road to Rammaka, four incalculable aeons and a hundred thousand aeons ago
Who
  • Sumedha, a wealthy brahmin who became an ascetic; the Bodhisatta
  • Dīpaṅkara, the Buddha of that age
Source
Buddhavaṃsa II; Jātaka introduction

The story

Sumedha was a brahmin of Amaravatī whose parents died young, leaving him great wealth. Looking over the treasure his family had stored for seven generations, he thought: they all took nothing with them. Birth is suffering, ageing is suffering, death is suffering; as there is heat and also cold, there must be a way out. He gave everything away, went to the Himalaya as an ascetic, and mastered the jhānas and the powers.

One day, flying over the city of Rammaka, he saw people clearing and decorating a road. It was for the Buddha Dīpaṅkara. He asked for a stretch of it to prepare himself, and was given a muddy patch. Before he had finished, the Buddha came, with four hundred thousand arahants.

Sumedha loosened his hair, spread his bark robe and deerskin on the mud, and lay down across it so that the Buddha would not soil his feet. Lying there, he thought: if I wished, I could end my defilements today. But what use is crossing alone? I will become a Buddha, and carry many across. Dīpaṅkara stopped at his head and declared: after four incalculable aeons and a hundred thousand more, this ascetic will be a Buddha named Gotama.

When the Buddha had gone, Sumedha sat and searched out the qualities that make a Buddha, and found ten perfections: giving, virtue, renunciation, wisdom, energy, patience, truthfulness, resolution, loving-kindness and equanimity. The rest of the Jātaka collection, life after life, is the record of their fulfilling.

A reading · a study aid, not the tradition's own reading

One volition (“An ensemble of fifty-two”), renewed and fulfilled across lives (“Kamma, death and rebirth”), in worlds measured in aeons (“Thirty-one planes”): the whole study in a single vow.

Read with: An ensemble of fifty-two · Kamma, death and rebirth · Thirty-one planes

The dhammas it shows

  • volition cetanā: one resolve, renewed across countless lives
  • desire to act chanda: the wish to become a Buddha
  • compassion karuṇā: refusing to cross alone, he chooses to carry many

Where it is told

  • Buddhavaṃsa 2, the story of Sumedha
  • Jātaka Nidānakathā, “the distant epoch”
  • Cariyāpiṭaka (the perfections in practice)

The Buddhavaṃsa is late among the canonical books. Other schools tell the same meeting with Dīpaṅkara, with a young man called Megha or Sumati who throws flowers to the Buddha and spreads his hair in the mud.
