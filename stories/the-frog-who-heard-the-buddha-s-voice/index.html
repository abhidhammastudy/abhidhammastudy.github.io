The frog who heard the Buddha's voice

A frog at the edge of a lake took pleasure in the sound of the Buddha's voice. A cowherd leaning on his staff crushed it without knowing. It died with that pleasure in mind, and was reborn as a deva in the Tāvatiṃsa heaven.

Where
The bank of the Gaggarā lotus lake at Campā
Who
  • A frog, living in the lake, later the deva Maṇḍūka
  • A cowherd, leaning on his staff at the edge of the crowd
  • The Buddha, teaching by the lake in the evening
Source
Vimānavatthu 5.1, with its commentary

The story

The commentary tells that the Buddha was teaching one evening beside the Gaggarā lake at Campā. A frog came up out of the water and settled at the edge of the gathering, taking the sound of the Buddha's voice as its object, pleased by it without understanding a word, only knowing that this was the Dhamma.

A cowherd came to listen too, and stood leaning on his staff. Without seeing it, he set the point of the staff on the frog's head, and the frog died at once, its mind still bright with that pleasure. It was reborn in the Tāvatiṃsa heaven, in a golden mansion, among a company of nymphs.

Waking there as if from sleep, the young deva wondered what he had done to come to such a place, and saw nothing but the sound of a voice by a lake. He came down with his mansion and his company to where the Buddha was still teaching. Asked who he was, he answered in verse: in my former birth I was a frog, living in the water; listening to your Dhamma, a cowherd killed me. The Buddha taught him, and the commentary says he and many in the gathering realised the fruit of stream-entry.

A reading · a study aid, not the tradition's own reading

“Who is reborn where” says a being's next life depends on what it dies with. The frog lived in a woeful plane, but died with a wholesome sense-sphere citta, and its rebirth-linking was among the devas.

Read with: Thirty-one planes · Who is reborn where

The dhammas it shows

  • sound sadda: the Buddha's voice, heard without understanding
  • object ārammaṇa: that sound was the object of its last javanas
  • faith saddhā: “a moment's confidence of mind”, the verse's own words
  • kamma kamma: one brief wholesome kamma gave a deva's rebirth

Where it is told

  • Vimānavatthu 5.1, the Frog Deva's mansion (Vv 51)
  • Vimānavatthu commentary (Paramatthadīpanī), on the same

The verses are canonical, in the Vimānavatthu; the setting by the lake and the stream-entry at the end are from the commentary. Teachers often tell it to show that even an animal's citta can be wholesome; and that the cowherd, who did not see the frog, did no killing.
