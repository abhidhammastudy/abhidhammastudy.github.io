The Buddha's life · Level 4

The night of awakening

Under the Bodhi tree, in the first watch of the night he recalled his past lives; in the second he saw beings pass away and reappear according to their kamma; in the third he realised the destruction of the taints. In the days that followed he reviewed dependent origination forward and in reverse.

Where
The foot of the Bodhi tree at Uruvelā, on the bank of the Nerañjarā
Who
  • The Bodhisatta, aged thirty-five, six years after leaving home
Source
MN 36; Ud 1.1–1.3

The story

Having given up the austerities and eaten again, he sat beneath the tree and entered the four jhānas. With his mind concentrated, pure and pliant, he turned it to knowledge.

In the first watch of the night he recollected his past lives: one birth, two, a hundred thousand, through many aeons of the world's contraction and expansion, with names, families, food, pleasures and pains, and the way each life ended. In the second watch, with the divine eye, he saw beings dying and being reborn, inferior and superior, fortunate and unfortunate, according to their deeds. In the third watch he knew the four noble truths, and the taints of sensual desire, becoming and ignorance were destroyed. “Ignorance was dispelled and knowledge arose, darkness was dispelled and light arose.”

The Udāna adds what followed. For seven days he sat in one posture at the foot of the tree, experiencing the bliss of release. At the end of the week he attended to dependent origination: in the first watch in forward order, how suffering arises; in the second in reverse, how it ceases; in the third in both. At the end of each watch he spoke a verse that opens the same way: when things become clear to the ardent, meditating brahmin. After the first, his doubts vanish because he understands things with their cause; after the second, because he has known the ending of conditions; after the third, he stands scattering Māra's army, like the sun lighting up the sky.

A reading · a study aid, not the tradition's own reading

The three watches map onto the readings: past lives across the planes (“Thirty-one planes”), beings reborn by kamma and its fruit (“Kamma, death and rebirth”), and the wheel with its ending (“The wheel across three lives” and “The tree of purification”).

Read with: The wheel across three lives · Kamma, death and rebirth · The four noble truths · The tree of purification

The dhammas it shows

  • jhāna jhāna: the jhāna factors holding the mind closely on its object in each of the four jhānas
  • wisdom paññindriya: three knowledges in three watches of the night
  • path magga: the eight path factors of the third watch, carrying the path citta to its goal

Where it is told

  • MN 36 Mahāsaccaka Sutta (the three knowledges)
  • Ud 1.1–1.3 Bodhi Suttas
  • Vinaya, Mahāvagga I.1–4 (the first weeks; here each watch reviews dependent origination forward and in reverse)

The commentaries spread the weeks after the awakening into seven, each at a different spot near the tree; the fourth, at the “jewel house”, is the setting of “Rays from the Jewel House”.
