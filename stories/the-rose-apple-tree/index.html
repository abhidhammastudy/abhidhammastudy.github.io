The Buddha's life · Level 6

The rose-apple tree

As a boy, the Bodhisatta sat in the shade of a rose-apple tree while his father was at work (at the ploughing festival, the commentaries say), and entered the first jhāna. Years later, worn out by austerities, he remembered it and asked: “Could that be the path to awakening?”

Where
Kapilavatthu in his childhood, and Uruvelā during the austerities
Who
  • The Bodhisatta, as a boy, and later as an exhausted ascetic
  • King Suddhodana, his father, at work that day; leading the ploughing festival, the commentaries say
Source
MN 36

The story

In MN 36 the Buddha tells the debater Saccaka about his years of striving. He had pressed his tongue to his palate and crushed mind with mind until sweat ran from his armpits. He had stopped his breath until his head felt split by a sword. He had lived on a handful of beans a day, until his spine stood out like a string of beads and the hair rotted from his skin. Whatever pain any recluse had ever felt, this was the utmost; and it had not brought him knowledge.

Then he remembered a day in childhood. His father the Sakyan was at work, and he was sitting in the cool shade of a rose-apple tree, secluded from sensual pleasures and unwholesome states, and he entered the first jhāna, with its rapture and happiness. “Could that be the path to awakening?” And the knowledge came: that is the path.

Why be afraid of that pleasure, he asked himself, which has nothing to do with sensual desire? But such a pleasure could not be reached with so starved a body. So he ate rice and porridge again, and the five ascetics who had waited on him left in disgust: he had given up the struggle. With his strength back he sat down, entered the jhānas, and that night awakened.

The commentaries fill in the childhood day. It was the royal ploughing festival. The nurses left the boy alone under the tree to watch the ceremony, and returned to find him sitting cross-legged in meditation; and though the shadows of the other trees had moved with the sun, the shadow of the rose-apple tree had not.

A reading · a study aid, not the tradition's own reading

On the tree in “The tree of purification”, concentration is a root. The commentaries say his subject was the breath, one of the eleven in “Forty meditation subjects” that can reach all five jhānas.

Read with: Forty meditation subjects · The tree of purification

The dhammas it shows

  • zest pīti: the rapture of the first jhāna under the tree
  • initial application vitakka: the first jhāna still has initial application
  • decisive support upanissaya: the remembered jhāna, as decisive support for the path to awakening

Where it is told

  • MN 36 Mahāsaccaka Sutta
  • MN 85 and MN 100 give the same account
  • Jātaka Nidānakathā (the ploughing festival and the still shadow)
  • Majjhima commentary on MN 36 (Papañcasūdanī, CS §§380–381): the same festival, the nurses and the shadow

The commentaries say his subject that day was mindfulness of breathing, which is why the tradition takes the breath as the Buddha's own meditation.
