The royal cook's sauce

King Milinda asked whether the factors that arise together in one mind could be pulled apart and shown: this is contact, this is feeling, this is perception. No, said Nāgasena. They are like the flavours in a sauce made by a royal cook: no one can lift out the sourness or the salt, yet each is there by its own mark.

Where
Sāgala, King Milinda's city in the north-west
Who
  • King Milinda, the Greek king Menander, fond of hard questions
  • Nāgasena, the elder who answers him
Source
Milindapañha, the questions on distinguishing marks

The story

The king and the elder had been going through the factors of mind one at a time, each by its mark. Contact is known by touching, Nāgasena said, like two rams butting, the eye one ram, the form the other, and their meeting the contact. Feeling is known by being felt, perception by perceiving, volition by willing, initial application by fixing the mind on its object. Earlier, answering whether there is a knower inside, he had said that where eye-consciousness arises, contact arises with it, and feeling, perception, volition, one-pointedness, the life faculty and attention.

Then the king asked the harder question. Once these states are blended into one, can they be taken apart, so that one could show the difference: this is contact, this is feeling, this is perception, this is volition, this is consciousness? They cannot, said Nāgasena. Suppose a king's cook made a sauce or a curry, with curds, salt, ginger, cumin, pepper and the rest. Could anyone take from it the taste of the curds alone, or the taste of the salt? No, said the king, but each taste is there by its own mark. Just so with these states.

A reading · a study aid, not the tradition's own reading

The cook's sauce is a picture of one column of “Which factors meet which cittas”: a single citta assembled from a fixed menu of factors, each present by its own mark and none separable from the moment.

Read with: Which factors meet which cittas

The dhammas it shows

  • contact phassa: known by touching, like two rams butting
  • feeling vedanā: felt in the blend, never tasted alone
  • perception saññā: one flavour of the sauce, present by its own mark
  • volition cetanā: willing, blended with the rest in every citta

Where it is told

  • Milindapañha, the questions on distinguishing marks; translated by T. W. Rhys Davids as The Questions of King Milinda, and by I. B. Horner as Milinda's Questions
  • Milindapañha 3.4.1 (SuttaCentral numbering), the cook's sauce
  • Milindapañha 3.3.6 (SuttaCentral numbering), the question on the knower (the factors that arise with eye-consciousness)

The Milindapañha is outside the canon in Sri Lanka and Thailand, but within it in Burma. The king is the Indo-Greek Menander of the second century BCE; the book was probably put together over the following centuries. The list of factors that arise with eye-consciousness names exactly the seven that the Abhidhamma calls the universals.

Opening question

If the factors of one moment cannot be lifted apart, what is the use of naming them one by one?
