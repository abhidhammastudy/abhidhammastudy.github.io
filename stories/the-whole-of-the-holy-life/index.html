Disciples · Level 6

The whole of the holy life

Ānanda said that good friendship is half of the holy life. “Not so, Ānanda,” said the Buddha. “Good friendship is the whole of the holy life.” A monk with a good friend can be expected to develop the noble eightfold path.

Where
Nāgaraka, a town of the Sakyans
Who
  • Ānanda, the Buddha's attendant
  • The Buddha, staying among the Sakyans
Source
SN 45.2

The story

Ānanda came to the Buddha, sat down, and said: venerable sir, this is half of the holy life: good friendship, good companionship, good comradeship. Not so, Ānanda, the Buddha answered. It is the entire holy life. When a monk has a good friend, a good companion, a good comrade, it is to be expected that he will develop and cultivate the noble eightfold path.

How? He develops right view, right intention and each of the other factors in a way that rests on seclusion, on fading away and on cessation, and ripens in letting go. And this is how you may know it, Ānanda: by relying on me as a good friend, beings subject to birth are freed from birth, beings subject to aging, sickness and death are freed from them.

In SN 3.18 King Pasenadi of Kosala tells the Buddha he has been thinking that the Dhamma is for one with good friends, not for one with bad friends. The Buddha agrees, and tells the king this same conversation with Ānanda, and advises him that a ruler with good friends should live by heedfulness in good things.

A reading · a study aid, not the tradition's own reading

In “The noble eightfold path” the path, in the strict sense, is eight factors in one citta at the moment of the path; this story is about how they come to arise at all. A good friend is a natural decisive support from outside.

Read with: The web of twenty-four conditions · The noble eightfold path

The dhammas it shows

  • decisive support upanissaya: a good friend: a person as natural decisive support for the path
  • wisdom paññindriya: right view, first of the eight path factors
  • faith saddhā: relying on the Buddha as one's good friend

Where it is told

  • SN 45.2 Upaḍḍha Sutta
  • SN 3.18 Kalyāṇamitta Sutta (the same words told to King Pasenadi)
  • SN 45.49, good friendship as the forerunner of the path, like dawn before sunrise

The exchange stands near the opening of the Saṃyutta on the path. The Buddha corrects not Ānanda's praise of friendship but its measure.

Opening question

In what sense could a friend be the whole of a path that each person walks alone?
