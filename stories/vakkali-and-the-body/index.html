Vakkali and the body

The monk Vakkali longed to keep looking at the Buddha. The Buddha said: “Enough, Vakkali. What is there to see in this foul body? Whoever sees the Dhamma sees me.”

Where
A potter's shed in Rājagaha, and Black Rock on the slope of Isigili
Who
  • Vakkali, a monk who loved the sight of the Buddha, now gravely ill
  • The Buddha, visiting the sick
Source
SN 22.87

The story

The commentary says Vakkali was a brahmin of Sāvatthī who went forth for one reason: so that he could always see the Buddha, whose body he found beautiful beyond anything. He followed him about and neglected his practice. At last the Buddha sent him away. In despair Vakkali climbed Vulture Peak to throw himself off, and the Buddha appeared before him and taught him.

In the sutta, Vakkali lies sick in a potter's shed in Rājagaha and asks for the Buddha to come. When the Buddha arrives, Vakkali tries to rise from his bed, and is told to lie still. He says he has long wanted to come and see the Buddha but had no strength. The Buddha answers: “Enough, Vakkali. Why do you want to see this foul body? One who sees the Dhamma sees me; one who sees me sees the Dhamma.”

Then the Buddha questions him, as he questioned the first five disciples: is form permanent or impermanent? Feeling, perception, formations, consciousness? Whatever is impermanent, is it fit to be regarded as “mine, I, my self”? Vakkali answers rightly each time.

Vakkali asks to be carried to Black Rock on the slope of Isigili, saying a monk like him should not die inside a house. That night two devas tell the Buddha that Vakkali is intent on release, and in the morning the Buddha sends word to him: do not fear; your death will not be a bad one. Vakkali sends back word that he has no doubt the five aggregates are impermanent, and no desire for them. Then he takes the knife. The Saṃyutta commentary explains what follows: he had overrated himself, and seeing no defilement stir, believed he was already an arahant. In the pain of the wound he saw that he was still an ordinary person, took up his meditation subject again at once, and became an arahant as he died. Afterwards the Buddha shows the monks a smoky cloud moving in all directions: Māra, searching for the place where Vakkali's consciousness has been established. It is nowhere, the Buddha says: Vakkali has attained final Nibbāna.

A reading · a study aid, not the tradition's own reading

The commentary takes this Dhamma as the ninefold supramundane Dhamma; the sutta goes straight on to teach him the five aggregates, impermanent and not self. Seen that way, even “the Buddha” is a convention for aggregates, the shift “A chariot of parts” asks of you.

Read with: A chariot of parts

The dhammas it shows

  • greed lobha: attachment to the sight of the Buddha
  • visible form vaṇṇa: the visible form he could not stop looking at
  • faith saddhā: foremost among those resolved through faith

Where it is told

  • SN 22.87 Vakkali Sutta
  • Saṃyutta commentary, Khandhavagga, on SN 22.87, CS ¶87 (Vakkalisuttavaṇṇanā: his overrating and his death)
  • Dhammapada commentary on v. 381 (the earlier episode at Vulture Peak)
  • AN 1.208, the list of foremost disciples: Vakkali, foremost among those resolved through faith
  • Visuddhimagga IV §62 (CS), Vakkali as the example of faith too strong

The commentaries tell Vakkali's story in more than one way. In the Dhammapada commentary he becomes an arahant on Vulture Peak; in the sutta he takes his own life when ill, and the Buddha declares him free; the Saṃyutta commentary has him reach arahantship only in the pain of the wound. Neither text praises the knife. The suttas treat a few such deaths (Channa, Godhika) in the same careful way, and the Visuddhimagga names Vakkali as its example of faith too strong for the other faculties, needing wisdom to balance it.
