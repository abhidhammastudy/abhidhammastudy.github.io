The Buddha's life · Level 1

Where the Abhidhamma was first taught

The commentary tells that the Buddha spent a rainy season in the Tāvatiṃsa heaven, teaching the Abhidhamma to the devas, among them his mother, reborn as a deva. Each day he came down to Lake Anotatta, where he gave Sāriputta the outline, and Sāriputta taught it to five hundred monks. At the end of the rains he descended at Saṅkassa. The Tipiṭaka itself does not tell this story.

Where

The story

In the seventh year after his awakening, the commentaries say, the Buddha performed the Twin Miracle at Sāvatthī, and then, as every Buddha does, went up to the Tāvatiṃsa heaven for the rains. He sat on Sakka's stone seat beneath the coral tree, and devas from ten thousand world-systems gathered to hear him. At their head was his mother. She had died seven days after his birth and was reborn as a deva; the Dhammapada commentary has her come down from the Tusita heaven to listen. The teaching he gave, with his mother as its chief witness, was the Abhidhamma; modern teachers often call it a debt repaid to the one who had carried him.

The teaching went on without a break for three months. At the hour for alms the Buddha created a likeness of himself to carry on the discourse, went to gather food, and ate beside Lake Anotatta in the Himalaya. Sāriputta waited on him there, and the Buddha gave him the method of that day's teaching in outline: "this much has been taught". Sāriputta, with his gift for analysis, could unfold the whole from the outline, and he taught it to the five hundred monks who were his pupils. The commentary adds that in the time of the Buddha Kassapa they had been bats hanging in a cave. They caught only the sound of two monks reciting the Abhidhamma, not its sense, and that was enough to bring them to a deva world. Now, reborn as men and ordained under Sāriputta, they learned all seven books just as the Buddha's teaching in heaven came to its end.

At the end of the rains, on the full-moon day of Pavāraṇā, the Buddha came down at the city of Saṅkassa by a stairway of jewels, with devas on stairways of gold and silver on either side. The place became one of the great pilgrimage sites. The textual order of the Abhidhamma as monks recite it, and the numerical series of the Paṭṭhāna, the commentary says, were laid down by Sāriputta. Does that make him the first master of the Abhidhamma? No, the commentary answers: the Buddha is, for he penetrated it under the Bodhi tree. Sāriputta is the first to pass it on.

A reading · a study aid, not the tradition's own reading

This is the tradition's account of where the seven books described in “The library” came from, and why Sāriputta is honoured as the one who first passed it on.

Read with: The library · Thirty-one planes

The dhammas it shows

  • wisdom paññindriya: Sāriputta unfolds the whole teaching from an outline
  • sound sadda: bats in a cave took in only the sound of recitation
  • decisive support upanissaya: that hearing, lives ago, supported learning it again

Where it is told

  • Atthasālinī, Nidānakathā (introduction); translated as The Expositor, by Pe Maung Tin
  • Dhammapada commentary on v. 181, story XIV.2 (the Twin Miracle and the descent at Saṅkassa)

The story is told in the commentaries of the fifth century, not in the canon. It is the tradition's answer to a question it took seriously: if the Abhidhamma is the Buddha's word, when and to whom did he teach it? Modern scholarship generally dates the books of the Abhidhamma Piṭaka later than the main body of the suttas.
